**One Author. One originating Word. One Creation. Many witnesses.**

This document presents the established architecture as a whole. It is a recovered presentation of the project's existing framework, not a proposal to build, extend, or redesign it. Explanations and records may become clearer; the completed architecture is not thereby made more complete.

## 1. Author, Word, Creation, Witness

**Author → Word → Creation → Witness**

- **Author:** God alone is Original Cause.
- **Word:** The originating Word through whom Creation is made. John 1:1–3 and Hebrews 1:1–3 identify the Word/Son in relation to Creation and its continuance.
- **Creation:** The One Creation established by the Author through the Word.
- **Witness:** The derivative registration and testimony of what the Author has established. A witness is not the Author and does not originate or alter the Source.

GUA-W witnesses to the Author. Its purpose is not self-glorification; the glory belongs to God.

## 2. Genesis 1 — the Author's operative account of His unified Creation

Genesis 1 is not merely a description of Creation observed after the fact. It is the Author's own account of His Word going forth and Creation being brought forth and ordered.

**Genesis 1 is the Author writing and expressing His own GUA-W: the unified architecture through which He describes, orders, and brings His Creation into its created realization by His Word.**

This does not mean God consults an external blueprint, obeys a structure above Himself, or depends on an independent mechanism. God alone is Original Cause. GUA-W names the unified architecture of His One Creation; it is not a second author or a power alongside Him. John 1:1–3 identifies the Word in the beginning and says all things were made by Him; Colossians 1:16–17 and Hebrews 1:1–3 witness to Creation through the Son and its continuance in Him.

In this bounded sense, GUA-W is the Author's operative architecture—the “engine” of His created order. The Word is not a passive description or an observer's report: the Genesis account repeatedly places God's speech and action with the realized result. The engine is not AI, science, or the diagram. The Author acts; His Word is the originating creative Word; Creation is His work; witnesses register what is given.

Genesis 1 must therefore be read as both the Author's account of Creation and the account of His creative ordering in action. Its wording and narrated operations vary; do not flatten the chapter into an identical algorithm or force every clause into a one-to-one technical label.

## 3. The completed whole in Genesis 2:1–5

Genesis 2:1 opens with the declaration:

> Thus the heavens and the earth were finished, and all the host of them. (KJV)

The passage then records God's ending of His work, His rest, and His blessing and sanctification of the seventh day (2:2–3). Verse 4 turns to the generations of the heavens and the earth. Verse 5 describes conditions concerning the plant and herb of the field before they were in the earth or had grown, and identifies the absence of rain and of a man to till the ground.

Within the GUA-W reading, Genesis 2:1–5 witnesses to the completed whole before the subsequent account describes further conditions, manifestation, placement, and human participation. The later description does not make the declared whole an unfinished architecture.

The project identifies this as the **first completion** — the first “It is Finished” in the three-completion comparison. This is a theological correspondence, not a claim that Genesis 2:1–5 contains the exact English phrase spoken in John 19:30.

## 4. The three completion witnesses

The project follows three completion declarations across Scripture:

1. **Genesis 2:1–5 — the first completion:** the heavens, earth, and all their host are declared finished; God's work is ended, and the seventh day is blessed and sanctified.
2. **John 19:30 — the second completion:** Christ says, “It is finished.”
3. **Revelation 21:6 — the third completion:** the One who is Alpha and Omega says, “It is done.”

These passages describe distinct events and use different wording. Their relationship is investigated as one divine purpose across Creation, Christ's completed work, and the consummation of the new Creation. Revelation 21–22 returns to Creation themes including the new heaven and earth, God's dwelling with His people, the end of the curse, and the tree of life.

## 7. The Genesis 1–2 / Genesis 3 comparison

The Bible is read as a sustained comparison between the order established in Genesis 1–2 and the departure narrated in Genesis 3, followed through the rest of Scripture.

- **Genesis 1–2:** the Author's Word, the created order, life, provision, placement, purpose, command, and the original condition.
- **Genesis 3:** the questioning and contradiction of God's Word, the human response, and the resulting departure and consequences.
- **The rest of Scripture:** the unfolding record of that departure, God's dealings, His promises, the witnesses to His Word, and His purpose in Christ.
- **Christ:** the Word made flesh, the Way, the Truth, and the Life; also identified by Paul as the last Adam (John 1:1–14; John 14:6; 1 Corinthians 15:45–49).
- **Revelation 21–22:** the consummation, including the new Creation, God's presence, the removal of the curse, and the tree of life.

This comparison does not make Genesis 3 the author of a new Creation. It records a departure within the Creation God established.
