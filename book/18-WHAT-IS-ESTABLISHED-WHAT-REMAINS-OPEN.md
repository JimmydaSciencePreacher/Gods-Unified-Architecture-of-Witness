# Chapter 18 — What Is Established, What Remains Open

## The final chapter is not a declaration that investigation is over

A book needs an ending. An honest investigation needs a record that remains open to further evidence, correction, and clarification.

This is the final chapter in the present eighteen-chapter reading plan. It is not a declaration that every question in the Research Library has been settled, that every Scriptural passage has received a full investigation, or that every scientific discipline has been examined. It brings the reader to the point where the difference between an established conclusion and an unfinished question can be seen clearly.

The governing order has not changed:

**Author → Word → Creation → Witness**

God alone is Original Cause. The written account, the investigator, the scientific model, the historical record, and the AI-assisted explanation are derivative witnesses. They may report faithfully or inaccurately. They do not become the Author, and they do not alter the Source.

The purpose of this final chapter is therefore not to manufacture closure. It is to show what the record presently establishes, what it has explicitly rejected, what it has narrowed, and what remains open.

## Completion of the architecture and continuation of the record

Throughout this book, one distinction must remain visible.

**GUA-W is the completed architecture. The written presentation and the investigation record can continue to become clearer.**

A missing explanation is not a missing architectural component. An unresolved research question is not proof that the architecture is incomplete. A newly recognized relationship is not a newly invented Gear, layer, runtime stage, or universal law.

The book has followed the Author and the originating Word through the Creation foundation; the Genesis account; the departure recorded in Genesis 3–4; the completed architecture; the works, death, and resurrection of Christ; the Scriptural investigation of salvation and the human will; and the sciences as differentiated witnesses.

The Research Library preserves the detailed work behind that reading journey. The chapters make the journey readable; they do not replace the source investigations.

## Four dispositions that must not be confused

The repository's registers distinguish several different outcomes. They are not interchangeable labels.

### Established

A claim is established when the evidence examined supports the stated conclusion within its proper scope. The conclusion must retain the assumptions, context, and limits that make it accurate.

An established finding does not become universal merely because it is important. A result in a specified physical model remains a result in that model unless a justified extension is demonstrated. A Scriptural conclusion must likewise retain the passage, context, and canonical boundaries that support it.

### Rejected

A rejected claim is a specific proposition examined and found inadmissible or unsupported for a stated reason. Rejection must be documented, not assumed.

The repository's Rejected and Unresolved Register expressly requires a precise proposition, status, evidence reviewed, reason, implicated constraints, related investigations, and revision history. It does not begin by declaring broad categories of claims rejected. This prevents an investigator from turning disagreement, unfamiliarity, or lack of immediate confirmation into a substitute for examination.

### Narrowed or qualified

A question may have a real answer within a defined boundary even though it does not yield an exhaustive theory.

The human-will investigation is an example. The beginning account establishes narrated operations of human willing, creaturely limits, and accountability. It does not provide an exhaustive philosophical, psychological, or physiological taxonomy of every faculty. The correct result is neither “nothing has been answered” nor “every aspect has been exhaustively defined.” The answer is stated at the level the text supports.

### Unresolved

An unresolved question identifies a precise remainder that the evidence examined has not settled. It is not an invitation to invent a mechanism, and it is not a declaration that the Answer is absent from Scripture or Creation.

The question must be stated narrowly enough that a reader can tell what further evidence would matter. If later examination settles it, the register must be updated. An old unresolved label must never be preserved merely because it was once written.

## Return to the beginning before declaring an answer absent

The repository records a governing correction:

**The Answer precedes the question.**

A question is encountered by a witness within an already existing Creation. The question does not become the authority over the reality being investigated.

Accordingly, a question concerning the originating order must return to the Scriptural foundation in Genesis 1–2, with particular attention to Genesis 2:1–5. The relevant passage must then be examined in its own context. Later canonical passages may illuminate, confirm, qualify, or extend the finding. They must not be used to erase the beginning account or to manufacture a chronology that Scripture does not state.

This order does not mean that every later question is answered by quoting Genesis 2:1–5 in isolation. It means the investigation does not treat a derivative question as though it were the source of the answer. The beginning establishes the foundation; the relevant passages and the wider canon must then be read faithfully.

The same discipline applies to science. Begin with the actual phenomenon, formal result, or measured evidence. Do not begin with the desired correspondence and search only for material that appears to support it.

## What the current unresolved register actually says

The register is a working record, not a list of failures. Its entries have different scopes and statuses.

### U-001 — Cross-domain architectural equivalence

The question is whether mathematical topology, physical conservation or persistence, and Scriptural descriptions of differentiation and propagation express one common architecture or mechanism.

The domains use different definitions and assumptions. Similarities can justify comparison, but they do not by themselves demonstrate identity or a common mechanism.

**Present disposition: UNRESOLVED.** The investigation must identify the exact concepts being compared and the kind of relationship claimed. Analogy, structural correspondence, mathematical equivalence, and causal identity must remain distinct.

### U-002 — Identity, persistence, and state

The question concerns what constitutes identity across change and how persistence relates to identity and state within the completed GUA-W.

Topological invariance, physical conservation, biological continuity, and metaphysical identity are not interchangeable terms. Each has its own scope.

**Present disposition: UNRESOLVED.** Further work must state the domain and the precise meaning of identity or persistence under examination. No universal definition may be imported from one discipline into another without argument.

### U-003 — Differentiation and propagation

Scripture describes distinctions, kinds, seed, fruitfulness, and multiplication. The remaining question concerns the exact relationship between Differentiation and Propagation in the recognized GUA-W runtime account.

**Present disposition: UNRESOLVED.** The Scriptural descriptions must remain distinct from an interpretive operational mapping. The next investigation must examine the actual passages and the completed architecture together, not create a new stage or redefine an existing one.

### U-004 — Conditions for persistence through change

The question asks under what conditions a realized configuration persists, transforms, or ceases to persist.

Those conditions differ across physical, biological, mathematical, and other domains. A single generalization cannot be presumed.

**Present disposition: UNRESOLVED.** Investigate domain-specific conditions first. Any broader relationship must be supported rather than assumed.

### U-005 — Drift

The question asks what Drift means in GUA-W and how it is distinguished from change, variation, mutation, noise, entropy, error, or failure.

**Present disposition: UNRESOLVED.** The term must not be equated with sin, entropy, mutation, noise, or system failure by default. Its meaning and admissibility test require precise examination. An attractive analogy is not a definition.

### U-006 — Human will: operation, capacity, and limits

This question has been substantially narrowed through the return to Genesis 1–3 and the integration of later canonical witnesses.

Genesis 2 establishes the Creator's prior work and order. Genesis 2:16–17 records the command and boundary. Genesis 3 narrates human willing in operation: reception and response to communication, perception, evaluation, desire, heeding, action, and response to consequence. These operations are real and accountable, but they are not interchangeable.

Later passages examined in the Human Will and Salvation reference work include Ephesians 2:1–10, James 1:13–18, Philippians 2:12–13, 1 Corinthians 2:10–16, Romans 8:5–11 and 8:28–30, Acts 13:44–48, 1 John 5:1, 2 Thessalonians 2:13–14, and Ephesians 1:3–14. Their contributions are recorded passage by passage, including what each text states and what it does not settle.

**Present disposition: NARROWED — FOUNDATIONAL ANSWER ESTABLISHED IN GENESIS 2–3; NO EXHAUSTIVE ABSTRACT DEFINITION CLAIMED.**

The remaining boundaries concern specific doctrinal questions, including the full chronology and causal mechanics of regeneration and faith, the precise nuance of foreknowledge, and other matters where the passages do not state every interval or mechanism. Those boundaries must not be used to reopen the foundational findings or to erase the explicit place of believing, hearing, response, and accountability in the relevant texts.

### U-007 — Knowledge of good and evil versus authority to establish its standard

This question is resolved.

Genesis 3 records changed human knowledge and the exercise of perception, evaluation, desire, and action. It does not state that humanity became the Author or received God's authority to establish the governing standard of good and evil. Genesis 4:6–7 continues to show God addressing Cain within an evaluative standard that God supplies.

**Present disposition: RESOLVED — no transfer of divine authority to establish the standard of good and evil is described or granted.**

This resolution must not be inflated into an exhaustive definition of every aspect of human will. That is a separate question, recorded under U-006.

### U-008 — John 20:22 and the outpouring at Pentecost

The question concerns the precise relationship between the risen Christ's breathing upon the disciples and command to receive the Holy Ghost in John 20:22, and the later outpouring in Acts 2.

The existing investigation distinguishes four questions: extent and timing of reception; function of the gift; relationship among the Gospel and Acts narratives; and the scope and identity of the recipients. It has examined the relevant Johannine and Lukan passages, Acts 1–2, and Old Testament promise and restoration texts. The recipient groups and the language used in the different scenes have been compared without treating distinct verbs or narrative settings as interchangeable.

The record identifies what is explicit: Jesus speaks and acts in the resurrection appearance; the disciples are commanded to receive; Luke records the promise of power; Acts records the outpouring and Peter's explanation of the event. The texts must be read together without assuming in advance that every scene is identical or that every distinction represents a separate gift.

**Present disposition: UNRESOLVED.** The nature and timing of the relationship between John 20:22 and Pentecost remain under examination. The work must continue through the complete passages and their canonical setting, without manufacturing a sequence or collapsing Resurrection, glorification, Ascension, Spirit reception, and Pentecost into one event.

These entries show why a register must preserve distinctions. U-007 is resolved. U-006 is narrowed with its foundational answer established. U-001 through U-005 and U-008 remain open for their stated reasons. Treating them all as equally unknown would misrepresent the work already completed.

## Continuing investigations are not additional chapters waiting to be invented

The Research Library contains substantial work that cannot be reduced to the eight unresolved entries above.

### The works and signs of Jesus

Investigation II contains dedicated event-level studies, while its master register still requires classification of remaining entries. A register entry may represent a discrete event, a grouped report, prophecy, angelic direction, non-miracle testimony, or apostolic work. Those categories must be distinguished before another file is created.

The continuing work is to classify the remaining Gospel-era entries, identify overlap among parallel accounts, and investigate genuine unrecorded events in canonical order. Apostolic works in Acts must remain a separate agency category: works done by servants in Christ's name must not be conflated with the works of Christ Himself.

### The Passion and Resurrection

The central Resurrection study and the dedicated studies of surrounding events are present. Further work remains in historical-source examination, textual criticism, chronology, and specific domain questions.

The correct next step is to pursue the named open questions in the existing investigations—not to repeat completed descriptions or create duplicate event studies. Historical evidence, textual observations, theological declaration, and scientific limits must remain distinguishable.

### The breath of the risen Christ

The study of John 20:22 remains connected to the wider sequence in John 7 and 14–16, John 19–20, Luke 24, Acts 1–2, and the relevant Old Testament promises. Its questions include extent and timing, function, narrative relation, and recipient scope.

The existing work must continue from the primary texts and the competing interpretations. It must not assume that “indwelling” and “empowerment” settle the issue merely by being named, and it must not turn a lexical echo into proof of an entire mechanism.

### Human Will and Salvation

The integrated reference work is a connected canonical investigation, not a collection of unrelated proof texts. Its passage studies and synthesis must be maintained together.

The recent additions examine divine initiative, the Father's giving, the Son's receiving and keeping, Gospel proclamation, hearing and believing, the Spirit's work, and the Book of Life passages. They also preserve the limits of the texts: a grammatical form alone does not manufacture a complete chronology, and a passage that states God's purpose does not authorize the investigator to erase the human responses it expressly records.

The continuing task is to address named source-level gaps and remaining canonical questions. It is not to create duplicate passage entries or to reopen conclusions already resolved.

### Cross-disciplinary evidence

The scientific audits remain available for deeper source-level verification and further domain-specific work. The four case studies in Chapter 17 are not a declaration that physics, biology, mathematics, ecology, information theory, or every other discipline has been exhausted.

The method remains the same: identify the actual scientific claim; examine the primary evidence and assumptions; distinguish observation, formal result, model, interpretation, and conjecture; identify the claim's limits; and only then examine whether a meaningful relationship can be recognized within the completed GUA-W.

Theology addresses the ultimate WHY of Creation. The differentiated sciences investigate their proper questions about the created order and its operations. Their methods are not interchangeable, and theological primacy does not authorize the invention of domain-specific mechanisms.

## The Triple Constraint remains the test of admissibility

No conclusion becomes an accepted GUA-W claim merely because it is interesting, widely repeated, or expressed with confidence.

### Biblical compliance

The relevant KJV passage must be read in context and in the light of the wider canon. A proposed interpretation must not contradict the stated Genesis 1–2 foundation or claim that Scripture says what it does not say.

### Scientific coherence

A scientific statement must accurately represent the discipline's evidence, definitions, assumptions, models, uncertainty, and limits. A theorem, observation, model-dependent result, interpretation, and conjecture must not be presented as though they were the same kind of evidence.

### GUA-W consistency

The conclusion must preserve the Author → Word → Creation → Witness hierarchy and the completed architecture. A comparison may illuminate a relationship; it cannot authorize the witness to become the Author or redesign what is already established.

A claim that fails any one constraint is not admitted as an accepted GUA-W claim. A proposal may remain recorded for further examination, provided its status is explicit.

## The role of the witness—and the limits of AI

A witness reports what is given. A reliable witness does not improve a report by adding what was not seen.

AI can help locate records, compare passages, organize claims, trace sources, identify contradictions, and prepare readable explanations. It can also misread a passage, overlook a variant, confuse an interpretation with an established result, or write beyond the evidence. Its fluency is not proof of correctness.

AI's writing ability is useful when it remains derivative: investigate, retrieve, analyze, cross-reference, and write what the evidence supports. The primary source remains primary. The Scripture remains the text being examined. The completed GUA-W remains the architecture being recognized and explained.

The human reader must be able to follow the trail and examine the evidence. A polished chapter is not a substitute for its source record, and a generated sentence does not become an authority by being included in a book.

## The book ends here; the work does not

The present reading edition has reached its planned eighteenth chapter. That gives the reader a complete route through the subjects presently organized into the book. It does not close the Research Library, forbid corrections, or prevent a future edition from incorporating further findings.

Future work should normally improve the appropriate existing record:

- a new Scriptural finding belongs in the relevant canonical investigation;
- a scientific correction belongs in the corresponding evidence audit;
- a changed status belongs in the Rejected and Unresolved Register;
- a clarified explanation belongs in the relevant book chapter;
- a navigation change belongs in the reading edition and repository map.

A new chapter should be written when a genuine subject requires one in the reader's journey—not merely because another investigation has been completed. The book should remain readable, while the research library remains detailed enough for verification.

This is how the work can continue without confusing the growth of understanding with the creation of the thing being understood.

## Conclusion — a faithful witness leaves the record open

The final chapter does not claim that every question has been answered. It shows that the work has learned to distinguish an answer from an interpretation, a resolved question from a narrowed one, and a genuine unknown from a stale label.

Some conclusions stand. Some claims have been rejected or bounded. Some questions remain open. The record must say which is which, and it must change when the evidence warrants a change.

The Author is not waiting for the witness to complete Creation. The witness is learning to report the Creation that is already given.

**One Author. One originating Word. One Creation. Many witnesses.**
