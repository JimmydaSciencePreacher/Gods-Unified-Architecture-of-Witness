## 1. Purpose of this return audit

The outward journey follows the differentiated HOW-sciences as they investigate light: physics, chemistry, biology, astronomy, Earth science, information science, and mathematics. Their findings are compared and cross-verified within their proper domains.

This document follows the return journey: the findings are brought to Theology—the Queen of the Sciences—to examine the ultimate WHY in relation to the Author, His Word, His Creation, and His purpose.

The two journeys must not be collapsed:

- **Outward:** Author → Word → Creation → differentiated HOW-science investigations → evidence and cross-verified findings.
- **Return:** findings → theological examination under Scripture → recognition of the Author and the stated purpose of Creation.

The return is epistemic and interpretive, not reverse causation. Scientific findings do not create, authorize, or alter the Author.

## 2. What the KJV text explicitly records

### Genesis 1:3–5

The passage records this order:

1. God said, “Let there be light.”
2. Light appeared: “and there was light.”
3. God saw the light, that it was good.
4. God divided the light from the darkness.
5. God called the light Day, and the darkness he called Night.
6. The evening and the morning are identified as the first day.

These are textual observations. They identify God as the acting Author and record command, result, evaluation, distinction, naming, and the stated day sequence.

The passage does not give a modern physical mechanism for the appearance of light. It does not use the vocabulary of photons, electromagnetic fields, wavelengths, or spectra. Those belong to scientific investigations of physical phenomena, not as replacement wording for the KJV text.

### Genesis 1:14–19

The later passage records the appointment of lights in the firmament of the heaven. The KJV states their purposes:

- to divide the day from the night;
- to be for signs;
- to be for seasons;
- to be for days and years;
- to give light upon the earth;
- to rule the day and over the night;
- to divide the light from the darkness.

The text then records that God made two great lights—the greater light to rule the day, and the lesser light to rule the night—and made the stars also. God set them in the firmament of the heaven to give light upon the earth, to rule over the day and over the night, and to divide the light from the darkness. God saw that it was good.

## 3. Keep the two Genesis passages distinct

Genesis 1:3–5 and Genesis 1:14–19 both speak of light, division, and God's ordering. They must be read in their own textual sequence.

Genesis 1:3–5 records the appearance of light and its division from darkness, followed by naming and the first-day statement.

Genesis 1:14–19 records the appointment of the luminaries and explicitly states their functions in relation to signs, seasons, days, years, rule, and giving light upon the earth.

The scientific account of electromagnetic radiation does not, by itself, determine how these passages relate. That remains a theological and textual question. This audit preserves the distinction and does not force a physical chronology or mechanism into the text.

## 4. What the outward sciences contribute—and what they cannot settle

The outward light investigation has established a set of domain-specific findings:

| HOW-science | Findings relevant to light | Boundary of its answer |
|---|---|---|
| Physics | Electromagnetic radiation, propagation, wavelength, frequency, photon interactions | Describes physical behavior; does not establish ultimate purpose |
| Chemistry | Absorption, emission, molecular transitions, photochemistry | Describes interactions with matter; does not establish why Creation exists |
| Biology | Photosynthesis, photoreception, vision, growth and physiological responses | Describes living responses; does not establish the Author's ultimate purpose |
| Astronomy | Radiation from stars and other distant sources; spectral evidence | Infers properties of celestial sources; does not identify ultimate cause by measurement alone |
| Earth science | Incoming, reflected, absorbed, and emitted radiation; energy balance | Describes Earth-system processes; does not settle ultimate theological WHY |
| Information science | Signal variation, encoding, transmission, detection, and noise | Formalizes information-bearing signals; does not by itself supply theological meaning |
| Mathematics | Quantitative relationships, models, and predictions | Expresses and tests formal relations; an equation is not the Author or the purpose of Creation |

These findings are not discarded when the return journey begins. They remain the scientific account of HOW. Theology examines the larger question under the Scriptural foundation.

## 5. The Scriptural WHY: what can responsibly be stated

The KJV text permits the following bounded statements:

1. **Originating command:** light appears in response to God's spoken command (Genesis 1:3).
2. **Divine evaluation:** God sees that the light is good (Genesis 1:4).
3. **Distinction and naming:** God divides light from darkness and names Day and Night (Genesis 1:4–5).
4. **Appointment and function:** the luminaries are appointed for signs, seasons, days, years, giving light, rule, and division (Genesis 1:14–18).
5. **Divine evaluation of the appointed lights:** God sees that this arrangement is good (Genesis 1:18).

These statements identify the Author's agency and the purposes explicitly stated in the text. They do not authorize us to invent additional purposes and present them as the wording of Genesis.

A broader theological reading may connect these acts to the ordering and governance of Creation, but each such conclusion must be labeled as interpretation and examined in its canonical context rather than presented as a direct quotation or explicit technical statement of Genesis.

## 6. The return journey in the light case

**Outward journey — HOW**

Author → Word → Creation → physical light investigated through differentiated sciences → measurements, mechanisms, models, and cross-verified findings.

**Return journey — WHY**

Cross-verified findings → Theology / Queen of the Sciences → examination under Genesis 1–2 and the wider Scriptural witness → recognition of the Author, His acts, and the purposes Scripture actually states.

The return journey does not mean physics, chemistry, or biology can measure God's purpose. Nor does it mean theology can replace the technical work of those disciplines. It means that the scientific findings are not treated as an ultimate authority over the Creation or its Author.

## 9. Working conclusion

The outward journey shows that one created phenomenon, light, is investigated through multiple sciences. Their findings can be compared and cross-verified while their methods remain differentiated.

The return journey brings those findings to Theology, the Queen of the Sciences, for examination under Scripture. Genesis 1:3–5 identifies God's command, the appearance of light, His evaluation, His division, and His naming. Genesis 1:14–19 identifies the appointed lights and states their functions in relation to signs, seasons, days, years, rule, division, and giving light upon the earth.

The sciences describe HOW within their domains. Theology examines the ultimate WHY in relation to the Author and His revealed Word. The findings do not confer authority on the Author; they are reports about His Creation.
