# Primary-Source Verification — Double-Slit Experiment and Observer Anomaly

**Review date:** 2026-11-17  
**Parent record:** [Evidence Audit — Diagnostic Case Trace 004: Double-Slit Experiment and Observer Anomaly](EVIDENCE-AUDIT-Double-Slit-and-Observer-Anomaly.md)  
**Historical source:** Original Diagnostic Case Trace 004, preserved unchanged in the original repository.  
**Scope:** Verify the claims about interference, which-path information, decoherence, quantum erasure, and the proposed gravitational which-path test. This is a focused literature check, not a comprehensive review of all interpretations of quantum mechanics.

## 1. Executive assessment

**Status: PARTIAL — the existing evidence audit's central corrections are supported.**

The primary literature supports a precise statement: coherent alternatives can produce interference; physical interactions can correlate path alternatives with a detector or environment; distinguishability of the alternatives limits the available fringe visibility; and quantum-eraser experiments can recover interference in appropriately sorted subensembles. This does not make consciousness a necessary cause of measurement.

The source trace's “data-type translation” language is not a standard physical derivation. It may be retained only as a clearly labelled analogy for the transition from coherent alternatives to a detector record. Decoherence is not, by itself, a complete solution to the measurement problem, and it should not be equated with every interpretation of wavefunction collapse.

The proposed gravitational which-path test is not yet a testable protocol: the source trace gives no Hamiltonian or interaction model, coupling, apparatus, observable, noise analysis, quantitative visibility prediction, or feasibility argument.

## 2. Claim Q-01 — Interference and distinguishable paths

**Primary source:** Berthold-Georg Englert, “Fringe Visibility and Which-Way Information: An Inequality,” *Physical Review Letters* 77, 2154 (1996): https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.77.2154

Englert derives an inequality relating fringe visibility (V) to which-way distinguishability (D):

\[
D^2+V^2\leq 1.
\]

The result quantitatively expresses a trade-off between the degree to which path alternatives can be distinguished and the visibility of interference fringes. It does not require that a conscious observer look at the detector. The relevant information can be physically stored in a which-way marker.

**Interpretive boundary:** This inequality is not a declaration that a particle is a classical fluid traveling as a material object through both slits. The quantum state and the probability amplitudes describe the alternatives. “Both paths” language should be tied to the formal state description and the experimental arrangement, not asserted as an unqualified classical trajectory.

**Disposition:** PASS for the complementarity relation within its assumptions; PARTIAL for popularized descriptions of what the particle literally “does” between source and screen.

## 3. Claim Q-02 — Physical path marking and quantum erasure

**Primary experimental source:** S. P. Walborn, M. O. Terra Cunha, S. Pádua, and C. H. Monken, “Double-slit quantum eraser,” *Physical Review A* 65, 033818 (2002): https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevA.65.033818

The experiment uses a Young double slit and polarization markers to make the paths distinguishable. This removes the unconditioned interference pattern. Measuring the correlated photon's polarization permits interference to be recovered in selected coincidence subensembles.

**Additional experimental source:** Stephan Dürr and Gerhard Rempe, “Complementarity and quantum erasure in an atom interferometer,” *Optics Communications* 179 (2000) 323–336: https://doi.org/10.1016/S0030-4018(99)00492-7

This atom-interferometer experiment studies partial which-way information and quantum erasure, comparing the obtained path knowledge with the visibilities of resulting interference patterns.

**What these experiments show:** The physical distinguishability of alternatives and the correlations with marker systems affect observed interference. In suitable experimental arrangements, conditional interference can be recovered when the path-marking information is erased or measured in a basis that does not reveal which path.

**What they do not show:** They do not show that a human mind creates the outcome, that the particle becomes a classical solid object, or that the wavefunction literally “prints” its terminal values onto a boundary.

**Disposition:** PASS for the experimental claims about path marking, reduced visibility, and quantum erasure in the stated setups.

## 4. Claim Q-03 — Decoherence and the measurement problem

**Primary theoretical distinction:** Decoherence models the entangling interaction between a system and environmental degrees of freedom and explains why interference between alternatives becomes suppressed in the reduced state when environmental degrees of freedom are not tracked.

The trace's account that apparatus/environment correlations can suppress phase coherence is qualitatively sound. However, decoherence does not alone select a unique outcome in every interpretation or settle the full measurement problem. A global system-plus-environment state may remain entangled even while the reduced state of the subsystem behaves as an incoherent mixture for the relevant observables.

**Terminology boundary:**
- **Measurement interaction:** physical coupling between system and apparatus/marker.
- **Entanglement:** correlations in the joint quantum state.
- **Decoherence:** suppression of observable interference in a reduced description due to correlations with untracked degrees of freedom.
- **Detector record:** a physical record produced by the apparatus.
- **Collapse/state update:** a formal or interpretive account of outcome assignment; its meaning differs across interpretations.

These are related but not interchangeable. A decoherence calculation must not silently be treated as a proof that every interpretation's account of definite outcomes is solved.

**Disposition:** PASS for the qualitative decoherence mechanism; PARTIAL if used as a complete explanation of definite outcomes.

## 5. Claim Q-04 — Consciousness and observation

The primary experiments above use physical apparatuses and correlations; no conscious observer is required for the which-way marker to affect interference. In technical quantum mechanics, “measurement” ordinarily concerns an interaction and an outcome-recording procedure, not necessarily a conscious act.

**Disposition:** FAIL for the source trace's blanket assertion that standard interpretations falsely attribute collapse to consciousness. Interpretations differ; it is inaccurate to attribute a consciousness requirement to standard quantum mechanics as a whole.

## 6. Claim Q-05 — “Data-type translation” and terminal printing

The proposed “uncompiled wave data → localized coordinate entry” description is not a standard physical law or a derivation of the measurement postulate. The phrase may be useful as an analogy for the fact that a coherent quantum description and a recorded detector outcome play different roles.

However:
- a detector record is produced by a physical interaction;
- the system and apparatus may become entangled;
- environmental correlations can suppress interference;
- the formal account of outcomes is not exhausted by saying the wavefunction “prints” a terminal value;
- decoherence alone does not settle all interpretations of definite outcomes.

**Disposition:** UNRESOLVED as a GUA-W analogy; NOT ESTABLISHED as a physical mechanism. Do not label it a verified data-type law.

## 7. Claim Q-06 — Proposed gravitational which-path test

The source proposes measuring the tiny gravitational effect of a photon passing through one slit without direct particle interaction.

No quantitative protocol is supplied. In particular, the trace does not specify:
1. the photon state, slit geometry, and relevant gravitational field;
2. the apparatus or probe that couples to the gravitational field;
3. the interaction Hamiltonian or coupling strength;
4. how the probe becomes correlated with a particular path;
5. a path-distinguishability measure and predicted fringe visibility;
6. noise, decoherence, back-action, and sensitivity limits;
7. how the proposed result would discriminate among competing physical models.

The general principle that any physical which-path information correlated with alternatives can affect interference is already covered by standard quantum theory and complementarity. A specifically gravitational implementation would need an explicit model and a feasible measurement scheme before it could count as a novel verification test.

**Disposition:** PROPOSED / NOT YET SPECIFIED. It cannot presently be called a demonstrated experiment, an independently verified prediction, or a unique test of GUA-W.

## 9. GUA-W comparison boundary

A detector is a physical system that interacts with another physical system and produces a record. A human witness may later interpret that record. Those roles should not be collapsed into one thing.

The possible GUA-W comparison is therefore a question about the distinction among interaction, registration, record, and interpretation. It is not evidence that a detector is the theological Witness, that quantum decoherence is GUA-W activation, or that the measurement formalism establishes the completed architecture.

The completed GUA-W is not altered by this study. The analogy remains subordinate and may be rejected if it fails any part of the Triple Constraint.
