# Repository Documentation: Provenance, Scope, and Governance Review

**Review date:** 2026-11-19  
**Scope:** Documentation quality and the distinction between repository subject matter, project procedures, and governing records.  
**Status:** Active component of the repository-wide witness audit; findings and coverage are maintained as files are examined.

## 1. Purpose

This review examines whether repository documents make their purpose, intended audience, provenance, and scope clear. Its concern is the quality and traceability of documentation, not the authority of any individual file over the repository as a whole.

## 2. Finding DPG-001 — Maintenance material requires clear document scope

**Severity:** Medium — documentation clarity and governance  
**Observation:** A draft repository audit combined research findings with a future-maintenance checklist. That combination made it less clear which passages were audit evidence, which were recommendations for repository maintainers, and which described project conventions.  
**Effect:** When these categories are mixed, readers can mistake a proposed procedure for an established repository rule or treat explanatory examples as adopted policy.  
**Recommended treatment:** Keep findings, recommendations, established project conventions, and historical examples in clearly labeled sections. Identify the intended audience and status of each procedural passage. Recommendations should not be presented as already-adopted governance.

## 3. Documentation categories

For consistent interpretation, repository records should distinguish:

- **Evidence:** sourced observations, data, quotations, and documented results.
- **Analysis:** interpretation of evidence, including limitations and alternative explanations.
- **Project convention:** an established naming, filing, or review practice.
- **Recommendation:** a proposed improvement awaiting review or adoption.
- **Historical record:** a dated account of earlier work, retained for provenance.
- **Unresolved item:** a question or claim whose status remains open.

These categories describe the status of repository content; they do not alter the governing GUA-W architecture.

## 4. Finding DPG-002 — Repository-wide consistency requires provenance and status labels

**Severity:** Medium — cross-file traceability  
**Observation:** A finding may appear in a source investigation, a synthesis, a book chapter, and one or more registers. If those appearances do not identify their source and status, readers may be unable to distinguish independent evidence from repeated summaries.  
**Effect:** Repetition can be mistaken for corroboration, and a provisional interpretation can appear settled merely because it has propagated into several documents.  
**Recommended treatment:** Where a material finding is reused, retain a traceable link to its source record and preserve its evidence status, scope, and limitations. A summary is not an independent source.

## 5. Review checklist

A documentation review can ask:

1. Is the intended purpose and audience of the document clear?
2. Are evidence, interpretation, recommendation, convention, and unresolved questions distinguishable?
3. Are quotations and claims traceable to their sources?
4. Are dates and status labels current?
5. Do cross-file summaries preserve the original scope and limitations?
6. Are proposed changes clearly distinguished from adopted project conventions?
7. Does the document describe the existing GUA-W architecture accurately without silently changing it?

## 6. Witnessing and GUA-W placement

The repository audit witnesses what the repository reports. Every file, claim, citation, interpretation, and cross-reference is within the scope of that witness: what is stated, where it is stated, what source is offered, how it is connected to other records, and whether another file reports something different.

**Witnessing a claim is not the same as declaring the claim true or placing it within GUA-W.** The audit records the claim faithfully and traces its supporting material. GUA-W's governing framework then provides the place to examine its admissibility and fit under the Triple Constraint Law: Biblical compliance, scientific coherence, and consistency with GUA-W. A claim may be faithfully witnessed while its interpretation, evidence, or placement remains unresolved.

The repository-wide audit is therefore not restricted to a preliminary documentation review. Its scope is the whole repository. File-by-file examination and cross-reference tracing are the means by which that complete witness record is established. Coverage should be recorded transparently as the work proceeds, and the completed audit should account for every tracked file and every material cross-file relationship.

The purpose is not to certify every reported statement as true. It is to ensure that nothing reported is overlooked, misrepresented, disconnected from its source, or silently promoted into GUA-W without examination.
