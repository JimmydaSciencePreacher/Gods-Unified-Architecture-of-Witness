# Evidence Audit — Diagnostic Case Trace 004: Double-Slit Experiment and Observer Anomaly

**Review date:** 2026-11-16  
**Source basis:** Claims examined in this audit are assessed against the quantum-mechanical sources cited below.  
**Purpose:** Examine the source trace's claims about superposition, which-path measurement, decoherence, observation, and the proposed GUA-W data-handoff analogy. This audit is self-contained and evaluates the claims against the cited scientific literature.

## 1. Executive assessment

**Overall status: PARTIAL, with major corrections required.**

The source trace correctly identifies the double-slit experiment, the role of measurement interactions, and decoherence as relevant to the suppression of interference.

But it presents several interpretations as settled physical facts:
- that an unmeasured particle literally travels through both slits “as a fluid, non-localized wave”;
- that placing a detector makes the particle become a classical solid object passing through one slot;
- that measurement is fundamentally a conversion from “uncompiled wave data” into a localized coordinate record;
- that standard interpretations falsely claim consciousness creates physical reality;
- that the experiment proves coherence is bound exclusively by how data is indexed and routed.

These formulations overstate or mischaracterize the formalism. The experiment demonstrates interference and the effect of obtaining which-path information; it does not, by itself, select one interpretation of quantum mechanics or establish a GUA-W mechanism.

## 2. Claim D-01 — Superposition and the two paths

**Source claim:** Without a which-path detector, a quantum particle travels through both paths simultaneously as a fluid, non-localized wave.

**Assessment:** **PARTIAL — useful shorthand, but literal wording is misleading.**

In the standard quantum description, amplitudes associated with the two available paths can interfere. The observed interference pattern is predicted by adding amplitudes, not by treating the particle as a classical object splitting into two ordinary pieces of matter. The phrase “goes through both slits” is interpretation-sensitive shorthand; it should not be presented as an unqualified classical picture.

**Recommended wording:** When both paths remain coherent and no which-path information is available, the quantum state includes contributions from both paths, and their amplitudes can interfere at the detector.

## 3. Claim D-02 — Which-path measurement removes interference

**Source claim:** Deploying a detector at the slits to determine which path the particle takes makes the superposition vanish and causes it to behave as a classical solid object through one slit.

**Assessment:** **PARTIAL — the loss of interference is supported; the classical-object conclusion is not.**

A which-path interaction correlates the path alternatives with detector and/or environmental states. When the alternatives become distinguishable through those correlations, interference in the relevant observed distribution is suppressed. The particle does not thereby become a classical solid object in every respect. Quantum predictions still govern the system.

**Reference:** Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, *The Role of Decoherence in Quantum Mechanics*: https://plato.stanford.edu/archives/sum2022/entries/qm-decoherence/

## 4. Claim D-03 — Decoherence and entanglement

**Source claim:** The measuring apparatus entangles with the particle, suppressing phase coherence and distributing which-path information into environmental states.

**Assessment:** **PASS — with precision.**

This is a sound qualitative description of a central decoherence mechanism. The system becomes correlated with apparatus/environment degrees of freedom. When those environmental degrees of freedom are not tracked, the reduced description of the system exhibits suppressed interference between the relevant alternatives.

**Important limit:** Decoherence explains the practical suppression of interference in the reduced system and helps explain the emergence of stable classical-looking records. It does not, by itself, settle the full measurement problem or explain why one particular outcome is experienced. The Stanford Encyclopedia review explicitly distinguishes decoherence's explanatory contribution from a complete solution to the measurement problem.

**Reference:** Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, *The Role of Decoherence in Quantum Mechanics*: https://plato.stanford.edu/archives/sum2022/entries/qm-decoherence/

## 5. Claim D-04 — Measurement as data-type translation

**Source claim:** Measurement is a hardcoded conversion from fluid wave data into localized coordinate entries; measurement “prints” terminal values onto a boundary.

**Assessment:** **UNRESOLVED AS A GUA-W ANALOGY; NOT AN ESTABLISHED PHYSICAL DESCRIPTION.**

A detector produces a physical interaction and a record. Quantum measurement can change the state of the system, and the formal treatment of measurement outcomes depends on the measurement model and interpretive framework. The phrase “data-type translation” may be retained as a metaphor for the transition from a coherent quantum description to a recorded measurement outcome, but it is not a standard derivation of wavefunction collapse.

The claim that the wavefunction merely “prints its terminal values” is especially incomplete: it leaves out the measurement interaction, correlations with the apparatus, the role of the environment, and the interpretive question of definite outcomes.

**Reference:** David Tong, *Quantum Mechanics*, section 1.2.3, “The Collapse of the Wavefunction”: https://davidtong.org/teaching/quantum-mechanics/qmhtml/S1

## 6. Claim D-05 — Consciousness and the observer effect

**Source claim:** Standard interpretations falsely claim that observation or consciousness alters physical reality.

**Assessment:** **FAIL — as a blanket characterization of standard interpretations.**

“Observation” in quantum mechanics does not generally mean conscious awareness. Measurement is modeled through physical interactions, measurement operators, apparatuses, and/or system-environment correlations, depending on the formal approach. Interpretations differ on the status of measurement and outcomes; it is inaccurate to claim that standard interpretations uniformly require human consciousness to create physical reality.

**References:**
- Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, *The Role of Decoherence in Quantum Mechanics*: https://plato.stanford.edu/archives/sum2022/entries/qm-decoherence/
- MIT OpenCourseWare, *Quantum Theory I*, Lecture 1 notes: https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/8-321-quantum-theory-i-fall-2017/d08f5dcf8551694381fe47bc9787bb49_MIT8_321F17_lec1.pdf

## 7. Claim D-06 — Proposed gravitational which-path test

**Source proposal:** Measure the tiny gravitational effect of a photon passing through one slit without direct particle interaction; if enough which-path information is extracted, interference should dissolve.

**Assessment:** **PROPOSED / NOT YET ESTABLISHED BY THIS TRACE.**

The proposal gestures toward an important general principle: in quantum experiments, information about path alternatives can become correlated with other physical degrees of freedom, and distinguishability is related to interference visibility. But the specific gravitational experiment is not specified with a complete physical model, apparatus, coupling strength, noise budget, observable, or feasibility analysis.

The source trace therefore has not demonstrated that its proposed test can be performed as described or that it uniquely tests the proposed GUA-W interpretation.

**Required before further use:** Define the quantum system, gravitational interaction, measurement apparatus, path-distinguishability observable, predicted interference visibility, competing explanations, and realistic sensitivity limits.

## 9. Revised claim boundaries for future use

The following wording is supportable:

> In a double-slit experiment, coherent contributions from the available paths can interfere. When a physical interaction correlates path alternatives with a detector or environment so that which-path information becomes available, interference in the relevant observed distribution is suppressed. Decoherence describes this suppression in the reduced description of the system. This does not mean that consciousness is required for measurement, that the particle becomes a classical solid object, or that the measurement problem is fully solved. A GUA-W mapping of measurement as witness registration remains an analogy requiring separate argument.
