# Strange and Fundamental Science Survey: Fifty Investigations Across Ten Domains

**Status:** Proposed cross-disciplinary research program  
**Prepared:** 2026-11-18  
**Scope:** Ten working scientific domains; five candidate investigations in each  
**Purpose:** Build a broad, evidence-led field of comparison without forcing different scientific mechanisms into one explanation.

## 1. Purpose

The biological investigations showed the value of selecting unusual, large-scale, fundamental, unresolved, and highly interconnected phenomena. This survey extends that method across ten scientific domains.

The ten domains are a practical working map, not a claim that the sciences have exactly ten mutually exclusive branches. Fields overlap. For example, thermodynamics is part of physics and chemistry, information theory connects mathematics and computer science, and materials science draws on physics, chemistry, and engineering.

The goal is to establish a broad range of evidence and questions. Each candidate must be investigated in its own scientific context before any cross-disciplinary comparison is attempted.

## 2. Selection principles

Candidates were chosen to represent one or more of these qualities:

- **Strange:** counterintuitive, surprising, or unlike ordinary experience.
- **Large-scale:** extraordinary in size, energy, duration, or consequence.
- **Fundamental:** relevant to basic properties, laws, structures, or processes.
- **Unresolved:** important questions remain open or competing interpretations exist.
- **Interconnected:** reveals relationships among parts, systems, environments, or disciplines.

A candidate's strangeness is not evidence of a supernatural cause, a universal mechanism, or a GUA-W architectural operation. The investigation must distinguish observation, experimental result, model, interpretation, and open question.

## 3. Ten domains and fifty candidate investigations

### Domain 1 — Physics

**Completed Investigation I:** [Quantum Entanglement](PHYSICS-INVESTIGATION-I-QUANTUM-ENTANGLEMENT.md).

**Completed Investigation II:** [Superconductivity](PHYSICS-INVESTIGATION-II-SUPERCONDUCTIVITY.md).

**Completed Investigation III:** [Bose–Einstein Condensation](PHYSICS-INVESTIGATION-III-BOSE-EINSTEIN-CONDENSATION.md).

**Completed Investigation IV:** [Neutron Stars](PHYSICS-INVESTIGATION-IV-NEUTRON-STARS.md).

1. **Quantum entanglement:** How do separated systems exhibit correlated measurement outcomes?
2. **Superfluidity:** How can matter flow with effectively zero viscosity?
3. **Superconductivity:** How can certain materials conduct electrical current with zero resistance?
4. **Black holes:** What happens to matter, energy, and information near an event horizon?
5. **Mandelbrot set:** How does a simple iterative mathematical rule generate an intricate boundary with structure at arbitrarily fine scales?

### Domain 2 — Chemistry

1. **Belousov–Zhabotinsky reaction:** How can a chemical reaction produce repeating waves and oscillations?
2. **Molecular self-assembly:** How do molecules organize into larger structures?
3. **DNA origami:** How can DNA be used to construct designed nanoscale shapes?
4. **Autocatalysis:** How can reaction products help generate more of themselves?
5. **Metal-organic frameworks:** How can crystalline materials contain extensive internal pore networks?

### Domain 3 — Astronomy and Cosmology

1. **Black holes:** How do objects form with event horizons, and how are they observed?
2. **Neutron stars:** How does matter behave at extraordinary densities?
3. **Cosmic web:** How did galaxies become organized into enormous structures?
4. **Gravitational lensing:** How does gravity bend the path of light?
5. **Dark matter:** What accounts for gravitational effects not explained by visible matter?

### Domain 4 — Earth Sciences and Geology

1. **Hydrothermal vents:** How can thriving ecosystems exist without sunlight as their primary energy source?
2. **Mantle plumes:** How can deep Earth processes contribute to volcanic hotspots?
3. **Columnar basalt:** How does cooling rock form remarkably regular geometric columns?
4. **Naica crystal caves:** Under what conditions do enormous crystals grow underground?
5. **Plate tectonics:** How does Earth's surface move and recycle over geological time?

### Domain 5 — Biology and Evolution

1. **Lichen symbiosis:** How do distinct organisms participate in a persistent living association?
2. **Tardigrade extremotolerance:** How can some microscopic animals survive severe environmental stress?
3. **Axolotl regeneration:** How can a lost limb be reconstructed?
4. **Butterfly metamorphosis:** How does extensive developmental remodeling produce an adult organism?
5. **Venus flytrap mechanosensation:** How does a plant detect and respond rapidly to mechanical stimulation?

**Continuity note:** The repository already contains five detailed biological investigations: bee orchid, spiny leaf insect, butterfly, axolotl, and zebrafish. These remain the established biological base. The five candidates above are a further shortlist, not a replacement or a claim that the existing cases are complete.

### Domain 6 — Neuroscience

1. **Split-brain experiments:** What changes when communication between brain hemispheres is disrupted?
2. **Neuroplasticity:** How can neural connections change with experience?
3. **Place cells and grid cells:** How does the brain represent spatial position and navigation?
4. **Visual illusions:** Why can perception differ from the physical stimulus?
5. **Neural synchronization:** How do populations of neurons coordinate their activity?

### Domain 7 — Mathematics

1. **Fractals:** How can patterns repeat across different scales?
2. **Strange attractors:** How can deterministic equations produce complex behavior?
3. **Topology:** Which properties remain invariant under continuous transformation?
4. **Non-Euclidean geometry:** What follows when familiar assumptions of geometry are changed?
5. **Infinity and paradoxes:** How can infinite sets behave differently from finite collections?

### Domain 8 — Computer and Information Science

1. **Cellular automata:** How can simple local rules generate complex global patterns?
2. **Emergent computation:** How can collective behavior perform computation?
3. **Distributed systems:** How can independent computers coordinate without one central controller?
4. **Information theory:** How can information be measured, compressed, and transmitted reliably?
5. **Error-correcting codes:** How can a message remain recoverable despite corrupted data?

### Domain 9 — Thermodynamics and Complex Systems

1. **Dissipative structures:** How can ordered patterns arise in systems exchanging energy and matter?
2. **Convection cells:** Why can heating produce organized geometric patterns?
3. **Reaction-diffusion systems:** How can local reactions and diffusion generate spatial patterns?
4. **Self-organizing flocks:** How can coordinated group movement arise from local interactions?
5. **Entropy and information:** How are physical entropy, information, and constraints related?

### Domain 10 — Materials Science

1. **Self-healing materials:** How can a material repair damage without conventional replacement?
2. **Shape-memory alloys:** How can a material recover a previously established shape?
3. **Aerogels:** How can solids contain extensive pore space while retaining a solid framework?
4. **Metamaterials:** How can engineered structures produce unusual electromagnetic or mechanical behavior?
5. **Spider silk:** How can a biological material combine strength, toughness, and flexibility?

## 4. Rules for conducting each investigation

Each selected study should use a consistent evidence record:

1. **Phenomenon:** What is observed?
2. **Scientific question:** What specifically needs explaining?
3. **Established evidence:** What has been measured, observed, replicated, or demonstrated?
4. **Mechanism:** What explanation is supported, and at what level?
5. **Competing explanations:** Are there alternatives or active disputes?
6. **Limits:** What does the evidence not establish?
7. **Open questions:** What remains unresolved?
8. **Cross-disciplinary relevance:** Which other domains may share a question, without assuming a shared mechanism?
9. **Triple Constraint disposition:** Biblical compliance, scientific coherence, and GUA-W consistency assessed separately.
10. **Repository placement:** Link the case into the relevant domain and comparative indexes without changing GUA-W architecture.

A striking headline is not sufficient evidence. Prefer primary research, authoritative scientific institutions, systematic reviews, and specialist references. Record publication date and distinguish established results from preliminary findings.

## 5. Cross-domain questions to preserve

As the investigations accumulate, useful comparison questions may include:

- How do constraints shape the range of possible outcomes?
- How do local interactions relate to large-scale patterns?
- How do systems preserve identity while their components change?
- How do energy and matter move through organized systems?
- How does information become available, stored, transmitted, or measured?
- What distinguishes a stable structure from a temporary pattern?
- How do systems interact with environments and other systems?
- Where does scientific description end and interpretation begin?

These are research questions, not proposed universal laws. Similar vocabulary across domains does not establish identical mechanisms.

## 6. Recommended research order

Proceed one domain at a time. For each domain:

1. Verify and refine the five candidate topics.
2. Select the strongest case for the first full investigation.
3. Write its evidence record with sources and limitations.
4. Compare it with existing investigations only after its own mechanism is clear.
5. Preserve unresolved questions.
6. Complete the domain's five cases before drawing a domain-level synthesis.

Suggested starting order: Physics, Chemistry, Astronomy and Cosmology, Earth Sciences and Geology, Neuroscience, Mathematics, Computer and Information Science, Thermodynamics and Complex Systems, Materials Science. Biology remains an active existing research base and can receive additional cases when a specific question warrants them.
