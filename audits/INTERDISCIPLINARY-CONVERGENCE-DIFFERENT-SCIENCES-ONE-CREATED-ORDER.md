# Interdisciplinary Convergence: Different Sciences, One Created Order

## Central research question

**Are the Sciences discovering different properties and relationships of the same created order, with each discipline revealing something that the others cannot independently explain, yet all ultimately pointing back to the One Author?**

This is the question under investigation, not a conclusion presumed in advance.

## Correction to the model

The sciences are **not arranged as a one-way sequence** such as Light → Water → Chemistry → Biology. That representation falsely suggests a linear chain in which one discipline simply hands a result to the next.

The domains are distinct and interconnected. Their relationships may be reciprocal, overlapping, conditional, or networked. A phenomenon can involve several domains at once, while each discipline examines different properties, scales, mechanisms, and forms of evidence.

The working model is therefore a **relational structure**, not a production line.

## Initial domain distinctions

- **Physics / light:** electromagnetic radiation, energy transfer, absorption, reflection, scattering, and physical interactions.
- **Water science:** phase behavior, solvent properties, transport, thermal effects, and participation in physical and biological processes.
- **Chemistry:** molecular structure, reactions, bonding, concentration, diffusion, and chemical signaling.
- **Biology:** living organization, metabolism, development, sensing, regulation, reproduction, and interactions with environments.
- **Mathematics and information theory:** cross-cutting tools for describing quantitative relationships, patterns, uncertainty, and information. These are not substitutes for the physical mechanisms being described.

These are working distinctions, not sealed compartments. No single discipline should be assumed to explain the whole phenomenon merely because it can model one important part.

## Examples of cross-domain relationships to investigate

1. Light is absorbed, reflected, or scattered by matter; absorption can produce molecular and thermal effects.
2. Water changes how light propagates through a medium and also participates in chemical transport and reactions.
3. Chemical reactions can depend on solvent conditions, temperature, and energy input.
4. Living organisms receive and respond to light, water availability, and chemical cues; organisms also alter their chemical and physical surroundings.
5. Chemical signals can move through air or water and be received by biological sensory systems. Signal production, transmission, reception, behavioral response, and biological outcome must be treated as distinct events.

These examples demonstrate why a linear sequence is inadequate. They do not, by themselves, establish a universal law or prove that every relationship is reciprocal.

## The Bee Orchid as a shared biological witness

The Bee Orchid (*Ophrys apifera*) remains a shared witness in both the light and water investigations. It provides a biological case in which multiple questions can be examined without collapsing them:

- How does the flower interact with incident light?
- How do water availability, humidity, and the plant's water relations affect its living function?
- What floral volatile compounds are produced, and under what conditions?
- What evidence shows that insects detect or respond to those compounds?
- How do chemical signaling, insect behavior, pollinia movement, self-pollination, and reproductive outcome relate—and where must they remain distinct?

A crucial boundary: the presence of floral volatile compounds does not by itself prove a particular communicative function or behavioral effect. Nor should water transfer between flower and bee be asserted without evidence. Environmental water relations, volatile chemical signaling, and physical pollen transfer are separate routes to investigate.

The species' predominantly self-pollinating tendency makes it especially useful: floral scent, possible insect interaction, and successful reproduction must not be assumed to be one and the same process. Regional variation and the limits of species-specific evidence must be preserved.

## Investigation method: outward journey and return journey

### Outward journey — scientific HOW

1. Identify a phenomenon or question.
2. Investigate it through the relevant sciences, allowing multiple disciplines to examine the same phenomenon from different angles.
3. Record the mechanism each discipline can explain and the questions it cannot independently settle.
4. Label every proposed connection by its actual relationship: energy transfer, material transport, chemical reaction, signal transmission, reception, regulation, or another evidenced mechanism.
5. Separate observation, measurement, model, interpretation, and unresolved hypothesis.
6. Cross-check the connections without forcing them into a single reductionist explanation.

### Return journey — theological WHY

Bring the cross-checked findings back to Theology, the Queen of the Sciences, and examine them under Scripture—beginning with the literal KJV Genesis 1–2 foundation and the established GUA-W constraints.

The return journey is not a claim that theology supplies missing laboratory mechanisms, nor that scientific findings prove Scripture. It asks what the differentiated findings disclose when considered within the biblical account of one Author and one Creation.

1. Biblically compliant.

If any one fails, reject or hold the claim unresolved.

- a relationship with a one-way sequence;

## Current status

**Research question established. Structure not yet declared.**

The evidence may reveal a coherent relational structure across differentiated sciences. Whether, how, and at what level that structure can be responsibly identified remains to be investigated. Preserve counterexamples, limits, and unresolved questions rather than forcing a premature unification.

The guiding hierarchy remains: **Author → Word → Creation → Witness.** The sciences are differentiated witnesses investigating properties and relationships within Creation; they are not the Author and do not become the authority over the Source.
