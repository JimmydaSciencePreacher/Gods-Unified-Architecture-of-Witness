# Rejected and Unresolved Register

This register prevents open questions from being silently treated as settled. Entries may be updated as evidence is gathered. A question listed here is not automatically rejected; its status is stated individually.

## Unresolved

### U-001 — Cross-domain architectural equivalence

**Question:** Do mathematical topology, physical conservation/persistence, and Scriptural descriptions of differentiation and propagation express one common architecture or mechanism?

**Current status:** UNRESOLVED.

**Reason:** The domains establish different kinds of results under different definitions and assumptions. Similarities may support a comparison, but a common mechanism or full equivalence has not been demonstrated.

**Guardrail:** Do not promote analogy or correspondence to identity.

**Related investigation:** Identity, Change, and Persistence evidence audit.

### U-002 — Identity, persistence, and state

**Question:** What precisely constitutes identity across change, and how does persistence relate to identity and state in GUA-W?

**Current status:** UNRESOLVED.

**Reason:** Topological invariance and physical conservation each have specific formal meanings. Neither, by itself, supplies a complete GUA-W definition of identity or persistence.

**Guardrail:** Do not equate mathematical invariance, conserved quantities, biological continuity, and metaphysical identity without a separately supported argument.

### U-003 — Differentiation and propagation

**Question:** What is the exact architectural relationship between Differentiation and Propagation?

**Current status:** UNRESOLVED.

**Reason:** Scriptural passages describe distinctions, kinds, seed, fruitfulness, and multiplication. The operational mapping of those descriptions to GUA-W runtime stages remains interpretive.

**Guardrail:** Preserve the distinction between what Genesis says and the proposed runtime mapping.

### U-004 — Conditions for persistence through change

**Question:** Under what conditions does a realized configuration persist, transform, or cease to persist?

**Current status:** UNRESOLVED.

**Reason:** Conditions vary by domain and have not yet been integrated into a rigorously defined GUA-W account.

**Guardrail:** Do not assume one universal persistence law across all domains.

### U-005 — Drift

**Question:** What does Drift mean in GUA-W, and how can it be distinguished from change, variation, mutation, noise, entropy, error, or failure?

**Current status:** UNRESOLVED.

**Reason:** The term has not yet received a sufficiently precise operational definition and admissibility test.

**Guardrail:** Do not equate Drift with sin, entropy, mutation, noise, or system failure by default.

### U-006 — Definition and components of human will

**Question:** Can human will be defined from Scripture without importing a modern philosophical definition, and how are perception, knowledge, evaluation, desire, choice, heeding, action, and authority related or distinguished?

**Current status:** UNRESOLVED.

**Evidence reviewed:** Genesis 3:1–24, especially 3:5–7, 3:8–13, 3:17, and 3:22; Genesis 2:16–17 as the prior command context.

**Reason:** Genesis 3 narrates distinguishable elements: competing communication, seeing and evaluating, desire, taking/eating, heeding another's voice, changed awareness, fear, and divine assessment. It does not provide a formal or exhaustive definition of “will” or “autonomous will.” The relation among these elements requires wider Scriptural examination.

**Guardrail:** Do not define will as merely the ability to choose, act, evaluate, or know. Do not assume that capacity to evaluate entails authority to establish the standard of good and evil.

**Related investigation:** [Genesis 3:1–24 — Text-First Reconstruction](scriptural-reconstruction/GENESIS-3-1-24-TEXT-FIRST-RECONSTRUCTION.md).

**Revision history:** Added 2026-11-16 following the first text-first reconstruction of Genesis 3.

### U-007 — Knowledge of good and evil versus authority to establish its standard

**Question:** When Genesis 3:22 says that man has become “as one of us, to know good and evil,” what kind of knowledge is acknowledged, and does any Scriptural evidence establish that humanity acquired independent authority to determine the standard of good and evil?

**Current status:** UNRESOLVED.

**Evidence reviewed:** Genesis 3:5 and 3:22, compared with Genesis 3:6–7; further comparison with Deuteronomy 1:39, 2 Samuel 19:35–36, 1 Kings 3:9, and Genesis 4:6–7 remains a priority.

**Reason:** Genesis 3:22 confirms a changed human condition involving knowledge of good and evil. The verse does not state that humanity became the Author, acquired God's authority, or became the independent source of the standard. The precise sense of “knowing” and its relation to discernment, experience, judgment, and authority require further contextual study.

**Guardrail:** Do not collapse knowledge, discernment, evaluation, judgment, and authority into one concept. Do not infer a transfer of divine authority from the phrase “as one of us.”

**Related investigation:** [Genesis 3:1–24 — Text-First Reconstruction](scriptural-reconstruction/GENESIS-3-1-24-TEXT-FIRST-RECONSTRUCTION.md).

**Revision history:** Added 2026-11-16 following the first text-first reconstruction of Genesis 3.


## 12. Evidence update — Genesis 4:6–7 (2026-11-16)

The following update supplements U-006 and U-007; it does not resolve either question or change their status.

### U-006 supplement — Human will, responsibility, desire, and mastery

**New evidence reviewed:** Genesis 4:6–7, in continuity with Genesis 4:1–8 and Genesis 3:16–17.

**Textual observations:**
- God addresses Cain's anger/distress and fallen countenance before the murder (4:6; compare 4:5 and 4:8).
- God presents the conditional contrast “if thou doest well” / “if thou doest not well” (4:7).
- The verse depicts a crouching figure/entity at the door, with desire toward Cain, and addresses Cain as the one to rule/master (4:7).
- The Hebrew imperfect תִּמְשָׁל (*timšāl*) does not, by morphology alone, settle whether the force is prediction, obligation, or potential capacity. Translation and context must be considered.
- The referent of the masculine suffixes in תְּשׁוּקָתוֹ (*təšûqātō*) and בּוֹ (*bō*) is complicated by the feminine grammatical gender of חַטָּאת (*ḥaṭṭāʾt*, “sin”) and the masculine participle רֹבֵץ (*rōḇēṣ*, “crouching”). The mismatch must remain visible; it does not by itself prove a separate demon/person.

**Effect on question:** Genesis 4:6–7 places human responsibility alongside anger/distress, an external or personified crouching threat, and desire. This weighs against collapsing responsibility into either passive exposure or mere choice. It does not provide a formal definition of will.

**Status remains:** UNRESOLVED.

### U-007 supplement — Knowledge, evaluation, and the governing standard

**New evidence reviewed:** Genesis 4:6–7, with Genesis 3:5–7, 3:17, and 3:22 as context.

**Textual observations:**
- God, not Cain, supplies the evaluative frame “if you do well / if you do not do well.”
- God addresses Cain's response and responsibility in relation to that frame.
- Genesis 4:6–7 does not repeat the expression “know good and evil” and therefore does not directly define the knowledge acknowledged in Genesis 3:22.
- The passage records no transfer of authority to Cain to establish or redefine the standard of good.

**Effect on question:** This passage supports keeping the capacity to evaluate distinct from authority to establish the standard. It does not settle the full meaning of Genesis 3:22.

**Status remains:** UNRESOLVED.

### Source and interpretation caution

The NET Bible notes offer a personified-animal interpretation of sin crouching and read the desire as an urge to dominate, while acknowledging alternatives for the force of the imperfect “rule.” A grammatical gender mismatch has also been discussed in translation scholarship. These are attributed interpretations, not unanimous findings. The repository reconstruction records the forms and competing possibilities rather than adopting one as settled.

## 13. Evidence update — Genesis 4:8 (2026-11-16)

This update supplements U-006 and U-007. Neither question is resolved.

### U-006 supplement — Responsibility and action after warning

Genesis 4:8 records Cain speaking to Abel, their being in the field, Cain rising against Abel, and Cain killing him. This follows immediately after God's address and warning in Genesis 4:6–7.

The sequence supports distinguishing emotional condition, divine warning, the desire/opposition described in 4:7, and Cain's subsequent action. It establishes Cain as the grammatical and narrative subject of the hostile rising and killing.

It does not establish a complete definition of will or prove that Cain acted from a philosophically autonomous will. Nor should Genesis 4:8 be described as Cain ruling or failing to rule Abel: the referent of the masculine suffixes in 4:7 remains disputed and is not simply Abel.

**Status remains:** UNRESOLVED.

### U-007 supplement — Conduct and the standard

Genesis 4:8 records Cain's action after God has spoken of doing well and not doing well. It does not say Cain claimed authority to redefine good and evil, nor does it describe his internal reasoning in detail.

The Masoretic reading does not preserve the content of Cain's speech to Abel. Some ancient witnesses include “Let us go out to the field”; this is documented as a textual variant, not silently imported into the KJV quotation.

**Status remains:** UNRESOLVED.
