# Rejected and Unresolved Register

This register distinguishes genuinely unresolved questions from matters already resolved. Entries must be updated when evidence and established conclusions settle a question; stale status labels must not be preserved as though the matter remained open. A question listed here is not automatically rejected; its status is stated individually.

## Unresolved

### U-001 — Cross-domain architectural equivalence

**Question:** Do mathematical topology, physical conservation/persistence, and Scriptural descriptions of differentiation and propagation express one common architecture or mechanism?

**Current status:** UNRESOLVED.

**Reason:** The domains establish different kinds of results under different definitions and assumptions. Similarities may support a comparison, but a common mechanism or full equivalence has not been demonstrated.

**Guardrail:** Do not promote analogy or correspondence to identity.

**Related investigation:** Identity, Change, and Persistence evidence audit.

### U-002 — Identity, persistence, and state

**Question:** What precisely constitutes identity across change, and how does persistence relate to identity and state in GUA-W?

**Current status:** UNRESOLVED.

**Reason:** Topological invariance and physical conservation each have specific formal meanings. Neither, by itself, supplies a complete GUA-W definition of identity or persistence.

**Guardrail:** Do not equate mathematical invariance, conserved quantities, biological continuity, and metaphysical identity without a separately supported argument.

### U-003 — Differentiation and propagation

**Question:** What is the exact architectural relationship between Differentiation and Propagation?

**Current status:** UNRESOLVED.

**Reason:** Scriptural passages describe distinctions, kinds, seed, fruitfulness, and multiplication. The operational mapping of those descriptions to GUA-W runtime stages remains interpretive.

**Guardrail:** Preserve the distinction between what Genesis says and the proposed runtime mapping.

### U-004 — Conditions for persistence through change

**Question:** Under what conditions does a realized configuration persist, transform, or cease to persist?

**Current status:** UNRESOLVED.

**Reason:** Conditions vary by domain and have not yet been integrated into a rigorously defined GUA-W account.

**Guardrail:** Do not assume one universal persistence law across all domains.

### U-005 — Drift

**Question:** What does Drift mean in GUA-W, and how can it be distinguished from change, variation, mutation, noise, entropy, error, or failure?

**Current status:** UNRESOLVED.

**Reason:** The term has not yet received a sufficiently precise operational definition and admissibility test.

**Guardrail:** Do not equate Drift with sin, entropy, mutation, noise, or system failure by default.

### U-006 — Definition and components of human will

**Question:** Can human will be defined from Scripture without importing a modern philosophical definition, and how are perception, knowledge, evaluation, desire, choice, heeding, action, and authority related or distinguished?

**Current status:** UNRESOLVED.

**Evidence reviewed:** Genesis 3:1–24, especially 3:5–7, 3:8–13, 3:17, and 3:22; Genesis 2:16–17 as the prior command context.

**Reason:** Genesis 3 narrates distinguishable elements: competing communication, seeing and evaluating, desire, taking/eating, heeding another's voice, changed awareness, fear, and divine assessment. It does not provide a formal or exhaustive definition of “will” or “autonomous will.” The relation among these elements requires wider Scriptural examination.

**Guardrail:** Do not define will as merely the ability to choose, act, evaluate, or know. Do not assume that capacity to evaluate entails authority to establish the standard of good and evil.

**Related investigation:** [Genesis 3:1–24 — Text-First Reconstruction](scriptural-reconstruction/GENESIS-3-1-24-TEXT-FIRST-RECONSTRUCTION.md).

**Revision history:** Added 2026-11-16 following the first text-first reconstruction of Genesis 3.

### U-007 — Knowledge of good and evil versus authority to establish its standard

**Status: RESOLVED — no transfer of divine authority to establish the standard of good and evil is described or granted.**

**Established conclusion:** Knowledge of good and evil does not confer God's authority or transfer to humanity the authority to establish the standard of good and evil. Capacity to perceive, evaluate, desire, choose, and act must be distinguished from authority to establish the governing standard.

**Evidence considered:** Genesis 2:16–17; 3:1–7, 3:22; 4:6–7, read in their narrative context.

**Findings:**
- In Genesis 3:1–5, the serpent challenges God's stated command and promises a changed condition of knowledge.
- Genesis 3:6 records Eve's seeing, evaluation, desire, taking, and eating. The text does not say that this action transferred God's authority to her or to humanity.
- Genesis 3:7 and 3:22 acknowledge a changed human condition and knowledge; neither passage says that humanity became the Author or the independent source of the standard.
- In Genesis 4:6–7, God supplies the evaluative frame—“if thou doest well” and “if thou doest not well”—and addresses Cain within that frame. The passage records no transfer to Cain of authority to define or redefine good.
- The wider Scriptural distinction is between a creature's capacity to evaluate/respond and the Author's authority as the source of the standard.

**Guardrail:** Do not collapse knowledge, discernment, evaluation, judgment, desire, choice, action, and authority into one concept. Do not infer a transfer of divine authority from “as one of us, to know good and evil” (Genesis 3:22).

**Boundary of this resolution:** This resolves the authority-transfer question. It does not, by itself, provide an exhaustive definition of human will or settle every semantic question concerning the Hebrew expression “to know good and evil.” U-006 remains a separate question.

**Related investigations:** [Genesis 3:1–24 — Text-First Reconstruction](scriptural-reconstruction/GENESIS-3-1-24-TEXT-FIRST-RECONSTRUCTION.md); [Genesis 4:6–7 — Text-First Reconstruction](scriptural-reconstruction/GENESIS-4-6-7-TEXT-FIRST-RECONSTRUCTION.md).

**Revision history:** Reclassified from UNRESOLVED to RESOLVED on 2026-11-17 after integrating the established Scriptural conclusion and the Genesis 4:6–7 evidence. Earlier U-007 supplements below are historical investigation notes and are superseded by this resolution; they are not evidence that the question remains open.

### U-008 — The relationship between John 20:22 and the outpouring at Pentecost

**Question:** What is the precise relationship between the risen Christ's breathing upon His disciples and command “Receive ye the Holy Ghost” (John 20:22), and the later outpouring in Acts 2? The inquiry must distinguish the extent/timing of reception from the function of the gift.

**Current status:** UNRESOLVED.

**Evidence reviewed:** Genesis 2:7; Ezekiel 37:1–14; John 7:37–39; 14:16–17, 26; 15:26; 16:7–15; 19:30, 34–35; 20:19–23; Luke 24:36–49; Acts 1:3–11; 2:1–39; Romans 8:11; 1 Corinthians 15:45–49.

**Established textual observations:**
- John 20:19–23 places Jesus' breathing and command to receive the Holy Ghost after His Resurrection, within His appearance and commission to the disciples.
- Acts 1 records Jesus' instruction to wait for the Father's promise and His promise of power for witness; Acts 2 records the disciples filled with the Holy Ghost and Peter's interpretation of the event in relation to the exalted Jesus.
- John 20:22's Greek breathing verb has a significant lexical correspondence with the LXX of Genesis 2:7; Ezekiel 37 remains a relevant Scriptural breath/Spirit/restoration parallel.
- The passages establish both the Resurrection-day scene and the later Pentecost event, but do not explicitly state the complete theological relation between them.

**Additional Lukan evidence (Luke 24:36–49; Acts 1:1–8):** The risen Jesus commands the disciples to wait in Jerusalem until they are endued with power from on high (Luke 24:49). Acts records the command to wait for the Father's promise, the promised baptism with the Holy Ghost, and power when the Holy Ghost comes upon them so that they will be witnesses (Acts 1:4–8). Acts 2:32–33 identifies the outpouring with the exalted Jesus' shedding forth of the Father's promise. This establishes a future endowment after the Resurrection; it does **not** explicitly say that no earlier reception or work of the Spirit occurred. The relationship to John 20:22 remains to be demonstrated.

**Ezekiel 36–37 evidence:** Ezekiel 36:25–27 places cleansing water, a new heart, a new spirit, and God's Spirit within Israel in a corporate restoration promise. Ezekiel 37:1–14 explicitly interprets the dry-bones vision as the restoration of the whole house of Israel from its death-like exile condition. Its breath sequence echoes Genesis 2:7; John 20:22's Greek verb ἐμφυσάω corresponds to the LXX verb in Genesis 2:7 and Ezekiel 37:9. This strongly supports a new-creation resonance in John 20:22 and a major Ezekiel 36 background for John 3:5. It does not prove that Ezekiel 37 is exclusively about Pentecost or settle the relation between John 20:22 and Acts 2.

**Questions that must remain distinct:**
1. **Extent/timing:** anticipatory sign; actual impartation; partial or temporary reception; complete gift; or a staged relation across more than one event.
2. **Function:** life/new-creation imagery; indwelling; revelation; mission empowerment; prophetic enablement; commissioned ministry; or more than one function.
3. **Narrative relation:** how John 7 and John 14–16, John 20:19–23, Luke 24:36–49, and Acts 1–2 fit together without one passage cancelling another.

**Reason:** Scholarly interpretation is not a simple two-position dispute between “symbolic” and “actual.” Adrian P. Rosen's 2025 survey identifies a wider range of proposals and questions but does not advocate one final interpretation. The lexical allusion, the imperative “receive,” the Spirit's function, and the relation to the commission each require separate assessment.

**Guardrail:** Do not erase or reduce John 20:22 to protect a preferred account of Pentecost; do not erase or collapse Acts 1–2 into John 20:22. Do not treat a scholarly proposal as an explicit biblical statement. Do not infer doctrine solely from the presence or absence of the Greek article. Do not invent a physical mechanism or declare the events identical in every respect without textual warrant.

**Related investigations:** [Investigation III.2 — The Breath of the Risen Christ](scriptural-reconstruction/INVESTIGATION-III-02-BREATH-OF-THE-RISEN-CHRIST.md); [Investigation III — Passion, Resurrection, Appearances, and Ascension Register](scriptural-reconstruction/INVESTIGATION-III-PASSION-RESURRECTION-REGISTER.md).

**Scholarly source:** Adrian P. Rosen, “Interpretive Questions Related to the Gift of the Spirit in John 20:22,” *Journal of the Evangelical Theological Society* 68, no. 2 (2025): 285–97. The article is used as a map of scholarly questions and proposals, not as an authority that settles the biblical interpretation.

**Revision history:** Expanded 2026-11-17 after the scholarly interpretive audit and comparison of Luke 24 / Acts 1 in Investigation III.2. The question remains open pending further canonical, historical, and textual-critical examination.

## 12. Evidence update — Genesis 4:6–7 (2026-11-16)

The following update supplements U-006. The U-007 notes are historical evidence records and are superseded by the resolution above.


**Further Johannine evidence (John 7:37–39; 12:23–33; 13:1, 31–32; 16:5–7; 17:1–5; 19:30–37):** John explicitly identifies the living-water promise with the Spirit believers were to receive and relates that reception to Jesus' glorification (7:39). The KJV's word “given” is supplied in italics; the shorter Greek wording is “the Spirit was not yet.” This does not mean the Spirit did not exist or act before Jesus' glorification; John has already described the Spirit's activity (1:32–33; 3:5–8). The Cross is central to John's glorification language, while Jesus' departure/return to the Father is also connected with the Comforter's coming (16:7). John 19:30 primarily records Jesus' death; whether it also carries a proleptic Spirit-gift resonance is debated and remains unproven as its primary meaning.

**Constraint on interpretation:** Any proposed account must explain the relation among the promised Spirit in John 7, the departure and Comforter sayings, the breathing and command in John 20:22, and the future endowment in Luke 24 / Acts 1–2. Do not claim that the Spirit did not exist before the Cross; do not make John 19:30 alone prove a Spirit impartation; do not collapse glorification into only one instant without accounting for John's narrative.


**Earlier Johannine Spirit testimony (John 1:32–34; 3:5–8, 34):** John records the Spirit descending and remaining upon Jesus and the Baptist's testimony identifying Jesus as the one who baptizes with the Holy Ghost. Jesus teaches that birth from water and Spirit is necessary for entrance into the kingdom, and that the Spirit's action is not controlled by the human observer. These passages establish that the Spirit's activity and teaching precede John 20:22; they do not by themselves settle the nature or timing of the disciples' later reception.

**Interpretive cautions:** John 1:33's wording identifies the Spirit-baptizer, but scholars debate whether the Gospel depicts this as direct bestowal or as the effect of Jesus' Spirit-endowed ministry. John 3:5's “water” has more than one proposed interpretation. The KJV's “unto him” in John 3:34 should be read with the Greek syntax and immediate context. None of these debates removes the explicit statements; they constrain claims about their precise mechanism or function.

### U-006 supplement — Human will, responsibility, desire, and mastery

**New evidence reviewed:** Genesis 4:6–7, in continuity with Genesis 4:1–8 and Genesis 3:16–17.

**Textual observations:**
- God addresses Cain's anger/distress and fallen countenance before the murder (4:6; compare 4:5 and 4:8).
- God presents the conditional contrast “if thou doest well” / “if thou doest not well” (4:7).
- The verse depicts a crouching figure/entity at the door, with desire toward Cain, and addresses Cain as the one to rule/master (4:7).
- The Hebrew imperfect תִּמְשָׁל (*timšāl*) does not, by morphology alone, settle whether the force is prediction, obligation, or potential capacity. Translation and context must be considered.
- The referent of the masculine suffixes in תְּשׁוּקָתוֹ (*təšûqātō*) and בּוֹ (*bō*) is complicated by the feminine grammatical gender of חַטָּאת (*ḥaṭṭāʾt*, “sin”) and the masculine participle רֹבֵץ (*rōḇēṣ*, “crouching”). The mismatch must remain visible; it does not by itself prove a separate demon/person.

**Effect on question:** Genesis 4:6–7 places human responsibility alongside anger/distress, an external or personified crouching threat, and desire. This weighs against collapsing responsibility into either passive exposure or mere choice. It does not provide a formal definition of will.

**Status remains:** UNRESOLVED.

### U-007 supplement — Knowledge, evaluation, and the governing standard

**New evidence reviewed:** Genesis 4:6–7, with Genesis 3:5–7, 3:17, and 3:22 as context.

**Textual observations:**
- God, not Cain, supplies the evaluative frame “if you do well / if you do not do well.”
- God addresses Cain's response and responsibility in relation to that frame.
- Genesis 4:6–7 does not repeat the expression “know good and evil” and therefore does not directly define the knowledge acknowledged in Genesis 3:22.
- The passage records no transfer of authority to Cain to establish or redefine the standard of good.

**Effect on question:** This passage supports keeping the capacity to evaluate distinct from authority to establish the standard. It does not settle the full meaning of Genesis 3:22.

**Historical status note:** Superseded by the U-007 resolution above; this is not an open status.

### Source and interpretation caution

The NET Bible notes offer a personified-animal interpretation of sin crouching and read the desire as an urge to dominate, while acknowledging alternatives for the force of the imperfect “rule.” A grammatical gender mismatch has also been discussed in translation scholarship. These are attributed interpretations, not unanimous findings. The repository reconstruction records the forms and competing possibilities rather than adopting one as settled.

## 13. Evidence update — Genesis 4:8 (2026-11-16)

This update supplements U-006. Any U-007 note in this historical update is superseded by the resolution above.

### U-006 supplement — Responsibility and action after warning

Genesis 4:8 records Cain speaking to Abel, their being in the field, Cain rising against Abel, and Cain killing him. This follows immediately after God's address and warning in Genesis 4:6–7.

The sequence supports distinguishing emotional condition, divine warning, the desire/opposition described in 4:7, and Cain's subsequent action. It establishes Cain as the grammatical and narrative subject of the hostile rising and killing.

It does not establish a complete definition of will or prove that Cain acted from a philosophically autonomous will. Nor should Genesis 4:8 be described as Cain ruling or failing to rule Abel: the referent of the masculine suffixes in 4:7 remains disputed and is not simply Abel.

**Status remains:** UNRESOLVED.

### U-007 supplement — Conduct and the standard

Genesis 4:8 records Cain's action after God has spoken of doing well and not doing well. It does not say Cain claimed authority to redefine good and evil, nor does it describe his internal reasoning in detail.

The Masoretic reading does not preserve the content of Cain's speech to Abel. Some ancient witnesses include “Let us go out to the field”; this is documented as a textual variant, not silently imported into the KJV quotation.

**Historical status note:** Superseded by the U-007 resolution above; this is not an open status.

## 14. Evidence update — Genesis 4:9–10 (2026-11-16)

This update supplements U-006. Any U-007 note in this historical update is superseded by the resolution above.

### U-006 supplement — Knowledge claims, acknowledgment, and responsibility

Genesis 4:9 records Cain's “I know not” (לֹא יָדַעְתִּי, *lōʾ yādaʿtî*) after the preceding narrative has explicitly identified Cain as Abel's killer. In context, this is a false denial, not a neutral instance of lacking information. The lexical root ידע (*yādaʿ*) itself means know/perceive; “lie” or “evade responsibility” is the contextual characterization of Cain's utterance, not the root's dictionary meaning.

Cain then asks whether he is his brother's keeper, using שֹׁמֵר (*šōmēr*) from שמר (*šāmar*). This is the same root used in Genesis 2:15 for Adam's charge to keep/guard the garden. The lexical connection is direct; whether it establishes a wider responsibility motif is a question for further contextual study. Genesis 4:9 does not explicitly assign Cain Adam's garden commission or a formal guardianship office over Abel.

Genesis 4:10 presents Abel's blood as crying out to God. Cain's denial does not alter the event or erase its evidence within the narrative.

**Effect on question:** Distinguish actual knowledge, a person's claim about what they know, truthful acknowledgment, and responsibility. This is relevant to a fuller account of will but does not define will.

**Status remains:** UNRESOLVED.

### U-007 supplement — Reality, knowledge, and assertion

Genesis 4:9–10 does not repeat “know good and evil.” It does depict a false statement about a known event. Cain's assertion does not change what happened; God's question and the blood's cry expose the contradiction.

This supports distinguishing reality from a person's statement about reality. It does not establish a formal theory of authority or resolve the precise meaning of Genesis 3:22.

**Historical status note:** Superseded by the U-007 resolution above; this is not an open status.

### Textual and lexical cautions

- The Masoretic Hebrew has דְּמֵי (*dəmê*), a plural form (“bloods”); the Septuagint and Vulgate render it with singular “blood.” The plural form is observable, while its precise interpretive force remains debated.
- The “voice” of the blood is explicit in the Hebrew (קוֹל, *qōl*). The NET's courtroom-witness analogy is commentary, not the lexical meaning of the words.
- The lexical link between Genesis 2:15 and 4:9 should be noted without claiming the two assignments are identical.

Related record: [Genesis 4:9–10 — Text-First Reconstruction](scriptural-reconstruction/GENESIS-4-9-10-TEXT-FIRST-RECONSTRUCTION.md).


## 15. Evidence update — Genesis 4:11–12 (2026-11-16)

This historical update supplements U-006 and contains an earlier U-007 note. **The U-007 authority question is resolved as stated above; this historical note is superseded and must not be read as reopening it.** U-006 remains unresolved.

### U-006 supplement — Action and consequence

Genesis 4:11–12 extends the sequence after Cain's killing of Abel:
- Abel's blood is received by the ground from Cain's hand (4:11).
- Cain's work/tilling of the ground will no longer be met by the ground giving him its strength (4:12).
- Cain is described as נָע וָנָד (*nāʿ wā nāḏ*), a paired expression for a wandering/fugitive condition (4:12).

This adds a distinction between the human act and consequences extending into the actor's future relation to work, ground, and place. It does not define will or establish a scientific causal mechanism.

**Status remains:** UNRESOLVED.

### U-007 supplement — Assertion does not erase the event

Genesis 4:11–12 follows Cain's denial in 4:9. His denial does not undo the killing, the blood received by the ground, or the judgment announced. The passage is consistent with distinguishing a person's assertion from the reality and judgment described in the narrative. It does not repeat “know good and evil” or settle the meaning of Genesis 3:22.

**Historical status note:** Superseded by the U-007 resolution above; this is not an open status.

### Textual and translation caution

The phrase מִן־הָאֲדָמָה (*min-hāʾădāmāh*) in 4:11 has competing readings: source/agency, separation/banishment, and a comparative reading (“more than the ground”) argued in published scholarship. The repository reconstruction records these alternatives rather than treating one as settled.

The ground's opening its mouth to receive Abel's blood is personification in the narrative. No scientific mechanism or independent moral agency of soil is inferred.

## 16. Evidence update — Genesis 4:13–16 (2026-11-16)

This update supplements U-006. Any U-007 note in this historical update is superseded by the resolution above.

### U-006 supplement — Assessment, anticipation, protection, and action

Genesis 4:13–16 records Cain's assessment of the announced judgment, his anticipation of being killed, God's protective response, and Cain's departure to Nod. These are distinct stages and should not be collapsed into a single category called “will.”

The Hebrew עָוֹן (*ʿāwōn*) in 4:13 can mean iniquity, guilt, or punishment. The immediate discourse favors “punishment” in the KJV/NET line of translation, while ancient versions preserve a guilt/forgiveness reading. The verse does not explicitly state repentance or confession.

Cain's fear is expressed as an expectation that anyone finding him will kill him. God responds with a sevenfold vengeance declaration and appoints an אוֹת (*ʾōṯ*, sign) for Cain. The text does not specify the sign's physical form.

**Effect on question:** Distinguish a person's assessment of consequences, anticipated danger, protective intervention, and subsequent action. This is relevant to the components of will but does not define will.

**Status remains:** UNRESOLVED.

### U-007 supplement — Judgment and protection remain distinct

God's protection of Cain does not cancel the previously announced ground-related judgment in the text. The sign is appointed by the LORD; Cain does not establish it or define its function. The narrative distinguishes divine judgment from divine protection within the same episode.

**Historical status note:** Superseded by the U-007 resolution above; this is not an open status.

### Textual and translation cautions

- עָוֹן (*ʿāwōn*) is semantically broad; the KJV/NET “punishment” is contextual, while the LXX and Vulgate support an interpretation involving guilt/forgiveness.
- אוֹת (*ʾōṯ*) means sign/token; its specific physical form is not disclosed.
- The Hebrew construction is לְקַיִן (*lə-Qayin*, “for Cain”); English “upon Cain” is idiomatic. Do not infer exact bodily placement from the English.
- The MT has לָכֵן (*lāḵēn*, “therefore/in that case”). Some ancient versions differ in the opening of God's reply; the MT/KJV reading is retained as the base text.
- נוֹד (*Nōḏ*) is a place-name associated by wordplay with wandering; its exact geography is not supplied.
