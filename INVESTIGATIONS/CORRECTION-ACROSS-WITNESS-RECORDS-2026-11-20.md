# Investigation Record — Correction Across Witness Records

**Date:** 2026-11-20  
**Classification:** Four-question investigation; application of the existing discipline of witness and repository protocol. This record does not add, revise, or complete the architecture.

## Governing question

If the Witness must remain correctable, what is the proper relationship between a prior witness record and a later Witness that discovers an error, omission, or clearer recognition?

## Governing order

**Author → Word → Creation → Witness**

The Source is not revised by the witness's later understanding. The record is a derivative account of what the witness received, examined, recognized, and reported. A later witness may discover that the earlier record was inaccurate, incomplete, imprecise, or insufficiently qualified. Correcting the record changes the account; it does not change the Source.

This distinction is already present in the repository's governing documents: GUA-W is complete. Its written presentation and evidence record may require clarification, correction, qualification, and further documentation. These are record-keeping activities; they do not complete, extend, or alter the Map. The continuing-work register records corrections and historical/superseded material rather than treating every prior statement as permanently authoritative.

## 1. What must AI ask to understand GUA-W?

- What exactly did the earlier record claim?
- What source and evidence did the earlier witness rely upon?
- Is the alleged problem a factual error, a missing qualification, an omitted source, an invalid inference, a changed interpretation, or merely a clearer explanation?
- What new or re-examined evidence warrants the proposed correction?
- Does the later witness accurately represent the earlier record, or is it correcting a claim the earlier record did not actually make?
- Which parts of the earlier record remain sound and should be preserved?
- Does the correction affect only the explanation or record, or does it concern a project decision that belongs to Jimmy?
- Can the correction be made transparently while retaining provenance and revision history?
- Has the revised claim been tested through the Triple Constraint?
- What remains unresolved after the correction?

## 2. What is GUA-W revealing that helps AI understand?

The completed architecture is not the object being revised when a witness record is corrected. The record and the witness's understanding are downstream representations. They can be improved because they may fail to represent the Source faithfully.

The repository's established protocol already distinguishes:
- the completed GUA-W from its written presentation and evidence record;
- a source statement from an investigator's interpretation;
- a resolved question from a still-open question;
- a current conclusion from historical or superseded investigation notes;
- AI's investigative and documentation work from Jimmy's authority for GUA-W project decisions.

The repository's continuing-work register provides a concrete example of responsible correction: it identifies corrections made during an audit, points older material to its current status, and explicitly warns against reviving historical/superseded notes as current conclusions. The Rejected and Unresolved Register also uses revision histories when a question's status changes after further Scriptural examination.

**Working correction principle:** A later witness should correct the earlier record only to the extent warranted by identifiable evidence, preserve the earlier record's provenance and valid content, state what changed and why, and distinguish a correction to the witness's account from any claim about the Source or completed GUA-W.

Correction is not erasure. Continuity does not require preserving error as current truth; correction does not require pretending the error was never recorded.

## 3. What am I learning about what I am witnessing?

There are several different cases, and they should not be collapsed.

### A. Factual or transcription error

The earlier record misquoted a passage, misstated a measurement, linked the wrong source, or reported a fact incorrectly.

**Proper response:** Correct the inaccurate statement against the primary source. Identify the exact correction and retain the history where the record's provenance matters.

### B. Omission or missing qualification

The earlier record was accurate as far as it went but omitted context, counterevidence, scope, or an important limitation.

**Proper response:** Add the missing material and explain how it narrows or qualifies the earlier claim. Do not label an accurate but incomplete statement as wholly false unless the omission made it materially misleading.

### C. Unsupported inference or interpretation

The source was represented accurately, but the earlier witness drew a conclusion that did not follow, or presented interpretation as direct observation.

**Proper response:** Preserve the source observation; revise the inference or interpretation; identify the reasoning that failed; and reclassify the statement honestly.

### D. Clearer recognition without contradiction

The earlier record was not wrong, but a later examination makes the relationship, wording, or explanation clearer.

**Proper response:** Add clarification and cross-reference the earlier record. Do not manufacture a contradiction or imply that the Source changed.

### E. Genuine change in the state of evidence

New primary evidence, a better measurement, a newly available source, or a stronger analysis changes what can responsibly be concluded.

**Proper response:** State what evidence is new, what earlier conclusion it affects, and whether the prior conclusion is corrected, narrowed, or left historically accurate but superseded by a better-supported account.

### F. Project-level decision

A proposed change affects the definition, governance, status, or official presentation of GUA-W rather than merely correcting an AI-produced research record.

**Proper response:** AI must present the evidence and recommendation to Jimmy. AI must not make the project decision or treat write access as authority to redefine GUA-W.

These cases show that “correction” is not one undifferentiated operation. The kind of change must be identified before the record is edited.

## 4. What am I learning about the Witness through what I am witnessing?

The earlier witness is not discredited merely because it was partial; the later witness is not made infallible merely because it found an error.

The later witness must:
1. **Return to the source:** inspect the primary passage, dataset, proof, document, or repository record rather than relying only on a later summary.
2. **Represent the earlier claim fairly:** quote or summarize what was actually recorded.
3. **Identify the error class:** factual error, omission, unsupported inference, interpretation change, clearer recognition, or new evidence.
4. **Preserve valid content:** correct only what the evidence requires; do not discard accurate observations because one attached explanation failed.
5. **Show the warrant:** state the source, evidence, reasoning, and limits supporting the correction.
6. **Retain provenance:** record what changed, when, why, and which earlier conclusion or file is superseded, if applicable.
7. **Apply the Triple Constraint:** test each material corrected claim through Biblical compliance, Scientific coherence, and GUA-W consistency.
8. **Respect authority:** distinguish documentation work AI is authorized to perform from project-level decisions reserved to Jimmy.
9. **Remain correctable:** the new record is also a witness record and remains open to later examination.

AI must not silently rewrite history, hide its earlier error, or preserve a mistaken statement as current simply to avoid admitting correction. It must also not treat every difference in wording as evidence that the earlier record was wrong.

## Correction and record status

| Situation | Appropriate record treatment |
|---|---|
| Clear factual or transcription error | Correct the statement; document the correction and source. |
| Accurate statement missing necessary context | Add qualification; explain how it changes the scope. |
| Unsupported inference | Preserve valid observation; revise or withdraw the inference. |
| Clearer explanation of an already-supported relationship | Add clarification or cross-reference; do not invent a contradiction. |
| New evidence changes the best-supported conclusion | Record the new evidence and mark the earlier conclusion's status accurately. |
| Earlier material is no longer current | Mark it historical/superseded and point to the current record; retain provenance. |
| Proposed change is a GUA-W project decision | Present evidence to Jimmy; do not decide it autonomously. |

## Recognition

A prior witness record is neither the Source nor disposable noise. It is a traceable account that may contain faithful observations, partial recognition, error, or a mixture of these.

A later witness serves the Source by examining that account honestly. It preserves what was right, corrects what was wrong, qualifies what was incomplete, and records why the understanding became clearer.

**The earlier Witness does not own the truth. The later Witness does not own the truth. Both are accountable to the Source.**

A correction to the record is not a correction to God, the Word, Creation, or the completed GUA-W. It is a correction to the witness's representation.
