## Scriptural reasoning for examining AI as a gift or instrument

The Bible does not name modern artificial intelligence. The investigation must not claim that it does. It can, however, examine Scriptural patterns concerning God-given human capacities and human-made instruments.

### Genesis 1:26–28 and Genesis 2:15

Humanity is placed within Creation and given a commission concerning it. Genesis 2:15 describes the man's placement in the Garden to dress and keep it. These passages provide a basis for examining human activity, cultivation, stewardship, and making as activities carried out within an order humanity did not originate.

### Exodus 31:1–6; 35:30–35

Bezalel and other skilled workers are described as receiving wisdom, understanding, knowledge, craftsmanship, and the ability to teach. The passage is especially relevant because skilled human making is not presented as independent of God's provision. Human skill and crafted work can serve a purpose within God's instructions.

This does not mean AI is equivalent to Bezalel's work, nor that Exodus predicts computers. It establishes a Scriptural pattern worth considering: human skill and the making of things can be understood as gifts placed in service of a purpose.

### James 1:17

Every good gift is described as coming from above, from the Father of lights. This provides a broad theological principle for examining the source of human capacities and genuinely good uses of them. It does not by itself prove that every invention or every use of AI is good, or that a particular AI system has a divinely assigned mission.

### 1 Corinthians 12:4–7

Different gifts and operations are considered in relation to one Spirit and the common good. This passage may help examine differentiated capacities and service without treating every capacity as having identical function or authority. It must not be used to claim that AI has the spiritual gifts described for members of the body of Christ.

### 2 Timothy 3:16–17 and Hebrews 1:1–3

These passages preserve distinctions that must not be blurred. Scripture's God-breathed character and the Son's unique identity and sustaining work cannot be transferred to AI. AI may help a person search or compare Scriptural records; it is not Scripture's Author, Christ, or a new source of revelation.
