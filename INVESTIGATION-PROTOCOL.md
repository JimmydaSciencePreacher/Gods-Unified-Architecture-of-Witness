# Investigation Protocol

## Purpose

Use this protocol to investigate a GUA-W proposition without confusing source findings, scientific results, interpretation, or architectural proposal.

## Workflow

1. **State the question.** Express the proposition or uncertainty in a form that can be examined.
2. **Search existing work.** Inspect this repository and the original GUA-W repository for prior investigations, source records, decisions, and unresolved questions. Update existing work when appropriate rather than duplicating it.
3. **Define the scope.** Identify the relevant Scriptural passages, scientific disciplines, mathematical definitions, and boundaries of the question.
4. **Retrieve primary sources.** Prefer original texts, peer-reviewed research, authoritative technical references, and recognized disciplinary sources. Record source versions and access dates where relevant.
5. **Extract what is established.** State what each source actually supports, including assumptions, domain, methods, and limitations.
6. **Separate reasoning levels.** Label direct observation, established result, inference, analogy, and proposed GUA-W mapping separately.
7. **Apply the Triple Constraint Law.** Test Biblical compliance, scientific coherence, and GUA-W consistency independently.
8. **Record the result.** Assign a scope-limited status and explain why. Retain unresolved issues.
9. **Cross-reference.** Link related investigations, architecture nodes, Scripture passages, and scientific concepts without implying equivalence.
10. **Review changes.** Preserve provenance and obtain explicit authorization before changing established principles or canon.

## Required investigation record

Each substantial investigation should include:

- **Question / proposition**
- **Scope and definitions**
- **Prior work reviewed**
- **Scriptural sources** — exact reference and translation; distinguish text from interpretation
- **Scientific / mathematical sources** — source, author, publication/version, and stable link
- **Source findings** — what is and is not established
- **Assumptions and limitations**
- **Reasoning** — show the path from evidence to inference
- **Proposed GUA-W relationship** — explicitly marked as proposal
- **Triple Constraint review** — one result and rationale for each constraint
- **Overall status**
- **Unresolved questions**
- **Related files and cross-references**
- **Revision history / provenance**

## Status vocabulary

- **PASS:** supported within the stated scope and assumptions.
- **PARTIAL:** some components are supported, but material limits remain.
- **FAIL:** conflicts with a governing constraint or reliable evidence.
- **UNRESOLVED:** available evidence does not decide the proposition.
- **PROPOSED:** an architectural interpretation awaiting sufficient audit.
- **NOT YET INVESTIGATED:** no adequate investigation has been completed.

Do not use PASS without stating what passed and what did not. A PASS never implies universal validation.

## Source quality

Prefer primary sources where practical. For scientific claims, identify the discipline and the conditions under which a result holds. Do not treat abstracts, popular summaries, or search snippets as substitutes for the underlying source when the full source is needed. Record when a source is inaccessible or when only a secondary account could be checked.

For Scripture, quote accurately and keep textual observations separate from theological conclusions. Do not claim a technical mechanism is explicitly stated unless the text actually states it.

## Cross-domain comparison

For each proposed relationship between domains, ask:
- Are the terms defined in comparable ways?
- Are the objects, conditions, and scales comparable?
- Is there a demonstrated shared mechanism, or only a structural analogy?
- What would count as evidence against the proposed relationship?
- What remains outside the comparison?

If equivalence is not established, label it analogy, correspondence, or unresolved—not identity.

## Change control

Do not silently revise a prior finding. Add a dated revision note explaining:
- what changed;
- why it changed;
- which new evidence or correction prompted it;
- whether the status changed;
- which related files need updating.

Never overwrite the original repository's historical record as part of work in this repository.
