# Investigation II.27 — The Man with Dropsy
**Primary text:** Luke 14:1–6 (KJV)  
**Related texts:** Luke 13:10–17; Luke 6:6–11; Luke 7:1–10; Luke 11:37–54  
**Status:** Text-first case study recorded; the condition and physical mechanism are not specified beyond the period term.

## Question
What does Luke report when Jesus heals a man with dropsy in the house of a chief Pharisee on the sabbath, and how does Jesus expose the observers' reasoning?

## Narrative sequence
1. Jesus enters the house of one of the chief Pharisees on the sabbath to eat bread; those present watch Him.
2. A man with dropsy is before Him.
3. Jesus asks the lawyers and Pharisees whether it is lawful to heal on the sabbath; they hold their peace.
4. Jesus takes the man, heals him, and lets him go.
5. He asks which of them would not immediately pull out an ass or an ox fallen into a pit on the sabbath day.
6. They cannot answer Him again.

## What the text states
The man has dropsy; Jesus asks a question, takes and heals him, and lets him go. The observers are silent before the healing and unable to answer His follow-up argument.

## Witness and agency
Jesus notices the man, questions the observers, heals, and releases him. The man receives healing. The assembled lawyers and Pharisees watch but remain silent. Jesus' argument exposes a discrepancy between their treatment of human need and their own sabbath practice.

## Historical and lexical caution
The KJV term “dropsy” translates the Greek medical term *hydropikos*, describing a person suffering from dropsy/abnormal fluid accumulation. That historical label is not sufficient to assign a modern diagnosis or determine its cause. The passage itself does not give symptoms beyond the term.

## What the text does not state
Luke does not specify duration, severity, underlying disease, measurements, or mechanism of healing. Do not equate the ancient diagnostic label automatically with one modern condition.

## Triple Constraint
- **Biblical compliance — PASS:** setting, condition, question, healing, release, and final silence are explicit.
- **Scientific coherence — PASS for historical/clinical restraint; modern diagnosis/mechanism UNRESOLVED:** the ancient term is not a complete modern case record.
- **GUA-W consistency — PASS at governing level; technical mapping UNRESOLVED:** Jesus acts; the man receives; observers witness and are challenged. No new architecture is proposed.

## Finding
Luke's account is brief but pointed: Jesus heals the man and then tests the observers' own sabbath reasoning. The silence of the watchers contrasts with the clarity of the action and argument.

**The man is healed and released. The watchers cannot answer.**
