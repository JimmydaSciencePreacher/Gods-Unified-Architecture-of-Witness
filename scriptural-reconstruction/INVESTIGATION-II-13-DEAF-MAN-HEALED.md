# Investigation II.13 — The Deaf Man with a Speech Impediment
**Primary text:** Mark 7:31–37 (KJV)  
**Related texts:** Isaiah 35:4–6; Mark 8:22–26; Matthew 11:2–6; John 9:1–7  
**Status:** Textual observations recorded; medical diagnosis and mechanism not supplied.

## Question
What does Mark record about Jesus' restoration of a man who is deaf and has an impediment in his speech, including the distinctive actions and the command “Ephphatha”?

## Narrative sequence
- Jesus comes through the region of Decapolis.
- People bring to Him a man who is deaf and has an impediment in his speech, and beseech Him to put His hand upon him.
- Jesus takes the man aside from the multitude.
- He puts His fingers into the man's ears, spits, touches his tongue, looks up to heaven, sighs, and says, “Ephphatha,” meaning, “Be opened.”
- Immediately the man's ears are opened, the string of his tongue is loosed, and he speaks plainly.
- Jesus charges those present that they should tell no man; the more He charges them, the more widely they publish it.
- The people are astonished beyond measure and say, “He hath done all things well: he maketh both the deaf to hear, and the dumb to speak.”

## What the text states
Mark gives unusually specific bodily actions and preserves the Aramaic expression with its translation. The restoration is immediate in the narrative. The crowd's response echoes the prophetic expectation that the deaf hear and the speechless speak.

## Witness and agency
Others bring the man and request help. Jesus takes him aside and performs the actions described, then speaks the command. The man receives the restoration; the crowd witnesses and reports. The crowd's actions do not originate the healing.

## Canonical context
Isaiah 35:5–6 speaks of the ears of the deaf being unstopped and the tongue of the dumb singing. The crowd's concluding words and the event's features are consistent with this prophetic hope. Matthew 11:2–6 records Jesus directing John's messengers to report that the blind see, the lame walk, the lepers are cleansed, the deaf hear, and the poor have the gospel preached to them. This is a significant canonical connection, though Mark 7 does not explicitly quote Isaiah.

## What the text does not state
Mark gives no audiological testing, anatomical diagnosis, or explanation of how hearing and speech are restored. The actions described must not be turned into a universal healing technique; Jesus' authority is not reducible to a ritual sequence.

## Triple Constraint
- **Biblical compliance — PASS:** the actions, command, immediate result, and public response are explicit.
- **Scientific coherence — PASS for restraint; medical mechanism UNRESOLVED:** no modern clinical measurements or mechanism are provided.
- **GUA-W consistency — PASS for governing relationship; technical mapping UNRESOLVED:** Jesus acts; the brought man and observing crowd are recipients and witnesses. No new architecture is proposed.

## Finding
Unlike the centurion's servant or the Syrophoenician woman's daughter, Mark records physical actions accompanying Jesus' command. The text does not establish that these actions are necessary mechanisms; it records what Jesus did in this instance. The result is connected to prophetic expectation and public testimony.

**Jesus says, “Be opened.” The man hears and speaks plainly. The people witness.**
