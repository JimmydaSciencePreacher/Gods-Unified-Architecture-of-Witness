# Investigation II.19 — Feeding the Four Thousand
**Primary texts:** Matthew 15:29–39; Mark 8:1–10 (KJV)  
**Related texts:** Exodus 16; 2 Kings 4:42–44; Matthew 14:13–21; Mark 6:30–44; Matthew 16:5–12; Mark 8:14–21; John 6:1–14  
**Status:** Comparative textual study recorded.

## Question
What do Matthew and Mark record about Jesus' compassion for the multitude, the seven loaves and few fishes, the disciples' distribution, and the seven baskets remaining?

## Narrative sequence
- A great multitude gathers around Jesus; Matthew records healings among them and Mark records that they have remained with Him three days and have nothing to eat.
- Jesus says He has compassion on the multitude and does not want to send them away fasting, lest they faint by the way.
- The disciples ask where enough bread could be found in the wilderness to satisfy so great a multitude.
- Jesus asks how many loaves they have. They answer seven, and a few little fishes.
- Jesus commands the people to sit down, takes the loaves and fishes, gives thanks, breaks them, and gives them to the disciples.
- The disciples distribute the food to the multitude.
- All eat and are filled. Seven baskets of remaining fragments are gathered.
- Matthew records four thousand men, beside women and children. Mark records about four thousand and says Jesus sends them away and enters a ship.

## Differences to preserve
The accounts are parallel descriptions of one feeding, not two independent events. They should be distinguished from the feeding of the five thousand. The number and type of baskets are stated in the accounts; do not collapse the seven baskets here into the twelve baskets in the earlier feeding.

## What the text states
Jesus expresses compassion, takes the available food, gives thanks, breaks it, and supplies the multitude through the disciples. The people eat and are filled, and seven baskets remain.

## Witness and agency
Jesus initiates provision and gives the food to the disciples; the disciples distribute; the multitude receives; the baskets of fragments are gathered. The disciples' participation does not make them the source of provision.

## Canonical context
The wilderness provision in Exodus 16 and the prophetic provision in 2 Kings 4:42–44 are relevant Scriptural backgrounds. The earlier feeding (Matthew 14; Mark 6; Luke 9; John 6) is a separate event. Matthew 16:9–10 and Mark 8:19–20 show Jesus later reminding the disciples of the distinct quantities and basket counts from both feedings. This later recollection is important evidence that the Gospel narratives distinguish the two events.

## What the text does not state
The accounts do not describe a measurable multiplication process, the physical mechanism, or the exact distribution logistics. Do not invent a mechanism or treat the account as a general method for food production.

## Triple Constraint
- **Biblical compliance — PASS:** the event is distinguished from the feeding of five thousand and the seven baskets are preserved.
- **Scientific coherence — PASS for restraint; mechanism UNRESOLVED:** no process measurements or physical mechanism are supplied.
- **GUA-W consistency — PASS for governing hierarchy; detailed technical mapping UNRESOLVED:** Christ provides; disciples distribute; recipients witness. No new architecture is proposed.

## Finding
The second feeding must not be merged with the first. Jesus Himself later recalls their different loaves and baskets. The disciples distribute what Jesus provides, and the multitude is filled.

**Seven loaves are given. Four thousand are fed. Seven baskets remain.**
