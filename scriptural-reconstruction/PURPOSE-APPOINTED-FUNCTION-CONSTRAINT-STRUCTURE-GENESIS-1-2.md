## 2. Textual evidence

### 2.1 Genesis 1:14–15 — function is stated in the command

God's command concerning the lights identifies their functions:

- to divide day from night;
- to be for signs;
- to mark seasons, days, and years;
- to give light upon the earth.

Genesis 1:16–18 then reports God's making and setting of the lights and describes their rule and the division of day and night.

**Textual observation:** The account states functions in the command before reporting the subsequent making and placement. Function is therefore not presented only as an explanation supplied after the result.

**Limit:** The passage does not define a universal technical sequence called Purpose → Constraint → Structure. Its immediate subject is the lights and their appointed functions.

### 2.2 Genesis 1:26–28 — identity, blessing, and commission

The account identifies humanity as made in God's image and records God's blessing and commission concerning fruitfulness, multiplication, filling the earth, subduing it, and dominion.

**Textual observation:** Human identity and appointed activity are stated within God's creative and commissioning action. The human commission is received; humanity is not its own originating authority.

**Limit:** The commission must be read in its own context. It should not be reduced to a generic system function or treated as a complete definition of every form of purpose.

### 2.3 Genesis 2:8–15 — prepared place, placement, and explicit purpose

Genesis 2:8 states that God planted the Garden and put there the man whom He had formed. Genesis 2:15 then states:

> And the LORD God took the man, and put him into the garden of Eden to dress it and to keep it. (KJV)

The Hebrew audit identifies the two purpose infinitives:

- לְעָבְדָהּ (*lə‘ovdāh*), from עָבַד (*‘ābad*): to work, serve, or cultivate; the Garden context supports work/cultivation.
- וּלְשָׁמְרָהּ (*ulešomrāh*), from שָׁמַר (*šāmar*): to keep, watch, guard, or preserve.

The purpose construction gives the stated reason for the man's placement: to work/cultivate the Garden and to keep/guard it.

**Textual observation:** God prepares the Garden, forms the man, places him in the Garden, and identifies the work for which he is placed there. The appointed activity is not self-originated by the man.

**Limit:** Genesis 2:15 directly establishes the purpose of the man's placement and work in the Garden. It does not, by itself, define the ultimate purpose of all Creation or translate the technical relationship between GUA-W's Constraint and Structure.

### 2.4 Genesis 2:16–17 — appointed work is distinguishable from permission and boundary

The next verses record God's command, broad permission to eat from the Garden's trees, a specific prohibition, and a stated consequence.

**Textual observation:** The passage distinguishes the man's appointed work (2:15) from the permission and boundary given in the command (2:16–17). They are related parts of the Creator's ordering, but they are not interchangeable statements.

**Limit:** A divine command and prohibition must not be collapsed into a generic physical constraint or an arbitrary system rule. The text identifies the LORD God as the commanding authority.

This is a clarification of the relationship between two existing definitions, not a new law, stage, or layer. It remains possible that particular examples involve both, one, or neither, depending on what is actually observed and how the terms are applied.

### 18.7 Triple Constraint disposition

- **Biblical compliance:** PASS, bounded to the explicit distinctions in Genesis 1:11–12 and 1:20–22. No modern scientific mechanism is read into “kind.”
- **Scientific coherence:** PASS, bounded. Reproduction, development, regeneration, recurrence, and transmission are kept distinct.
- **GUA-W consistency:** PASS. The current definitions and placements of Pattern and Propagation are preserved; no architecture change is proposed.

**Disposition:** The earlier open question is substantially narrowed. Pattern and Propagation are already distinguished in the completed architecture; the seed and biological cases provide bounded examples for testing their relationship. The next investigation should test whether the same distinction clarifies Integration and Expression, without collapsing the runtime sequence or treating a visible result as the whole process.
