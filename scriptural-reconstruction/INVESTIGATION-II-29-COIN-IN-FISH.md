# Investigation II.29 — The Tribute Money in the Fish's Mouth
**Primary text:** Matthew 17:24–27 (KJV)  
**Related texts:** Exodus 30:11–16; Matthew 22:15–22; Romans 13:6–7  
**Status:** Text-first case study recorded; mechanism and historical coin identification not settled.

## Question
What does Matthew report when Jesus instructs Peter to catch a fish and find a piece of money in its mouth to pay the tribute for both of them?

## Narrative sequence
1. Those who receive tribute money come to Peter and ask whether his master pays tribute.
2. Peter answers yes.
3. Jesus anticipates Peter's arrival and asks whether earthly kings take custom or tribute from their own children or from strangers.
4. Peter answers, “Of strangers.” Jesus concludes that the children are free.
5. Jesus nevertheless says, “Notwithstanding, lest we should offend them,” and instructs Peter to go to the sea, cast a hook, and take the first fish that comes up.
6. Jesus says that when Peter opens its mouth he will find a piece of money; Peter is to take it and give it to the collectors “for me and thee.”

## What the text states
Jesus predicts the specific sequence: Peter will catch the first fish, find money in its mouth, and use it to pay the tribute for Jesus and Peter. Matthew records the instruction but does not narrate Peter's return or explicitly describe the moment of finding and payment after verse 27.

## Witness and agency
Jesus teaches Peter about sonship and freedom, then gives the instruction. Peter is to go, fish, retrieve the money, and pay. The collectors' question occasions the teaching. The narrative emphasizes Jesus' knowledge and the provision while avoiding an unnecessary offence.

## Historical and textual cautions
The Greek term *stater* is commonly understood as a stater coin, whose value could cover the two-drachma temple tax for two persons. The exact coin type is not identified by Matthew; do not claim a specific mint or denomination beyond what the text and relevant numismatic evidence can establish. The tribute in context is generally understood as the half-shekel temple tax, but this identification should be distinguished from the wording of the passage itself.

## What the text does not state
Matthew does not explain how the coin came to be in the fish's mouth, how long it had been there, or the biological/ecological process. The text gives no independently observed coin specimen or modern verification. Do not invent one.

## Triple Constraint
- **Biblical compliance — PASS:** dialogue, distinction of children and strangers, Jesus' instruction, first fish, coin, and two-person payment are explicit.
- **Scientific/historical coherence — PARTIAL:** historical identification of the tax and coin can be examined; the fish/coin mechanism is not described and remains unresolved.
- **GUA-W consistency — PASS at governing level; technical mapping UNRESOLVED:** Jesus speaks with knowledge and authority; Peter carries out the instruction. No new architecture is proposed.

## Finding
The episode is not simply a fishing miracle. Jesus first teaches Peter about the relation of sonship and tribute, then chooses to pay rather than cause offence. The provision is subordinate to that teaching and to Jesus' stated purpose.

**The Son is free; nevertheless, He provides the tribute and avoids needless offence.**
