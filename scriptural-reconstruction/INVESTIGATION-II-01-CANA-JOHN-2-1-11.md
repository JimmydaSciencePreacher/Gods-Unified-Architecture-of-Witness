# Investigation II.1 — Cana: The Word Acts and the Sign Is Witnessed
**Primary text:** John 2:1–11 (KJV)  
**Related texts:** Genesis 1:1–3; Genesis 2:1–5; John 1:1–14; John 5:36; John 10:25, 37–38; John 20:30–31; Hebrews 1:1–3; Colossians 1:15–17  
**Status:** TEXTUAL OBSERVATIONS RECORDED; theological and GUA-W relationship assessed provisionally; scientific mechanism not described by the text.

## 1. Investigative question

What does John 2:1–11 actually show when the Word made flesh acts? Does the passage present Jesus as one who operates from the authority of the Creator, with the resulting event bearing witness to His identity and glory?

The phrase **“Unified State”** is used here as an investigative description of the completed Creation established by the Author—not as a new GUA-W layer, runtime stage, or mechanism. The repository's completed architecture does not use that exact phrase. The phrase must therefore remain explanatory language, not a newly asserted technical definition.

## 2. Read the passage in sequence

### John 2:1–2 — Setting and presence
The event takes place at a marriage in Cana of Galilee. Jesus' mother is present; Jesus and His disciples are also invited.

**TEXT STATES:** A real social occasion, with named participants and a defined location. The sign occurs within ordinary human life, not in an abstract demonstration setting.

### John 2:3 — A lack is disclosed
The wine fails: “They have no wine.”

**TEXT STATES:** A need is reported. John does not say that the shortage is caused by sin, a curse, or a failure in the original Creation. Do not import those explanations.

### John 2:4 — Jesus' response and “mine hour”
Jesus addresses His mother as “Woman” and says, “what have I to do with thee? mine hour is not yet come.”

**TEXT STATES:** Jesus distinguishes the present request or circumstance from “mine hour.”  
**UNRESOLVED:** The full Johannine meaning of “mine hour” must be read across the Gospel, especially John 7:30; 8:20; 12:23, 27; 13:1; and 17:1. It must not be reduced to a casual reference to timing or assumed to mean only the cross in this verse without examining that wider pattern.

### John 2:5 — The servants are directed to obey Jesus
His mother says to the servants, “Whatsoever he saith unto you, do it.”

**TEXT STATES:** The servants are directed toward Jesus' instruction.  
**LIMIT:** This does not establish that Mary commands the sign, controls Jesus, or supplies its authority. The action that follows is initiated by Jesus' words.

### John 2:6–7 — Vessels, water, command, completion of filling
Six waterpots of stone are present, described as being “after the manner of the purifying of the Jews”; their capacity is given. Jesus says, “Fill the waterpots with water.” The servants fill them “up to the brim.”

**TEXT STATES:** The vessels already exist; water is placed in them in response to Jesus' command; the servants carry out the instruction fully.  
**TEXTUAL OBSERVATION:** The narrative distinguishes Jesus' command from the servants' obedient execution. The servants do not produce the sign by their own authority.  
**LIMIT:** John does not describe the water's source, chemical composition, or any intermediate physical process.

### John 2:8 — A second command and human conveyance
Jesus tells them, “Draw out now, and bear unto the governor of the feast.” They do so.

**TEXT STATES:** Jesus directs the next action. The servants convey the contents to the governor.  
**TEXTUAL OBSERVATION:** The servants are participants and witnesses to the sequence, but the passage attributes the transformation to Jesus (see verse 9).

### John 2:9–10 — The result is tasted and evaluated
The governor of the feast tastes the water that was made wine. He does not know where it came from; the servants who drew the water know. He comments that the good wine has been kept until now.

**TEXT STATES:** The governor tastes wine; the narrator identifies it as water made wine. The governor does not know its source; the servants do.  
**WITNESS DISTINCTION:** Not all witnesses possess the same information. The servants know something the governor does not. The governor can evaluate the quality of what he tastes without knowing its origin.  
**LIMIT:** The governor's statement about the usual order of serving wine is his observation within the feast; it is not a scientific explanation of the transformation.

### John 2:11 — John interprets the event's significance
John calls this “the beginning of miracles” (Greek: signs) Jesus did in Cana of Galilee; Jesus “manifested forth his glory”; and His disciples believed on Him.

**TEXT STATES:** John explicitly identifies the event as a sign, connects it with the manifestation of Jesus' glory, and records the disciples' belief.  
**CENTRAL OBSERVATION:** The narrative's stated endpoint is not merely that a shortage was remedied. The sign manifests Jesus' glory and bears upon the disciples' belief in Him.

## 3. What the text attributes—and what it does not

The narrative's attribution is clear:

- Jesus speaks the operative commands.
- The servants obey and carry out the instructed actions.
- The water is made wine.
- The servants know the source of the wine's origin; the governor tastes and evaluates the result without knowing that origin.
- John identifies the event as a sign through which Jesus manifests His glory.
- The disciples believe on Him.

The passage does **not** state:
- that Mary caused or authorized the miracle;
- that the servants transformed the water;
- that Jesus used a natural process, a hidden technology, or a physical mechanism described by John;
- that the event is a repair of a defect in Creation;
- that the wine's transformation is a repeatable laboratory procedure;
- that every miracle must follow the same sequence of commands and human participation.

The correct record is neither to explain away the event nor to invent a mechanism the text does not give.

## 4. Canonical relationship: the Word and Creation

John's prologue supplies the crucial identity context. John 1:1–3 declares that the Word was in the beginning, was with God, was God, and that all things were made by Him. John 1:14 identifies the Word as made flesh. Hebrews 1:1–3 describes the Son as the One by whom the worlds were made and who upholds all things by the word of His power. Colossians 1:15–17 likewise identifies all things as created by Him and states that by Him all things consist.

**SUPPORTED CANONICAL RELATIONSHIP:** The One acting at Cana is not presented in John's Gospel as an ordinary created agent attempting to gain control over Creation. The Gospel has already identified Jesus with the incarnate Word through whom all things were made. The sign must be read in that identity context.

**IMPORTANT DISTINCTION:** John 2:1–11 itself does not repeat the whole doctrine of John 1 or Hebrews 1. The relationship is established by reading the Gospel and canon together, not by pretending every detail of the prologue is restated in the Cana narrative.

## 5. Relationship to Genesis 1–2 and the completed GUA-W

Genesis 1:1–3 presents God as Creator and records His speaking. Genesis 2:1–5 declares the heavens, earth, and all their host finished, and records God's completed work and rest. In the completed GUA-W, this is read within the governing order:

**Author → Word → Creation → Witness**

Cana offers a canonical point of contact:
- Genesis establishes the Creator and the created order.
- John identifies the Word as the One through whom all things were made.
- John 2 records the Word made flesh acting in the created world.
- The sign is registered by human witnesses and explicitly interpreted by John as manifestation of glory.

This is a supported canonical relationship, not a claim that Cana reproduces the six days of Genesis, repeats the original act of Creation, or reveals a new stage in the GUA-W sequence.

### What “operating from the Unified State” can responsibly mean here

If by this phrase we mean that Jesus acts as the incarnate Word whose relation to Creation is prior and foundational—not as a created being dependent upon an independent authority over Creation—then John 1:1–3, 1:14 and the wider canonical testimony support that description.

If the phrase is intended to mean a specific technical operation, state transition, or physical mechanism, John 2 does not define one. That stronger claim remains **UNRESOLVED**, pending an explicit definition grounded in the completed GUA-W and further Scriptural evidence.

## 6. Triple Constraint Law

### Biblical compliance — PASS for the limited claims recorded
John 2:1–11 explicitly attributes the sign to Jesus, records the servants' obedience and differentiated knowledge, and states that Jesus manifested His glory and the disciples believed. John 1:1–14 and Hebrews 1:1–3 supply the canonical identity context. No claim is made that Mary or the servants possess the authority to produce the sign.

### Scientific coherence — PASS for restraint; mechanism UNRESOLVED
The report is treated as a unique event narrated by the Gospel. The text does not provide chemical measurements, a process description, or experimental conditions. Scientific coherence requires not fabricating such details. A modern account of ordinary fermentation or wine production would not, by itself, explain the event John reports; nor does the absence of a described mechanism constitute a scientific disproof of the reported event. Historical assessment of the report is a distinct inquiry and remains open.

### GUA-W consistency — PASS for the canonical relationship; technical mapping UNRESOLVED
The reading preserves God as Original Cause, the Word's unique relation to Creation, and the derivative role of human witnesses. It does not add a layer, stage, or mechanism to the completed architecture. A more exact placement than the governing Author–Word–Creation–Witness relationship is not asserted from this passage alone.

## 7. Findings and open questions

**Finding 1 — The sign's source is attributed to Jesus.**  
The narrator calls the result water made wine and places Jesus' commands at the center of the event.

**Finding 2 — Human participation is real but derivative.**  
The servants fill, draw, and carry. Their actions are part of the narrative, but the sign's agency is not attributed to them.

**Finding 3 — Witnesses can register different aspects of one event.**  
The servants know the source; the governor tastes and evaluates the wine without knowing where it came from. John then supplies the theological interpretation for the reader.

**Finding 4 — The sign has an expressly stated testimonial purpose.**  
John 2:11 connects the sign with Jesus' glory and the disciples' belief. John 20:30–31 later states the purpose of the written signs in the Gospel.

**Open question 1:** How does “mine hour” develop across John's Gospel, and how does that development shape the Cana account?  
**Open question 2:** What is the relationship between the sign's material result and the Johannine theme of glory?  
**Open question 3:** How does Cana compare with later signs where Jesus speaks and the created condition changes—especially the healing at a distance (John 4:46–54), the man born blind (John 9), and Lazarus (John 11)?  
**Open question 4:** Does “Unified State” name only the completed Creation's unity under its Author, or is a more precise meaning intended in the user's GUA-W vocabulary? The repository's current architecture document does not define the phrase.

## 8. Provisional disposition

**CANA (JOHN 2:1–11):** Biblical attribution of the sign to Jesus — **PASS**.  
**Canonical relationship to the Word through whom Creation was made — PASS** as a Bible-wide reading.  
**Exact physical mechanism — UNRESOLVED / not described by the text.**  
**Specific GUA-W runtime-stage assignment — NOT ESTABLISHED and not required for this finding.**

The passage presents a sign, not a laboratory protocol. Its own stated testimony is that Jesus manifested His glory and His disciples believed on Him.

**The Word speaks. The servants obey. The sign is witnessed. The glory is manifested.**
