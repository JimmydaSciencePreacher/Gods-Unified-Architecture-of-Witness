# Investigation II.24 — Blind Men Near Jericho; Bartimaeus
**Primary texts:** Matthew 20:29–34; Mark 10:46–52; Luke 18:35–43 (KJV)  
**Related texts:** Matthew 9:27–31; Isaiah 35:5; Matthew 11:2–6  
**Status:** Comparative case study recorded; the differences in number and setting are retained.

## Question
What do the three accounts report about blind men near Jericho, especially the appeal to Jesus as Son of David, the restoration of sight, and the response that follows?

## Narrative sequence
- Jesus approaches or departs from Jericho in the accounts. Matthew and Mark place the event as Jesus goes out of Jericho; Luke introduces it as He draws nigh unto Jericho.
- Matthew reports two blind men. Mark names Bartimaeus, the son of Timaeus, and narrates one man. Luke narrates one blind man.
- The men/man cry for mercy and address Jesus as Son of David. The crowd attempts to silence them; they cry out more.
- Jesus stands still and calls the man/men to Him. Mark records Bartimaeus casting away his garment, rising, and coming to Jesus.
- Jesus asks what they want Him to do. The request is for sight.
- Jesus has compassion / says the man's faith has made him whole, according to the respective accounts.
- They receive sight and follow Jesus; Luke records the man glorifying God and all the people giving praise.

## Differences that must remain visible
Matthew says two men; Mark and Luke focus on one, with Mark naming Bartimaeus. Matthew and Mark say Jesus was going out of Jericho; Luke says He was drawing near. Proposed harmonizations exist, but this case study does not treat any one harmonization as established. The differences are recorded for further textual and historical examination.

## What the text states
The appeal is persistent, the messianic title “Son of David” is used, Jesus stops and responds, and sight is restored. The recipients follow Jesus; Luke explicitly notes praise to God.

## Witness and agency
The blind men cry out; the crowd tries to silence them; Jesus calls and responds; they receive sight and follow. The crowd's attempt to silence is itself part of the witness environment. The healed man's/men's response includes following Jesus, and Luke adds public glorification.

## Canonical context
The “Son of David” title and restored sight connect with Matthew's and Luke's wider presentation of Jesus and with prophetic expectation (Isaiah 35:5; Matthew 11:2–6). The account must be read as a journey narrative on the way to Jerusalem and the Passion, not as an isolated healing detached from its setting.

## What the text does not state
No medical cause, eye examination, or physical mechanism is given. The text does not provide enough detail to decide the exact route or chronology by imposing a harmonization.

## Triple Constraint
- **Biblical compliance — PASS:** each Gospel's number, naming, location wording, and sequence are retained.
- **Scientific/historical coherence — PARTIAL:** geographical and compositional questions remain open; no unsupported harmonization is asserted.
- **GUA-W consistency — PASS at governing level; detailed mapping UNRESOLVED:** Christ acts; recipients follow and praise; witnesses respond differently. No new architecture is proposed.

## Finding
This event is a useful control against forced harmonization. The shared core is clear, but the accounts preserve distinct details. Faithful investigation records both the common testimony and the differences still requiring explanation.

**They cry for mercy. Jesus stops. Sight is restored. The healed follow Him toward Jerusalem.**
