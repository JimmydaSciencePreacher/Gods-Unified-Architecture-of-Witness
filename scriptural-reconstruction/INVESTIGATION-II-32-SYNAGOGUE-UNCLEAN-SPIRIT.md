# Investigation II.32 — The Man with an Unclean Spirit in the Synagogue
**Primary texts:** Mark 1:21–28; Luke 4:31–37 (KJV)  
**Status:** Comparative case study recorded; no modern diagnosis inferred.

## Question
What do Mark and Luke report when Jesus commands an unclean spirit to come out of a man in the synagogue at Capernaum?

## Narrative sequence
- Jesus teaches in the synagogue on the sabbath; the people are astonished at His doctrine because He teaches with authority.
- A man with an unclean spirit cries out, addressing Jesus as “Jesus of Nazareth” and “the Holy One of God.”
- Jesus rebukes the spirit and commands it to hold its peace and come out.
- The spirit throws the man among the people / convulses him, cries with a loud voice, and comes out without injuring him.
- The people are amazed and ask what new doctrine this is, noting that Jesus commands unclean spirits with authority and they obey.
- The report of Jesus' fame spreads through the region.

## Differences to preserve
Mark and Luke share the event and central sequence. Mark includes the spirit's question, “Art thou come to destroy us?” and the plural “I know thee who thou art.” Luke emphasizes the spirit's recognition of Jesus as the Christ and says it came out without hurting him. Keep each Gospel's wording visible.

## What the text states
The spirit recognizes Jesus; Jesus commands; the spirit obeys and departs; the man is not injured by the final expulsion. The crowd identifies the authority of Jesus' command as astonishing.

## Witness and agency
Jesus teaches and commands with authority. The spirit speaks and is subject to His command. The man is the one delivered. The congregation witnesses and reports. The narrative's emphasis is not a technique performed by Jesus but authority expressed in His word.

## What the text does not state
The text does not supply a modern psychiatric or neurological diagnosis, nor a clinical mechanism. Do not reduce the passage's explicit spiritual account to a modern diagnostic label.

## Triple Constraint
- **Biblical compliance — PASS:** setting, recognition, command, departure, and public response are explicit.
- **Scientific coherence — PASS for restraint; clinical classification/mechanism UNRESOLVED:** no modern clinical evidence is presented.
- **GUA-W consistency — PASS at governing level; detailed mapping UNRESOLVED:** Jesus' authority is explicit; the spirit obeys; the delivered man and congregation witness. No new architecture is proposed.

## Finding
This account makes the authority of Jesus' word conspicuous: the unclean spirit recognizes Him and obeys His command. The crowd's question concerns the kind of authority by which He acts.

**He commands. The spirit comes out. The people witness the authority of His word.**
