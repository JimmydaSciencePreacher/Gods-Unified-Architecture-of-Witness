# Genesis 2–3 — Recognized Relationships and Operational Distinctions

**Status:** Investigation record of Scriptural relationships and operational distinctions already examined against the completed GUA-W. This is not a queue for constructing, adding, or completing Gears.  
**Source scope:** Genesis 2:18–25 and Genesis 3:1–13, read with Genesis 2:16–17 and the later accountability statement in Genesis 3:17.  
**Purpose:** Record the relationships made visible by Genesis 2–3 and recognize their place in the completed GUA-W. The observations below are not proposals to add new architecture. Where a precise technical generalization is not established, the limitation is stated at that level.

## Governing hierarchy

**Author → Word → Creation → Witness**

God alone is Original Cause. A derivative witness may receive, recognize, name, interpret, communicate, and act, but does not become the Author or acquire authority to alter the Source.

This record does not create new revelation, doctrine, or architecture. It identifies textual observations and their relationship to established GUA-W distinctions. Biblical compliance, scientific coherence, and GUA-W consistency remain jointly required under the Triple Constraint Law.

## 1. Desirability and Admissibility

**Primary textual basis:** Genesis 2:9, 2:16–17; 3:5–6.

**Observed sequence**
- God provides trees described as good for food and pleasant to sight (Genesis 2:9).
- God establishes permission and a specific prohibition (Genesis 2:16–17).
- The serpent introduces an alternative representation and promise (Genesis 3:1–5).
- The woman evaluates the prohibited tree as good for food, pleasant to the eyes, and desirable for wisdom, then takes and eats (Genesis 3:6).

**Observed distinction:** An apparent benefit, desirability, usefulness, or promised outcome does not independently establish admissibility. Evaluation must be tested against the Author's already-established Word and constraint.

**Recognized formulation:** Desirability is not admissibility. Apparent benefit does not override an established constraint.

**Architecture relationship:** Recognized in relation to Admissibility and Constraint. It may be a gate or audit operation rather than a new layer.

**Limits:** The passage supports the distinction in this narrated event. It does not yet establish a universal algorithm or complete decision procedure for all domains.

**Finding:** The narrated event distinguishes apparent desirability from admissibility under the Author's prior command. This is recognized in relation to the existing Admissibility and Constraint elements. The event does not by itself define a universal decision algorithm.

## 2. Perception, Evaluation, Desire, Action, and Resulting State

**Primary textual basis:** Genesis 3:6–7.

**Observed sequence**
1. Seeing/perception.
2. Evaluation as good for food.
3. Evaluation as pleasant/desirable to the eyes.
4. Evaluation as desirable for gaining wisdom.
5. Taking.
6. Eating.
7. Giving to the husband.
8. His eating.
9. Changed awareness and self-covering (Genesis 3:7).

**Observed operation/distinction:** Distinguish perception, evaluation, desire, taking, consumption/action, propagation to another participant, and resulting state rather than collapsing them into a single event.

**Architecture relationship:** Recognized in relation to the existing Runtime operations and Witness-state. It may be a sequence of sub-operations, not one Gear.

**Limits:** Genesis narrates this sequence but does not provide a complete psychological or biological mechanism. The account does not establish that seeing, evaluation, desire, or seeking wisdom is inherently defective. The decisive textual boundary is the prior command.

**Finding:** Genesis 3:6–7 explicitly distinguishes these steps in the narrated event. They are recognized in relation to the existing Runtime and State descriptions; a complete psychological or biological mechanism is not claimed.

## 3. Source, Message, Reception, and Reported Wording

**Primary textual basis:** Genesis 3:1–5, 3:11–13.

**Observed sequence**
- God gives the original command (Genesis 2:16–17).
- The serpent questions and reframes it, then directly contradicts it (Genesis 3:1, 4–5).
- The woman reports the command with wording differences (Genesis 3:2–3).
- After the action, God asks Adam, “Who told thee that thou wast naked?” (Genesis 3:11).
- Adam does not directly identify an informant in his answer; he reports fear, refers to the woman, and admits eating (Genesis 3:10, 12).
- The woman identifies the serpent's deception and admits eating (Genesis 3:13).

**Observed operation/distinction:** Track the originating source of information, the communicating witness, the recipient, the reported wording, and any difference between the original message and its retelling.

**Audit questions raised by the text**
- What is the original source?
- Who communicated the information?
- What wording was received or reported?
- What changed between source and report?
- Which parts are explicit, inferred, or unknown?

**Architecture relationship:** Recognized in relation to Reception, Witness-state, and the distinction between Source and representation.

**Limits:** Genesis does not narrate the exact transmission event by which the woman received the original command. God's question in Genesis 3:11 makes provenance salient but does not record Adam naming an informant. Do not fill those gaps with assumptions.

**Finding:** The passage makes source and message fidelity materially relevant. This is recognized in relation to Source/representation, Reception, and Witness-state. The exact transmission by which the woman received the original command is not narrated and remains unknown.

## 4. Influence, Action, and Responsibility

**Primary textual basis:** Genesis 3:6, 3:12–13, 3:17.

**Observed sequence**
- The woman identifies the serpent's deception and says, “I did eat” (Genesis 3:13).
- Adam identifies the woman's giving and says, “I did eat” (Genesis 3:12).
- God later identifies Adam's heeding of his wife's voice in connection with eating what God had prohibited (Genesis 3:17).

**Observed operation/distinction:** Distinguish an influence or preceding communication from the recipient's own action and accountability. Identifying an influence does not automatically transfer or erase the actor's responsibility.

**Architecture relationship:** Recognized in relation to Runtime action, attribution, and accountability review.

**Limits:** Do not infer identical responsibility, identical knowledge, or identical psychological circumstances for Adam and the woman. Do not infer physical compulsion. The text explicitly records each person's action and gives a specific divine assessment of Adam's heeding.

**Finding:** The text distinguishes influence, each person's action, and divine attribution. This is recognized as a distinction within the existing Runtime and accountability inquiry; identical responsibility or psychological circumstances are not inferred.

## 5. Author-Initiated Accountability Encounter

**Primary textual basis:** Genesis 3:9–13.

**Observed sequence**
- God calls Adam (3:9).
- Adam responds (3:10).
- God asks about the source of Adam's awareness and separately asks whether he ate (3:11).
- Adam answers and acknowledges eating (3:12).
- God questions the woman about her action (3:13).
- The woman identifies deception and acknowledges eating.

**Observed operation/distinction:** The Author initiates an encounter in which the recipient is called to respond and the account of knowledge and action is examined.

**Architecture relationship:** An audit/governance relationship, not necessarily a runtime Gear; it relates to witness accountability and Author primacy.

**Limits:** Do not imply that God asks because He lacks knowledge. The text records the call and questions; claims about the full purpose of each question require wider Scriptural study.

**Finding:** The event is explicit in Genesis 3:9–13 and is recognized in relation to Author primacy and witness accountability. Whether it should be called a universal Gear is not required to preserve the observed relationship.

## 6. Recognition and Alignment

**Primary textual basis:** Genesis 3:8–10, read with Genesis 2:16–17.

**Observed sequence**
- Adam and the woman hear the LORD God (3:8).
- Adam correctly identifies that he heard God's voice (3:10).
- Adam reports fear and hiding rather than approaching openly.

**Observed operation/distinction:** Distinguish recognition of a source from alignment or response to that source. Knowing who is speaking does not by itself establish that the recipient is aligned with the speaker.

**Architecture relationship:** A distinction within existing Witness-state / Alignment concepts rather than a new Gear.

**Limits:** This is a textual and theological observation about the narrated response, not a complete theory of alignment.

**Finding:** Recognition of the source and alignment of response are distinct in the narrated event. This belongs in the investigation of the existing Alignment and Witness-state terms, not as an assumed new layer.

## 7. Correspondence and Joining

**Primary textual basis:** Genesis 2:18–25.

**Observed sequence**
- God identifies that man is alone and declares His intention to make a corresponding help (2:18).
- God forms and brings creatures; Adam names them, but no corresponding help is found among them (2:19–20).
- God makes the woman and brings her to the man (2:21–22).
- Adam recognizes her correspondence and names her (2:23).
- The text states leaving, cleaving, and one flesh (2:24).

**Observed operation/distinction:** Distinguish classification/naming from correspondence and joining. Naming creatures does not fulfill the relational need; the corresponding partner is made and brought by God, recognized by Adam, and the marital joining is stated.

**Architecture relationship:** Related to the existing Joining Primacy investigation. It may provide a specific relational example, but must not be generalized prematurely into a universal integration law.

**Limits:** Genesis 2:24 explicitly concerns the marital relation. “One flesh” must not be reduced to a mathematical identity or used by itself to prove a universal law of integration. The persons remain distinguishable in the text.

**Finding:** Genesis 2:18–25 explicitly records the marital correspondence and joining pattern. This is recognized in relation to the project's Joining Primacy investigation. The passage is not, by itself, a universal mathematical law.

## 8. Relationship among the observations

These seven observations need not represent seven independent Gears. They may describe interacting sub-operations or audit dimensions within existing architectural relationships:

- Reference–Admissibility may govern the evaluation-to-action sequence.
- Information Provenance may be part of Reception and Witness-state auditing.
- Influence–Action–Responsibility may be an accountability distinction.
- Recognition–Alignment may refine an existing state variable.
- Correspondence–Joining may belong to the existing Joining Primacy investigation.

These observations may describe interacting aspects of the existing Admissibility, Constraint, Reception, Runtime, Alignment, Witness-state, and Joining relationships. Similarity of wording is not enough to collapse distinct operations; difference of wording is not enough to invent additional Gears. The completed architecture remains the reference for recognizing their relationships.

## 11. Changed Conditions, Continuing Function, and Consequence

**Primary textual basis:** Genesis 2:7, 2:15; 3:17–19.

**Observed sequence**
- Man is formed from the dust/ground and receives life from God (Genesis 2:7).
- Man is placed in the Garden with the assigned work of dressing and keeping it (Genesis 2:15).
- Following the disobedience, God pronounces the ground cursed in connection with Adam (Genesis 3:17).
- The ground brings forth thorns and thistles; food is obtained through sorrow and sweat (Genesis 3:17–19).
- Man is told he will return to the ground from which he was taken (Genesis 3:19).

**Observed operation/distinction:** A consequential action is followed by a changed environmental condition under which an existing function continues with altered conditions and outcomes.

**Recognized formulation:** The function may remain while the conditions of its realization change.

**Architecture relationship:** Potentially connects State, Constraint, Structure, Runtime, and environment. It may be a state transition or consequence-propagation relation rather than a separate Gear.

**Limits:** Genesis identifies the ground as cursed in connection with Adam's action and describes hardship in cultivation. It does not state that all physical laws changed, that every ecological process became wholly different, or that work itself originated with the Fall. Work was assigned in Genesis 2:15 before the Fall. The text distinguishes the prior assignment from the post-Fall conditions.

**Finding:** Genesis 3:17–19 explicitly describes a changed human–environment condition and the continuation of work under altered conditions. This relationship is recorded in relation to the existing State, Constraint, Structure, Runtime, and environment distinctions. The text does not establish that every physical law changed or specify every mechanism.


## 12. Provision, Authorization, and Access Boundary

**Primary textual basis:** Genesis 2:9; 3:22–24.

**Observed sequence**
- The tree of life is present among the Garden's trees (Genesis 2:9).
- After the disobedience, God identifies the possibility that man might take from the tree of life and live forever (Genesis 3:22).
- God sends man out of the Garden (Genesis 3:23).
- God places cherubim and a flaming sword to guard the way to the tree of life (Genesis 3:24).

**Observed operation/distinction:** Distinguish the existence of a provision from a recipient's authorization to access it. The Author establishes the boundary and appoints its enforcement.

**Recognized formulation:** The existence of a provision does not establish unrestricted access to it. Authorization and access boundaries remain under the Author's authority.

**Architecture relationship:** Recognized in relation to Admissibility, Constraint, Reception, and Governance. It is related to but not identical with the Reference–Admissibility Gate: that relationship examines whether an action conforms to the Author's Word; this access-boundary relationship examines whether access to a provision is authorized and how an established boundary is maintained.

**Limits:** Genesis 3:22–24 explicitly records exclusion and guarding of the way to the tree of life. It does not provide a universal access-control algorithm or explain every possible condition for later access. Revelation 22:1–2 supplies a canonical endpoint for further study, not permission to erase the distinction between the two passages.

**Finding:** Genesis 3:22–24 explicitly records exclusion and guarding of access to the tree of life. This is recognized in relation to the existing Admissibility and Constraint distinctions and the Author's authority over access. No universal access-control algorithm is inferred.
