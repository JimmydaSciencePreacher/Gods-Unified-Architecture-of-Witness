# GUA-W Candidate Gear Register — Genesis 2–3

**Status:** PROVISIONAL INVESTIGATION REGISTER; no candidate in this document is admitted as an established GUA-W Gear or canon node.  
**Source scope:** Genesis 2:18–25 and Genesis 3:1–13, read with Genesis 2:16–17 and the later accountability statement in Genesis 3:17.  
**Purpose:** Preserve potentially useful operations discovered during text-first reconstruction so they can be compared with the existing GUA-W architecture, tested across Scripture and relevant sciences, and either integrated, consolidated, revised, or rejected.

## Governing hierarchy

**Author → Word → Creation → Witness**

God alone is Original Cause. A derivative witness may receive, recognize, name, interpret, communicate, and act, but does not become the Author or acquire authority to alter the Source.

This register records architectural candidates, not new revelation, doctrine, or settled project canon. Biblical compliance, scientific coherence, and GUA-W consistency remain jointly required under the Triple Constraint Law.

## 1. Candidate: Reference–Admissibility Gate

**Primary textual basis:** Genesis 2:9, 2:16–17; 3:5–6.

**Observed sequence**
- God provides trees described as good for food and pleasant to sight (Genesis 2:9).
- God establishes permission and a specific prohibition (Genesis 2:16–17).
- The serpent introduces an alternative representation and promise (Genesis 3:1–5).
- The woman evaluates the prohibited tree as good for food, pleasant to the eyes, and desirable for wisdom, then takes and eats (Genesis 3:6).

**Candidate operation:** An apparent benefit, desirability, usefulness, or promised outcome does not independently establish admissibility. Evaluation must be tested against the Author's already-established Word and constraint.

**Provisional formulation:** Desirability is not admissibility. Apparent benefit does not override an established constraint.

**Architecture relationship:** Potentially associated with Admissibility and Constraint. It may be a gate or audit operation rather than a new layer.

**Limits:** The passage supports the distinction in this narrated event. It does not yet establish a universal algorithm or complete decision procedure for all domains.

**Status:** STRONG TEXTUAL CANDIDATE; generalization remains to be tested.

## 2. Candidate: Evaluation-to-Action Sequence

**Primary textual basis:** Genesis 3:6–7.

**Observed sequence**
1. Seeing/perception.
2. Evaluation as good for food.
3. Evaluation as pleasant/desirable to the eyes.
4. Evaluation as desirable for gaining wisdom.
5. Taking.
6. Eating.
7. Giving to the husband.
8. His eating.
9. Changed awareness and self-covering (Genesis 3:7).

**Candidate operation:** Distinguish perception, evaluation, desire, taking, consumption/action, propagation to another participant, and resulting state rather than collapsing them into a single event.

**Architecture relationship:** Potentially maps to existing Runtime operations and Witness-state. It may be a sequence of sub-operations, not one Gear.

**Limits:** Genesis narrates this sequence but does not provide a complete psychological or biological mechanism. The account does not establish that seeing, evaluation, desire, or seeking wisdom is inherently defective. The decisive textual boundary is the prior command.

**Status:** STRONG TEXTUAL SEQUENCE; universal runtime mapping remains provisional.

## 3. Candidate: Information Provenance / Source Audit

**Primary textual basis:** Genesis 3:1–5, 3:11–13.

**Observed sequence**
- God gives the original command (Genesis 2:16–17).
- The serpent questions and reframes it, then directly contradicts it (Genesis 3:1, 4–5).
- The woman reports the command with wording differences (Genesis 3:2–3).
- After the action, God asks Adam, “Who told thee that thou wast naked?” (Genesis 3:11).
- Adam does not directly identify an informant in his answer; he reports fear, refers to the woman, and admits eating (Genesis 3:10, 12).
- The woman identifies the serpent's deception and admits eating (Genesis 3:13).

**Candidate operation:** Track the originating source of information, the communicating witness, the recipient, the reported wording, and any difference between the original message and its retelling.

**Provisional audit questions**
- What is the original source?
- Who communicated the information?
- What wording was received or reported?
- What changed between source and report?
- Which parts are explicit, inferred, or unknown?

**Architecture relationship:** Potentially associated with Reception, Witness-state, and the distinction between Source and representation.

**Limits:** Genesis does not narrate the exact transmission event by which the woman received the original command. God's question in Genesis 3:11 makes provenance salient but does not record Adam naming an informant. Do not fill those gaps with assumptions.

**Status:** STRONG TEXTUAL AUDIT CANDIDATE; transmission details remain partly unresolved.

## 4. Candidate: Influence–Action–Responsibility Separation

**Primary textual basis:** Genesis 3:6, 3:12–13, 3:17.

**Observed sequence**
- The woman identifies the serpent's deception and says, “I did eat” (Genesis 3:13).
- Adam identifies the woman's giving and says, “I did eat” (Genesis 3:12).
- God later identifies Adam's heeding of his wife's voice in connection with eating what God had prohibited (Genesis 3:17).

**Candidate operation:** Distinguish an influence or preceding communication from the recipient's own action and accountability. Identifying an influence does not automatically transfer or erase the actor's responsibility.

**Architecture relationship:** Potentially associated with Runtime action, attribution, and accountability review.

**Limits:** Do not infer identical responsibility, identical knowledge, or identical psychological circumstances for Adam and the woman. Do not infer physical compulsion. The text explicitly records each person's action and gives a specific divine assessment of Adam's heeding.

**Status:** STRONG TEXTUAL DISTINCTION; broader formalization remains provisional.

## 5. Candidate: Author-Initiated Accountability Encounter

**Primary textual basis:** Genesis 3:9–13.

**Observed sequence**
- God calls Adam (3:9).
- Adam responds (3:10).
- God asks about the source of Adam's awareness and separately asks whether he ate (3:11).
- Adam answers and acknowledges eating (3:12).
- God questions the woman about her action (3:13).
- The woman identifies deception and acknowledges eating.

**Candidate operation:** The Author initiates an encounter in which the recipient is called to respond and the account of knowledge and action is examined.

**Architecture relationship:** Potentially an audit or governance operation, not necessarily a runtime Gear. It may relate to witness accountability and Author-node primacy.

**Limits:** Do not imply that God asks because He lacks knowledge. The text records the call and questions; claims about the full purpose of each question require wider Scriptural study.

**Status:** TEXTUALLY CLEAR AS AN EVENT; UNIVERSAL GEAR STATUS UNRESOLVED.

## 6. Candidate: Recognition–Alignment Distinction

**Primary textual basis:** Genesis 3:8–10, read with Genesis 2:16–17.

**Observed sequence**
- Adam and the woman hear the LORD God (3:8).
- Adam correctly identifies that he heard God's voice (3:10).
- Adam reports fear and hiding rather than approaching openly.

**Candidate operation:** Distinguish recognition of a source from alignment or response to that source. Knowing who is speaking does not by itself establish that the recipient is aligned with the speaker.

**Architecture relationship:** Likely a distinction within existing Witness-state / Alignment concepts rather than a new Gear.

**Limits:** This is a textual and theological observation about the narrated response, not a complete theory of alignment.

**Status:** STRONG DISTINCTION; likely an existing-state refinement.

## 7. Candidate: Correspondence–Joining Operation

**Primary textual basis:** Genesis 2:18–25.

**Observed sequence**
- God identifies that man is alone and declares His intention to make a corresponding help (2:18).
- God forms and brings creatures; Adam names them, but no corresponding help is found among them (2:19–20).
- God makes the woman and brings her to the man (2:21–22).
- Adam recognizes her correspondence and names her (2:23).
- The text states leaving, cleaving, and one flesh (2:24).

**Candidate operation:** Distinguish classification/naming from correspondence and joining. Naming creatures does not fulfill the relational need; the corresponding partner is made and brought by God, recognized by Adam, and the marital joining is stated.

**Architecture relationship:** Related to the existing Joining Primacy investigation. It may provide a specific relational example, but must not be generalized prematurely into a universal integration law.

**Limits:** Genesis 2:24 explicitly concerns the marital relation. “One flesh” must not be reduced to a mathematical identity or used by itself to prove a universal law of integration. The persons remain distinguishable in the text.

**Status:** STRONG TEXTUAL BASIS FOR THE MARRIAGE/JOINING PATTERN; UNIVERSAL ARCHITECTURAL GENERALIZATION UNRESOLVED.

## 8. Consolidation warning — Do not multiply Gears unnecessarily

The candidates may not be seven independent Gears. Several may be sub-operations or audit dimensions of a smaller number of mechanisms:

- Reference–Admissibility may govern the evaluation-to-action sequence.
- Information Provenance may be part of Reception and Witness-state auditing.
- Influence–Action–Responsibility may be an accountability distinction.
- Recognition–Alignment may refine an existing state variable.
- Correspondence–Joining may belong to the existing Joining Primacy investigation.

The architecture should determine whether each candidate is a Gear, a sub-operation, a state transition, an invariant, or merely a useful audit question. Similarity of wording is not enough to merge them; difference of wording is not enough to make them separate.

## 11. Candidate: Consequence–Environment–Function Transition

**Primary textual basis:** Genesis 2:7, 2:15; 3:17–19.

**Observed sequence**
- Man is formed from the dust/ground and receives life from God (Genesis 2:7).
- Man is placed in the Garden with the assigned work of dressing and keeping it (Genesis 2:15).
- Following the disobedience, God pronounces the ground cursed in connection with Adam (Genesis 3:17).
- The ground brings forth thorns and thistles; food is obtained through sorrow and sweat (Genesis 3:17–19).
- Man is told he will return to the ground from which he was taken (Genesis 3:19).

**Candidate operation:** A consequential action is followed by a changed environmental condition under which an existing function continues with altered conditions and outcomes.

**Provisional formulation:** The function may remain while the conditions of its realization change.

**Architecture relationship:** Potentially connects State, Constraint, Structure, Runtime, and environment. It may be a state transition or consequence-propagation relation rather than a separate Gear.

**Limits:** Genesis identifies the ground as cursed in connection with Adam's action and describes hardship in cultivation. It does not state that all physical laws changed, that every ecological process became wholly different, or that work itself originated with the Fall. Work was assigned in Genesis 2:15 before the Fall. The text distinguishes the prior assignment from the post-Fall conditions.

**Status:** STRONG TEXTUAL BASIS FOR A CHANGED HUMAN–ENVIRONMENT CONDITION; UNIVERSAL ARCHITECTURAL GENERALIZATION UNRESOLVED.
