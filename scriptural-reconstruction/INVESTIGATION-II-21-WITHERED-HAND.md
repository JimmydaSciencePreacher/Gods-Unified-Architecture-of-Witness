# Investigation II.21 — The Man with the Withered Hand
**Primary texts:** Matthew 12:9–14; Mark 3:1–6; Luke 6:6–11 (KJV)  
**Related texts:** Exodus 20:8–11; Deuteronomy 5:12–15; Isaiah 1:10–17; John 5:1–18  
**Status:** Comparative case study recorded; underlying condition and physical mechanism are not specified.

## Question
What do the Synoptic accounts report when Jesus heals a man with a withered hand on the sabbath, and why does the controversy matter to the narrative?

## Narrative sequence
- Jesus enters a synagogue on the sabbath. A man with a withered hand is present.
- The accounts report that the Pharisees/watchers observe Jesus, seeking an accusation. Matthew says they ask whether it is lawful to heal on the sabbath.
- Jesus answers by reasoning from the value of rescuing a sheep or doing good rather than evil on the sabbath. Mark and Luke preserve the question, “Is it lawful to do good on the sabbath days, or to do evil? to save life, or to kill?”
- Jesus tells the man to stand forth / rise into the midst and commands him to stretch forth his hand.
- The man does so; the hand is restored whole like the other.
- The opponents are filled with anger and take counsel against Jesus. Mark records the Pharisees going out and taking counsel with the Herodians against Him.

## Differences to preserve
Matthew gives the sheep illustration and frames the act as doing good on the sabbath. Mark and Luke emphasize Jesus' question about doing good or evil and saving or killing. Mark records Jesus looking round with anger, being grieved at the hardness of their hearts. Do not flatten these narrative emphases.

## What the text states
The man's hand is withered; Jesus commands him to stretch it forth; the man does so and it is restored. The healing occurs in a synagogue on the sabbath and provokes opposition.

## Witness and agency
Jesus sees, questions, commands, and restores. The man obeys and displays the result. The observers witness but interpret the act through hostility and seek grounds against Jesus.

## Canonical context
The sabbath controversy is not incidental. Jesus argues from doing good and preserving life. Compare His other sabbath healings (John 5; Luke 13:10–17; Luke 14:1–6). The passage challenges the opponents' application of sabbath law; it does not authorize careless disregard of Scripture.

## What the text does not state
No diagnosis, injury history, anatomy, duration, or biological mechanism is given. The text does not say the hand was amputated or that the man had a particular modern condition.

## Triple Constraint
- **Biblical compliance — PASS:** command, response, restoration, sabbath setting, and opposition are explicit.
- **Scientific coherence — PASS for restraint; mechanism UNRESOLVED:** no clinical details are supplied.
- **GUA-W consistency — PASS at governing level; technical mapping UNRESOLVED:** Jesus acts with authority; the man receives and witnesses; opponents interpret. No new architecture is proposed.

## Finding
The event reveals more than restoration of a limb's function. It exposes a conflict over the meaning of lawful action and the purpose of the sabbath. The healed man obeys Jesus' command; the same visible event becomes evidence to observers whose responses diverge.

**Jesus commands the hand to stretch forth. The man obeys. The hand is restored—and the witnesses divide.**
