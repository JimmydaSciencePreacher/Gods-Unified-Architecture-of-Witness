## 1. Governing order

**Author → Word → Creation → Witness**

God alone is Original Cause. The Word is the originating Word through whom Creation is made. Human beings are real, responsible, willing, acting creatures; their agency does not make them the Author or grant them authority to originate the divine standard.

The completed GUA-W remains unchanged. This synthesis records the relationships among the Scriptural investigations; it does not construct a salvation mechanism or add an architectural stage.

The Triple Constraint remains:
1. Biblical compliance.
2. Scientific coherence, using the methods appropriate to theological, linguistic, historical, and other relevant inquiry.
3. GUA-W consistency.

Theology is a Science within GUA-W. The passages below are examined through their Scriptural contexts, language, canonical relations, and theological claims. Physical or biological mechanisms are not inferred where the texts do not specify them.

## 2. The foundational connection: Genesis 1–3 defines the question before later passages answer it

The Human Will Reference Work begins by distinguishing choice, responsibility, ability, willingness, divine initiative, regeneration, faith, and salvation. These distinctions are necessary, but the repository's governing correction requires that questions about the human will return first to Genesis 1–2.

Genesis 1–2 records humanity as created by God, made in His image, blessed, commissioned, provided for, placed, and addressed by divine command. Genesis 2:16–17 gives both broad permission and a specific prohibition. Genesis 3 then records the serpent's challenge to God's Word, the woman's seeing and evaluation, desire, taking, eating, giving, and the man's eating.

The foundational finding is not that humans have no will. Genesis 3 displays human perception, evaluation, desire, decision, and action. Nor does the account say that the creature becomes the Author. The Creator retains authority over the command and the standard; the creature acts within a created and accountable relation to that authority.

**Combined finding:** Human willing is real creaturely agency. It is not original authorship, and the capacity to perceive, desire, choose, and act does not itself establish an independent authority to determine good and evil or an autonomous power to originate spiritual life.

This distinction prevents two opposite errors:
- reducing the human being to an inactive object with no meaningful agency;
- turning the reality of human choice and responsibility into proof that the human will is the originating cause of salvation.

Genesis 3:22–24 and the wider canonical record must be retained when discussing the consequences of the Fall. The precise nature and scope of the knowledge described in Genesis 3:22 remain a bounded interpretive question; the transfer of God's authority to establish the standard is not stated or granted.

## 3. Genesis 3 and Romans 1: the departure is not the disappearance of knowledge

The Genesis 3 reconstruction and Romans 1:18–32 illuminate one another.

### Genesis 3

The serpent questions and contradicts the Word already given. The woman sees, evaluates, desires, takes, eats, and gives; Adam eats. The account records real human actions and their consequences. The creature's evaluation does not become the Author's authority.

### Romans 1:18–32

Paul describes people who have knowledge of God made manifest, yet do not glorify Him as God or give thanks. They suppress truth in unrighteousness, exchange the truth of God for a lie, refuse to retain God in knowledge, and are given over to consequences of their departure.

Romans 1 does not say that human perception, reasoning, investigation, or practical action disappears. It describes the suppression and exchange of truth, darkened reasoning, and disordered conduct under God's judgment.

### Combined finding

The Fall is not described as the loss of every human capacity to know or act. The Scriptural account describes a departure in the relation of the creature to the Creator and His truth. Human beings continue to perceive, reason, desire, choose, make, and investigate; those capacities do not authorize them to replace the Creator as the source and standard of truth.

**GUA-W relationship:** This is a central witness to the difference between the Source and derivative witness. Creation remains God's; human interpretations and claims are derivative and accountable.

**Boundary:** Romans 1 is a theological argument about knowledge of God, suppression, exchange, and judgment. It is not a claim that every human being has equal information or identical technical knowledge of every created process.

## 4. John 1, John 3, and John 6: receiving, believing, birth, giving, drawing, and life

These passages must be read together without making their distinct terms interchangeable.

### John 1:12–13 — receiving and the source of birth

John records receiving Christ and believing on His name (v. 12), then identifies the source of the birth of those described: not blood, the will of the flesh, or the will of man, but God (v. 13).

**Finding:** Receiving and believing are real responses; the source of birth is explicitly attributed to God. Verse 13 prevents the reader from treating human will as the originating cause of birth from God.

### John 3:1–8 — necessity of birth from above/from the Spirit

Jesus tells Nicodemus that a man must be born again/from above and distinguishes birth of the flesh from birth of the Spirit. The wind/Spirit comparison emphasizes the Spirit's sovereign action. The passage establishes the necessity and divine source of spiritual birth; the precise interpretation of “water” in John 3:5 remains subject to its own contextual and canonical examination.

### John 6:35–45, 63–65 — coming, believing, the Father's giving and drawing

Jesus explicitly speaks of the person who comes and believes. He also says:
- all that the Father gives Him shall come to Him (6:37);
- no one can come unless the Father draws him (6:44);
- the one who hears and learns from the Father comes to Him (6:45);
- the Spirit gives life (6:63);
- coming is not possible unless it is given by the Father (6:65).

The same discourse records people who see Jesus and do not believe (6:36, 64). Seeing, hearing, or proximity alone must not be equated with believing.

John 12:32 uses the same drawing verb in a different context and must be considered when assessing the lexical scope of “draw.” Its existence cautions against making the verb alone prove every detail of the doctrine of drawing. It does not erase what John 6 explicitly says about the necessity of the Father's drawing and grant.

### John 10:26–30 and John 17:6, 9, 24 — the sheep given to the Son

The further canonical examination in HW-006 compares the Father's giving in John 6 with Jesus' words about His sheep in John 10 and the people given to Him in John 17.

The integrated finding is:
1. The Father gives people to the Son (John 6:37, 39; 10:29; 17:6, 9, 24).
2. Those given come to the Son (John 6:37).
3. Jesus' sheep hear His voice and follow Him (John 10:27).
4. The Son receives and keeps those who come; He gives them eternal life (John 6:37–40; 10:28–29; 17:12).

**Combined finding:** The Father's giving is not presented as a passive response to people who independently arrive. The Son's saving work includes receiving and keeping those given to Him. At the same time, coming, hearing, following, and believing are genuine human responses in the text.

**Bounded question:** These passages establish the Father's giving and the Son's keeping, but do not by themselves identify the exact temporal moment at which each person is given to the Son. The question of the Book of Life and the timing of names written must be examined through its own full canonical record, not inferred solely from the phrase “the Father giveth me.”

## 5. Romans 9–11 and Ephesians 1–2: purpose, mercy, calling, and the real place of faith

### Romans 9–11

Romans 9:6–24 addresses the question of whether God's Word has failed in relation to Israel. Paul discusses promise, election, mercy, Pharaoh, hardening, the potter and clay, vessels of wrath, vessels of mercy, and the calling of Jews and Gentiles.

Romans 9:16 expressly locates the showing of mercy in God rather than the willing or running person. Yet Romans 9 must not be isolated from chapters 10–11:
- Romans 10 describes Christ, the word of faith, believing, confessing, calling, hearing, and preaching.
- Romans 11 continues the remnant, partial hardening, Gentile inclusion, and God's mercy.

The corporate and historical setting of Israel and the nations must be preserved. So must Paul's language concerning children of promise, mercy, calling, vessels, and glory. Romans 9 cannot responsibly be reduced to earthly usefulness alone; neither can it be made to answer every question about the individual order of regeneration and faith without the wider argument.

The grammatical asymmetry in Romans 9:22–23 also remains important: verse 23 explicitly says God prepared the vessels of mercy beforehand; verse 22 does not name the agent in the clause describing vessels of wrath as fitted to destruction. The text must not be made to say more than its grammar says.

### Ephesians 1:3–14

Paul places election, adoption, redemption through Christ's blood, forgiveness, the mystery of God's will, the purpose of the One who works all things after the counsel of His own will, hearing the word of truth, believing, and sealing with the Holy Spirit within one extended account of God's purpose in Christ.

The sentence's order must be retained. Hearing the Gospel, believing, and being sealed are explicitly related in verses 13–14. The passage identifies God's initiative and purpose, but it should not be converted into an exhaustive timetable of every aspect of regeneration and faith.

### Ephesians 2:1–10

Paul describes the former condition as death in trespasses and sins, while also describing real walking, conduct, desire, and disobedience. The turning point is “But God,” who is rich in mercy and makes people alive together with Christ.

Salvation is by grace through faith, not of works. Believers are God's workmanship, created in Christ Jesus unto good works prepared beforehand.

The KJV supplies “hath he quickened” in verse 1; the Greek main verb “made alive” appears in verse 5. This grammatical detail must be retained when describing Paul's sentence.

**Combined finding:** Human conduct and response are real, but do not originate God's mercy or life-giving action. God's purpose, election, mercy, calling, and work are central. Faith is not erased: it is explicitly named in the Gospel account of salvation. The relationship between God's initiative and human believing must be described from the combined testimony, not reduced to a slogan.

## 6. Acts, James, Peter, and Philippians: the means and grant must both be preserved

The canonical completion review of HW-001–HW-020 identified further passages that illuminate specific limits in individual entries.

### Acts 11:18; 13:44–48; 16:14

- Acts 11:18 says God granted repentance unto life to the Gentiles.
- Acts 13:44–48 places Gospel proclamation, rejection, and believing in one narrative; those appointed to eternal life believed. The grammatical form of “ordained” does not itself expressly name its agent, so the contextual conclusion must preserve that qualification.
- Acts 16:14 says the Lord opened Lydia's heart so that she attended to the things spoken by Paul.

These passages preserve both the public proclamation of the Word and divine action in repentance, appointment, and opening.

### Romans 10:14–17

Paul joins preaching, hearing, believing, and calling. Faith comes by hearing, and hearing by the Word of God. The preacher is a real instrument in the proclamation; the preacher is not the originating Author of salvation.

### James 1:16–18

James denies that God is the source of temptation and identifies human lust as involved in temptation and sin. He then says that God, of His own will, begat believers with the Word of truth. The text distinguishes the origin of temptation from the origin of new birth.

James 1:18 does not mention faith or provide a complete chronology of regeneration and faith. It does directly attribute begetting to God's will and identifies the Word of truth in the statement.

### 1 Peter 1:3, 23

Peter blesses God, who according to His abundant mercy has begotten believers again unto a lively hope by the Resurrection of Jesus Christ. Verse 23 describes being born again, not of corruptible seed but of incorruptible, by the Word of God which lives and abides forever.

These passages identify God's mercy and the enduring Word in the new-birth account. They do not independently specify the exact instant at which faith first occurs.

### 1 John 5:1

John says that everyone believing that Jesus is the Christ is born of God. The perfect form translated “is born” describes the believer as one who has been born of God. This supports the source-of-birth distinction, while the exact temporal mechanics and relation between new birth and the act of faith remain a separate question.

### Philippians 1:27–30, especially 1:29

Paul says that it is given to believers not only to believe on Christ but also to suffer for His sake. The active infinitive “to believe” describes the recipients' real believing; it is included within what has been graciously granted. The passive verb does not itself name God as agent, but verse 28's “and that of God” supplies the immediate contextual connection.

**Combined finding:** Scripture identifies the Gospel/Word and its proclamation as the public means through which people hear and believe. It also identifies the Father's giving and drawing, the Lord's opening of the heart, the Spirit's life-giving and sanctifying work, God's begetting, and the gracious grant that includes believing. These descriptions are related but not interchangeable.

## 7. Titus 3:3–8: the clearest compact statement of mercy, regeneration, renewal, justification, and good works

Titus 3:3–8 is especially important because it places several salvation realities in a compact canonical unit.

Paul first describes the former condition: foolishness, disobedience, deception, enslavement to diverse lusts and pleasures, malice, envy, and hatred (v. 3). The text does not portray human beings as without desire or action.

Then the kindness and love of God our Saviour appear (v. 4). Paul says salvation is:
- not by works of righteousness which we have done;
- according to God's mercy;
- by the washing of regeneration and renewing of the Holy Ghost (v. 5);
- with the Holy Ghost poured out abundantly through Jesus Christ our Saviour (v. 6);
- so that, being justified by grace, believers should be made heirs according to the hope of eternal life (v. 7).

Verse 8 continues with the fitting response: those who have believed God should be careful to maintain good works.

**Combined finding:** Titus explicitly attributes the saving action to God, according to His mercy, and describes regeneration and renewal by the Holy Ghost as integral to that saving action. It distinguishes the former human conduct, God's saving action, justification by grace, inheritance, and the good works believers are to maintain.

**Boundary:** Titus 3:3–7 does not name faith in the regeneration sentence; verse 8 immediately speaks of those who have believed God. The passage does not by itself provide a complete chronological sequence specifying the precise moment of regeneration relative to the first act of faith. It should not be left isolated, however: John 1, John 3, John 6, Ephesians 2, Philippians 1:29, 1 Peter 1, and 1 John 5 each contribute relevant information in their own contexts.

## 9. The bounded unresolved question: do not confuse an incomplete chronology with an absent answer

The repository's U-006 remains a bounded investigation concerning the operation, capacity, and limits of the human will. The foundational Genesis 1–3 work has narrowed the question substantially: it establishes the principal operations of human willing and distinguishes creaturely capacity from the Author's authority. An exhaustive abstract definition of the human will is not supplied by Genesis 1–3 and must not be used to keep the entire question perpetually unresolved.

The salvation investigations also establish substantial conclusions:
- autonomous human will is not identified as the originating source of spiritual birth;
- God's mercy and life-giving action are central;
- the Father gives and draws;
- the Spirit gives life and renews;
- the Word/Gospel is proclaimed and heard;
- people truly hear, attend, come, believe, follow, confess, and obey;
- believing itself is included in the gracious grant described in Philippians 1:29.

What remains open is more precise than “Who causes salvation?” or “Is faith a gift?” The reviewed passages identify divine source and initiative, human response, and the Gospel means, but do not yield one exhaustive chronology or technical causal model that assigns every act to an exact instant and relation. The sequence and relation of regeneration, faith, justification, sealing, and other aspects must be stated only to the degree the relevant texts establish them.

**The correct status is not “nothing is known.” It is “substantial canonical findings are established; the remaining chronology and mechanics are bounded.”**

## 10. The Book of Life question: a distinct follow-on investigation, not a shortcut

The Father's giving of people to the Son and the Scriptural references to names written in a book are related subjects, but they must not be treated as identical expressions without examination.

The question “When were the names written?” requires a dedicated comparison of the relevant passages, including their exact wording, textual variants where material, and immediate contexts. The salvation synthesis does not settle that question by inference from John 6:37 or John 17. It identifies the relationship as a next canonical investigation: divine purpose and giving, the Son's receiving and keeping, and the Book of Life testimony must be compared without assuming that each phrase describes the same event or temporal reference.

No date, chronology, or mechanism should be supplied until that passage-level work is complete.

## 12. Triple Constraint disposition

### Biblical compliance

The synthesis reads the passages in their immediate and wider canonical contexts, preserves human response and responsibility, distinguishes source from means and result, and does not manufacture a chronology. The Genesis foundation remains controlling for the Creator/creature distinction.

### Scientific coherence

The claims are theological and textual. Their support is assessed through KJV context, Greek and Hebrew grammar where material, canonical comparison, and documented interpretive limits. No unsupported physical or psychological mechanism is asserted.

### GUA-W consistency

The synthesis preserves God as Original Cause, the Word as originating Word, Creation as God's work, and human beings as derivative yet responsible witnesses and agents. No new layer, Gear, runtime stage, State term, or universal law is added.

**Disposition:** The combined findings are admissible within their stated scope. The exact chronology and mechanics that remain open are retained as bounded questions, not promoted into universal uncertainty.

## Closing statement

The Scriptural record does not present salvation as the achievement of an independent human author. It presents God as the source of life and mercy, the Father giving and drawing, the Spirit quickening and renewing, the Word proclaimed and heard, Christ receiving and keeping, and people truly hearing, coming, believing, following, and living in the good works prepared for them.

The distinctions matter. The unity matters. Neither should be sacrificed to the other.

**One Author. One originating Word. One Creation. Many witnesses.**
