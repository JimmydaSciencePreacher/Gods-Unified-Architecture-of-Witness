# KJV “Freewill” Phrase Register

**Purpose:** Maintain a separate, auditable register of the exact English expression **freewill** in the King James Version. This register is not a register of every biblical word translated “choose,” “will,” “willing,” “freely,” or “voluntary.” Those words require their own contextual and lexical studies.

## Governing distinction

The KJV's joined spelling **freewill** appears in contexts of voluntary offerings and, in Ezra 7:13, a person's voluntary decision to go up to Jerusalem. These occurrences do not, by themselves, define a philosophical doctrine of autonomous human will or establish the source of spiritual birth, faith, or salvation.

The KJV phrase register must therefore remain distinct from the broader Human Will and Salvation Reference Work.

## Original-language caution

The phrase is not represented by one single Hebrew lemma in every occurrence. Relevant forms include the Hebrew נדב word-family (including נְדָבָה, commonly indexed as H5071) and an Aramaic form in Ezra. In Ezra 7:13 and 7:16, the Aramaic root/form requires its own occurrence-level parsing. Do not infer one identical grammatical function from the English spelling.

The Hebrew noun נְדָבָה can denote voluntariness or a voluntary offering; its contextual use includes the willing disposition and the gift. It is not a technical term that automatically means the philosophical thesis “man's will is independently free in every domain.”

## Important scope warning

The KJV also uses other expressions such as “of his own voluntary will” (Leviticus 1:3), “willing heart” (Exodus 35:5), “offered freely” (Ezra 2:68), and “offered willingly” (1 Chronicles 29:6, 9). Those are not occurrences of the exact joined word **freewill**. They may be examined in the broader study, but must not be silently added to this exact-phrase count.

Likewise, “freely” is a different English form and has multiple meanings, including without cost, spontaneously, or without restraint. It requires a separate concordance if studied.
