# Investigation II.22 — Two Blind Men Receive Sight
**Primary text:** Matthew 9:27–31 (KJV)  
**Related texts:** Matthew 20:29–34; Mark 10:46–52; Luke 18:35–43; Isaiah 35:5; Matthew 11:2–6  
**Status:** Textual case study recorded; no modern diagnosis or mechanism supplied.

## Question
What does Matthew record about two blind men following Jesus, appealing to Him as Son of David, and receiving sight?

## Narrative sequence
- As Jesus departs, two blind men follow Him, crying, “Thou Son of David, have mercy on us.”
- Jesus enters a house; the men come to Him there.
- Jesus asks whether they believe He is able to do this.
- They answer, “Yea, Lord.”
- Jesus touches their eyes and says, “According to your faith be it unto you.”
- Their eyes are opened.
- Jesus strictly charges them to let no man know.
- They depart and spread His fame throughout the country.

## What the text states
Matthew identifies two blind men, their appeal to Jesus as Son of David, their affirmation of His ability, His touch and words, and the opening of their eyes. It also records their disobedience to His instruction concerning publicity.

## Witness and agency
The men follow, appeal, answer Jesus, and receive sight. Jesus asks, touches, speaks, and restores. The men then become public reporters despite His charge. Faith is present as their trust in Jesus' ability; the text does not make faith an independent source of healing.

## Canonical context
“Son of David” is a messianic designation, and the appeal for mercy connects the event with Matthew's presentation of Jesus' identity. Isaiah 35:5 and Matthew 11:2–6 provide the wider Scriptural association between the coming salvation and the blind receiving sight. Matthew 20:29–34 is another account involving blind men near Jericho; it should not be merged with this earlier episode without evidence.

## What the text does not state
Matthew does not specify whether the blindness was congenital or acquired, nor provide medical diagnosis, tests, or a physical mechanism. The passage does not establish a universal formula that reproduces healing by saying particular words.

## Triple Constraint
- **Biblical compliance — PASS:** two men, their appeal, Jesus' question and touch, restored sight, and subsequent publicity are explicit.
- **Scientific coherence — PASS for restraint; mechanism UNRESOLVED:** no clinical measurements or mechanism are supplied.
- **GUA-W consistency — PASS at governing level; technical mapping UNRESOLVED:** Christ is the acting authority; the men receive and then witness. No new architecture is proposed.

## Finding
The account joins restored sight to recognition of Jesus as the Son of David. It also records a tension between Jesus' instruction to keep the event quiet and the recipients' decision to publicize it. The witness response is not automatically identical to faithful obedience.

**They appeal to the Son of David. He opens their eyes. They leave and publish what happened.**
