# Genesis 4:13–16 — Text-First Reconstruction, Unit 13

**Status:** Text-first investigation; lexical and translation ambiguities retained; observed relationships are recorded in relation to the completed GUA-W  
**Date:** 2026-11-16  
**Scope:** KJV Genesis 4:13–16, read with Genesis 3:8–24 and 4:11–12  
**Questions:** What does Cain say about his punishment? What does he fear? What does God appoint as a sign? What does the text actually identify about the sign? What does Cain's departure mean in the narrative?

## 1. KJV base text

Genesis 4:13:
> And Cain said unto the LORD, My punishment is greater than I can bear.

Genesis 4:14:
> Behold, thou hast driven me out this day from the face of the earth; and from thy face shall I be hid; and I shall be a fugitive and a vagabond in the earth; and it shall come to pass, that every one that findeth me shall slay me.

Genesis 4:15:
> And the LORD said unto him, Therefore whosoever slayeth Cain, vengeance shall be taken on him sevenfold. And the LORD set a mark upon Cain, lest any finding him should kill him.

Genesis 4:16:
> And Cain went out from the presence of the LORD, and dwelt in the land of Nod, on the east of Eden.

## 3. Genesis 4:13 — “My punishment is greater than I can bear”

Word-level observations:
- וַיֹּאמֶר (*wayyōʾmer*) — and he said; Qal wayyiqtol, third-person masculine singular, אמר (*ʾāmar*).
- קַיִן (*Qayin*) — Cain.
- אֶל־יְהוָה (*ʾel-YHWH*) — to the LORD.
- גָּדוֹל (*gādōl*) — great, large, severe.
- עֲוֹנִי (*ʿăwōnî*) — my עָוֹן (*ʿāwōn*), first-person singular suffix.
- מִנְּשֹׂא (*min-nəśōʾ*) — from bearing / than to bear; מִן (*min*) plus Qal infinitive construct of נשׂא (*nāśāʾ*), lift, carry, bear.

### 3.1 The ambiguity of עָוֹן (*ʿāwōn*)

The noun עָוֹן can refer to:
1. iniquity or sin;
2. guilt arising from iniquity;
3. punishment or consequence for iniquity.

The immediate context has just announced consequences for Cain, so many modern translations render it “my punishment is too great to bear.” The KJV also chooses “punishment.” The Septuagint and Vulgate take the line in the direction of guilt/crime too great to be forgiven. The Hebrew expression is compact enough to sustain a translation discussion; this is not a license to claim that Cain is necessarily confessing repentance.

**Textual finding:** Cain says that his עָוֹן is great beyond bearing. The lexical range allows “iniquity/guilt” and “punishment,” while the immediate discourse favors the punishment sense adopted by the KJV and NET.

**Interpretive boundary:** The verse does not explicitly say “I repent,” “I confess,” or “forgive me.” Nor does the absence of those words prove Cain has no remorse. His statement must be read with his description of the consequences in verse 14.

## 4. Genesis 4:14 — Ground, presence, concealment, and fear

Word-level observations:
- הֵן (*hēn*) — behold, surely, look.
- גֵּרַשְׁתָּ (*gērašətā*) — you have driven out/expelled; Piel perfect, second-person masculine singular, גרש (*gāraš*).
- אֹתִי (*ʾōtî*) — me.
- הַיּוֹם (*hayyōm*) — today / this day.
- מֵעַל (*mēʿal*) — from off, away from.
- פְּנֵי (*pənê*) — face/surface of; construct form of פָּנִים (*pānîm*).
- הָאֲדָמָה (*hāʾădāmāh*) — the ground.
- וּמִפָּנֶיךָ (*ûmippāneḵā*) — and from your face/presence.
- אֶסָּתֵר (*ʾessātēr*) — I shall be hidden; Niphal imperfect, first-person singular, סתר (*sāṯar*).
- וְהָיִיתִי (*wəhāyîtî*) — and I shall be / become; היה (*hāyāh*), first-person singular.
- נָע (*nāʿ*) — fugitive/wandering, from נוע (*nûaʿ*), move/waver.
- וָנָד (*wānāḏ*) — and wandering/fugitive, from נוד (*nûḏ*), wander/be unsettled.
- בָּאָרֶץ (*bāʾāreṣ*) — in the earth/land.
- וְהָיָה (*wəhāyāh*) — and it shall happen.
- כָל־מֹצְאִי (*kol-mōṣəʾî*) — everyone who finds me; כל (*kol*) plus Qal participle of מצא (*māṣāʾ*) with first-person suffix.
- יַהַרְגֵנִי (*yahargēnî*) — will kill me; Hiphil imperfect, third-person masculine singular, רצח? No: from הרג (*hārag*), kill, with first-person suffix.

### 4.1 Cain's description of the consequence

Cain describes two related but distinct forms of displacement:
- being driven from the face/surface of the ground;
- being hidden from God's face/presence.

The phrase מֵעַל פְּנֵי הָאֲדָמָה (*mēʿal pənê hāʾădāmāh*) means from/off the face or surface of the ground. It echoes the ground language in 4:11–12. Cain then uses a different phrase, וּמִפָּנֶיךָ (*ûmippāneḵā*), “and from your face/presence.”

The two phrases should not be collapsed. The first concerns Cain's relation to the ground; the second concerns his relation to the LORD.

### 4.2 “From thy face shall I be hid”

The verb אֶסָּתֵר (*ʾessātēr*) is Niphal, expressing being hidden/concealed. Cain anticipates being hidden from God's presence. This is Cain's stated expectation; the verse does not explicitly declare that God becomes unable to see him or that divine presence is spatially limited.

Compare Genesis 3:8–10, where Adam and Eve hide themselves after hearing the LORD God. There, the humans hide; here, Cain says he will be hidden from God's face as part of the consequence he anticipates.

### 4.3 “Every one that findeth me shall slay me”

Cain fears being killed by anyone who finds him. The text records his fear; it does not identify the potential killer(s) or explain the population structure at this point. Do not use this sentence alone to settle questions about the number or identity of other people.

The verb הרג (*hārag*) is the verb for killing. It is not the same verb as the verb used for Cain's killing of Abel in 4:8? The narrative uses וַיַּהַרְגֵהוּ (*wayyahargēhû*) there, from this same root הרג. The repeated root links Cain's act with the death he now fears.

## 5. Genesis 4:15 — Divine response and the sign

Word-level observations:
- וַיֹּאמֶר (*wayyōʾmer*) — and He said.
- לוֹ (*lō*) — to him.
- לָכֵן (*lāḵēn*) — therefore / in that case; its pragmatic force in this reply is discussed by translators.
- כָּל־הֹרֵג (*kol-hōrēg*) — whoever kills; כל plus Qal active participle of הרג (*hārag*).
- קַיִן (*Qayin*) — Cain.
- שִׁבְעָתַיִם (*šiḇʿātayim*) — sevenfold / seven times.
- יֻקָּם (*yuqqām*) — vengeance shall be taken / he shall be avenged; Hofal imperfect, third-person masculine singular, נקם (*nāqam*).
- וַיָּשֶׂם (*wayyāśem*) — and He set/placed/appointed; Qal wayyiqtol, third-person masculine singular, שׂים (*śîm*).
- לְקַיִן (*lə-Qayin*) — for/to Cain; preposition לְ (*lə*) plus Cain.
- אוֹת (*ʾōṯ*) — sign, mark, token, signal.
- לְבִלְתִּי (*ləḇiltî*) — so as not to / lest.
- הַכּוֹת (*hakkōṯ*) — striking/smiting; Hiphil infinitive construct of נכה (*nāḵāh*).
- אֹתוֹ (*ʾōṯō*) — him.
- כָּל־מֹצְאוֹ (*kol-mōṣəʾō*) — anyone finding him.

### 5.1 “The LORD set a mark upon Cain”

The Hebrew is וַיָּשֶׂם יְהוָה לְקַיִן אוֹת (*wayyāśem YHWH lə-Qayin ʾōṯ*): “And the LORD set/appointed a sign for Cain.”

The preposition is לְ (*lə*, to/for), not בְּ (*bə*, in/on). English translations often say “upon Cain,” but the Hebrew construction is literally “for Cain.” The sign is associated with Cain and serves the stated purpose that anyone finding him should not strike/kill him.

The noun אוֹת (*ʾōṯ*) means sign, token, or signal. It does not itself specify a bodily brand, tattoo, facial mark, letter, or other physical form.

**What the text states:** God appoints a sign for Cain so that anyone finding him will not kill him.

**What the text does not state:** what the sign looked like, where it was located, whether it was a bodily mark, whether it was visible on Cain, or how it functioned.

The NET note explicitly acknowledges that the text does not identify the sign's nature. Jewish commentators preserve several later proposals (including a letter, an animal, or an inward strengthening), but these are interpretations, not details supplied by the verse. They should not be imported into the text-first finding.

### 5.2 Sevenfold vengeance

The phrase שִׁבְעָתַיִם יֻקָּם (*šiḇʿātayim yuqqām*) expresses sevenfold vengeance for anyone who kills Cain. The number seven can function as an intensified or complete measure; the NET note reads it as emphasizing severe punishment. The verse does not specify a literal seven-person or seven-generation mechanism.

The immediate narrative effect is protective: Cain fears being killed, and God declares a deterrent against anyone who kills him, then appoints a sign for his protection.

**Important distinction:** God's protection of Cain does not erase the judgment already pronounced in 4:11–12. Protection from being killed and removal of the ground-related judgment are not stated as the same thing.

## 6. Genesis 4:16 — Departure and Nod

Word-level observations:
- וַיֵּצֵא (*wayyēṣēʾ*) — and he went out; Qal wayyiqtol, third-person masculine singular, יצא (*yāṣāʾ*).
- קַיִן (*Qayin*) — Cain.
- מִלִּפְנֵי (*millipnê*) — from before / from the presence of.
- יְהוָה (*YHWH*) — the LORD.
- וַיֵּשֶׁב (*wayyēšeḇ*) — and he dwelt/settled; Qal wayyiqtol, third-person masculine singular, ישב (*yāšaḇ*).
- בְּאֶרֶץ־נוֹד (*bəʾereṣ-Nōḏ*) — in the land of Nod.
- קִדְמַת־עֵדֶן (*qiḏmaṯ-ʿĒḏen*) — east of / toward the east of Eden.

### 6.1 “Went out from the presence of the LORD”

The phrase מִלִּפְנֵי יְהוָה (*millipnê YHWH*) means from before/from the presence of the LORD. The narrative says Cain departed from that presence. It does not explain the geography or mechanics of divine presence.

Compare Genesis 3:8–24: Adam and Eve are driven from the Garden, and the way to the tree of life is guarded. Cain now goes out from before the LORD and dwells east of Eden. These are related narrative movements, but the text does not say Cain is expelled from precisely the same place as Adam and Eve.

### 6.2 “The land of Nod”

נוֹד (*Nōḏ*) is associated by sound and likely wordplay with נוּד (*nûḏ*), to wander or be unsettled. The preceding description of Cain as נָע וָנָד (*nāʿ wā nāḏ*, 4:12, 14) makes the association especially salient.

The Septuagint transliterates Nod as a place name (Naid); the Vulgate renders the phrase by sense as dwelling as a fugitive. The Hebrew text presents “land of Nod.” Whether Nod is a proper geographical name, a literary name evoking wandering, or both should remain open.

The text locates it קִדְמַת־עֵדֶן (*qiḏmaṯ-ʿĒḏen*), east of Eden.

## 7. Sequence across Genesis 4:11–16

| Verse | Narrative event |
|---|---|
| 4:11–12 | God announces ground-related judgment and Cain's wandering condition. |
| 4:13 | Cain says his עָוֹן is too great to bear. |
| 4:14 | Cain describes expulsion, concealment from God's face, wandering, and fear of being killed. |
| 4:15 | God declares sevenfold vengeance against anyone who kills Cain and appoints a sign for his protection. |
| 4:16 | Cain goes out from the presence of the LORD and dwells in Nod, east of Eden. |

This sequence distinguishes:
- announced judgment;
- Cain's assessment of the judgment;
- Cain's anticipation/fear;
- God's protective response;
- Cain's departure and settlement.

## 8. Relevance to U-006 — Will, perception, fear, and response

Genesis 4:13–16 does not provide a technical definition of will. It does provide evidence relevant to several components that must not be conflated:

1. **Assessment:** Cain describes his עָוֹן as too great to bear.
2. **Perception/anticipation:** Cain anticipates being hidden from God's face and killed by anyone who finds him.
3. **Fear:** Fear is expressed through his prediction of being killed; the noun “fear” is not used in these verses, so do not claim an explicit Hebrew emotion-word here.
4. **Divine response:** God appoints a sign and declares protection against anyone killing Cain.
5. **Action:** Cain goes out and dwells in Nod.

Cain's account of his situation is not identical to the whole divine judgment. His fear is his stated expectation; God's response changes the danger of being killed but does not say the earlier ground judgment is cancelled.

**U-006 remains UNRESOLVED.**

## 9. Relevance to U-007 — Judgment, protection, and authority

The passage distinguishes God's announced judgment from Cain's assessment of it and from God's later protective declaration. Cain does not determine the outcome by describing it; God speaks and appoints the sign.

The sign is not a humanly invented counter-authority. It is an action attributed in the text to the LORD. The passage does not state that Cain receives authority to establish the standard of good and evil.

**U-007 status: RESOLVED.** This passage does not reopen the settled conclusion: knowledge, evaluation, and human action do not confer God's authority or transfer authority to establish the standard of good and evil. Any remaining question about the precise nature of the knowledge in Genesis 3:22 is separate.

## 10. Preliminary Triple Constraint review

### Biblical compliance — PARTIAL

The reconstruction follows the KJV and Masoretic Hebrew while recording the ambiguity of עָוֹן in 4:13 and the unspecified nature of the sign in 4:15. The construction לְקַיִן is noted without insisting that English “upon” is impossible.

### Scientific coherence — theological discipline

No scientific mechanism or empirical claim is proposed. The sign is not assigned a physical mechanism or form.

### GUA-W consistency — PARTIAL

The record distinguishes judgment, human assessment, anticipated consequence, divine protection, and subsequent action. No new architectural node or universal law is proposed.

**Overall status:** INITIAL TEXTUAL FINDINGS; U-006 and U-007 remain unresolved.

## 11. Unresolved questions retained

1. In Genesis 4:13, is עָוֹן (*ʿāwōn*) best rendered in context as iniquity, guilt, or punishment? What does each translation imply and not imply?
2. Does Cain's statement express complaint, fear, recognition of guilt, or some combination? What does the text actually establish?
3. Does “from the face of the ground” in 4:14 restate or intensify the judgment in 4:11–12?
4. How should Cain's “from thy face shall I be hid” be compared with Adam and Eve hiding in Genesis 3:8–10?
5. What is the exact function of the Niphal אֶסָּתֵר (*ʾessātēr*) in Cain's statement?
6. Is נוֹד (*Nōḏ*) primarily a geographical place-name, a literary wordplay on wandering, or both?
7. What was the sign appointed for Cain? The text does not disclose its physical form; later interpretations must remain separately labelled.
8. Does לְקַיִן (*lə-Qayin*, “for Cain”) affect how the sign should be described in English? How should this be balanced against idiomatic translation?
9. What is the force of “sevenfold” in 4:15: literal multiplication, conventional intensification, or a legal deterrent formula?
10. How does God's protection of Cain coexist with the judgment already announced in 4:11–12?
11. How do Cain's departure from the presence of the LORD and his settlement east of Eden relate to Adam and Eve's expulsion from the Garden?
12. What does the sequence contribute to U-006 and U-007 without turning it into a definition or universal law?
