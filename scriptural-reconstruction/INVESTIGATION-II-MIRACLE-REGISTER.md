# Investigation II — Master Miracle and Sign Register

**Purpose:** Complete event-by-event coverage of the miracles and miraculous events of and surrounding Jesus, plus a separately identified register of signs performed by His servants in His name. This register is a coverage control: it prevents familiar examples from crowding out less familiar accounts and keeps parallel Gospel reports connected without assuming they are always identical events.

**Primary text:** KJV.  
**Governing test:** Triple Constraint Law — Biblical compliance, scientific coherence, GUA-W consistency.  
**Architecture boundary:** GUA-W is already complete. This investigation identifies supported relationships; it does not add stages or redesign the architecture. Jesus Christ is not reduced to an architectural component.

## J. Required method for every dedicated case study

Each event study must include:

1. **Primary KJV text and parallel accounts** — quote or accurately summarize the relevant passage; note meaningful differences.
2. **Narrative sequence** — setting, need/occasion, word or action, result, witness, and stated outcome.
3. **Textual vocabulary** — examine relevant Hebrew/Greek terms only where they clarify the text; do not overstate word studies.
4. **What Scripture states / does not state** — keep explicit claims distinct from inference.
5. **Canonical context** — relevant Old and New Testament cross-references, including the identity of Christ where appropriate.
6. **Witness and agency** — who acts, who obeys, who sees, who reports, who understands, and who does not.
7. **Scientific/historical assessment** — appropriate to the claim; no invented mechanisms or modern data absent from the record.
8. **Triple Constraint disposition** — separate finding for Biblical compliance, scientific coherence, and GUA-W consistency.
9. **Unresolved questions** — preserve uncertainty honestly.
10. **Provisional conclusion** — no forced architectural mapping and no new GUA-W components.

## K. Coverage and counting cautions

- Parallel Gospel accounts should be cross-referenced and compared, not automatically counted as distinct miracles.
- A single Gospel may describe multiple healings in a summary without naming every individual.
- Some events are signs, prophecy, angelic appearances, providential direction, judgment, or resurrection. Do not call every extraordinary event a “healing miracle.”
- The Gospels do not all use the same selection, sequence, or terminology. Preserve each writer's presentation.
- “All” coverage means all identifiable narrated events in the defined scope, together with grouped summaries and explicit acknowledgment of non-exhaustive statements (e.g., John 20:30). It does not mean pretending the Gospels record every work Jesus ever performed.
