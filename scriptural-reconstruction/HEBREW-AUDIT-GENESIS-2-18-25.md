## 1. Genesis 2:18 — The stated condition and God's declared action

KJV:

> And the LORD God said, It is not good that the man should be alone; I will make him an help meet for him.

### 1.1 לֹא־טוֹב — *lo-tov*, “not good”

The text uses the ordinary adjective טוֹב (*tov*, good) with the negative לֹא (*lo*, not). This is the first “not good” declaration in the creation account, in contrast with the repeated “good” evaluations in Genesis 1.

**Textual finding:** The condition identified as “not good” is the man's being alone. The verse does not say that the man himself is evil or defective.

**Contextual question:** Genesis 1:28 has already stated the blessing and commission to be fruitful, multiply, fill the earth, and exercise dominion. The NET note proposes that aloneness prevents the man from fulfilling the creation design. This is a contextual interpretation worth testing against the full passage, not a lexical definition of *tov*.

### 1.2 עֵזֶר — *‘ezer*, “help/helper”

The noun עֵזֶר (*‘ezer*) means help or helper. It is also used of God as the help of His people in other biblical passages (for example, Psalm 33:20; 70:5 KJV numbering varies; 121:1–2).

**Finding:** The noun itself does not encode inferiority or servitude. Its force must be determined by context and usage.

**Limit:** The word's use for God elsewhere does not by itself settle every aspect of the woman's role in Genesis 2. It does rule out treating “helper” as automatically meaning a subordinate assistant.

### 1.3 כְּנֶגְדּוֹ — *kenegdo*, “meet for him / corresponding to him”

This expression combines the preposition כְּ (*ke*, like/as), נֶגֶד (*neged*, in front of/opposite), and the suffix וֹ (*-o*, him). The NET note glosses the expression as “according to the opposite of him” and understands it as correspondence, matching, or suitability.

**Lexical reading:** “A help corresponding to him” captures the relational sense more transparently than a modern reading of “helpmeet” as a job title.

**Important caution:** The phrase should not be reduced to “opposite” in the sense of adversarial conflict, nor should it be expanded into a complete theory of male/female roles from the phrase alone.

**GUA-W recognition:** The phrase describes a corresponding counterpart, not a duplicate or an unrelated being. This witnesses to relationship and correspondence within the completed Map; it is not a lexical definition of “joining” or “symmetry.”

## 2. Genesis 2:19–20 — Animals brought to the man and named

KJV (selected wording):

> And out of the ground the LORD God formed every beast of the field, and every fowl of the air; and brought them unto Adam to see what he would call them...

### 2.1 יָצַר — *yatsar*, “formed”

The verb is יָצַר (*yatsar*), the same root used in Genesis 2:7 for God's forming of the man from the dust of the ground.

**Finding:** The text uses the same formation verb for the man and for the animals in this passage. The objects and narrative roles differ, but the repeated root is significant.

**Caution:** Do not claim that the shared root means the man and animals are identical in status or nature. Nor does the root itself establish a technical GUA-W operation.

### 2.2 בּוֹ — *bo*, “brought”

The text says God brought the animals to the man. God is the agent of this presentation.

**Finding:** The man does not go out and independently assemble the animals; God brings them to him in the narrative.

### 2.3 קָרָא — *qara’*, “called/named”

The verb קָרָא (*qara’*) means call, proclaim, or name, depending on context. The man gives names to the animals.

**Finding:** The text assigns the naming action to the man. This is an act of recognition/classification within the narrative.

**Authority distinction:** The man names the animals presented to him; he does not create them. The text keeps God's forming and bringing distinct from the man's naming.

### 2.4 “There was not found an help meet for him”

The passive construction says that no corresponding help was found for the man among the animals.

**Finding:** The animals do not supply the correspondence declared necessary in verse 18. The passage does not say they are worthless; it says none corresponds to the man as the needed counterpart.

**GUA-W recognition:** The narrative distinguishes the creatures' presentation, Adam's naming/recognition, and the absence of a corresponding help among them. Naming does not create correspondence or fulfill the need. This is a specific Scriptural distinction, not a proposed universal matching algorithm.

### 2.5 Narrative order and Genesis 1

Genesis 2:19 narrates the forming of animals after the formation of the man in the literary sequence of this chapter. Genesis 1 places the creation of land animals before humankind. Some translations and commentators understand the verb in Genesis 2:19 as “had formed,” treating the action as prior to the man's formation; others retain the narrative sequence as written.

**Textual boundary:** Preserve the KJV wording and the literary sequence of Genesis 2:19. The precise chronological relation to Genesis 1 is an interpretive question involving grammar and discourse; do not silently insert “had,” and do not make this question a condition of the completed Map.

## 3. Genesis 2:21–22 — The woman formed and brought

KJV (selected wording):

> And the LORD God caused a deep sleep to fall upon Adam, and he slept: and he took one of his ribs, and closed up the flesh instead thereof;  
> And the rib, which the LORD God had taken from man, made he a woman, and brought her unto the man.

### 3.1 תַּרְדֵּמָה — *tardemah*, “deep sleep”

The noun תַּרְדֵּמָה (*tardemah*) denotes a deep sleep or divinely induced sleep in this context. The text attributes its coming upon the man to God.

**Finding:** The man is passive during the described operation. The action is initiated by God; the man does not perform the formation of the woman.

### 3.2 צֵלָע — *tsela‘*, “rib/side”

The noun צֵלָע (*tsela‘*) can mean side, flank, or rib, depending on context. Elsewhere it is used for the side of a structure (for example, parts of the tabernacle). In Genesis 2:21–22, “rib” is the traditional KJV rendering.

**Finding:** The KJV wording “rib” is retained. The broader lexical range “side” should be noted because it is relevant to translation discussion.

**Limit:** The lexical range alone does not prove whether the text intends one anatomical rib, a side portion, or a broader description. Do not use the alternative “side” to overwrite the KJV; record it as a lexical question.

### 3.3 סָגַר — *sagar*, “closed”

The verb סָגַר (*sagar*) means shut or close. God closes the flesh at the place described.

**Finding:** The verse includes both taking and closing, with God as the agent.

### 3.4 בָּנָה — *banah*, “built”

Genesis 2:22 uses בָּנָה (*banah*), whose ordinary sense is build or construct. The KJV renders it “made”: “made he a woman.”

**Important lexical observation:** The Hebrew verb here is not *yatsar* (formed), used in Genesis 2:7 and 2:19. It is *banah* (built).

**Caution:** The use of *banah* is textually important, but it does not authorize an elaborate architectural metaphor by itself. It is a concrete verb in the narrative. Any GUA-W comparison with structure/building must be labeled as analogy and tested elsewhere.

### 3.5 וַיְבִאֶהָ — *vayvi’eha*, “and brought her”

The verb is from בּוֹא (*bo’*, come/bring), in a causative form: God brought her to the man.

**Finding:** God forms/builds the woman and brings her to the man. The text does not describe the man creating, selecting, or retrieving her.

**Narrative sequence:** God causes sleep → takes the *tsela‘* → closes the flesh → builds the woman → brings her to the man.

This sequence should be retained without collapsing its actions into one generic “creation” label.

## 4. Genesis 2:23 — The man's recognition and naming

KJV:

> And Adam said, This is now bone of my bones, and flesh of my flesh: she shall be called Woman, because she was taken out of Man.

### 4.1 עֶצֶם — *‘etsem*, “bone”

The man identifies the woman as “bone of my bones.” The phrase expresses kinship or shared substance in the narrative, not a claim that the woman is merely an object.

### 4.2 בָּשָׂר — *basar*, “flesh”

“Flesh of my flesh” parallels “bone of my bones.” The same noun *basar* will recur in “one flesh” in verse 24.

**Finding:** Verse 23 supplies an immediate textual bridge to verse 24: the man's recognition of shared bone and flesh precedes the narrator's statement about one flesh.

### 4.3 אִישׁ — *’ish*, “man”

### 4.4 אִשָּׁה — *’ishshah*, “woman/wife”

The verse presents a wordplay between *’ish* (man) and *’ishshah* (woman). It is a literary association in the Hebrew text.

**Caution:** The wordplay should not be treated as a complete historical-linguistic derivation of the words. It functions in the narrative as the man's recognition and naming of the woman in relation to himself.

### 4.5 The man's speech

The man responds after God brings the woman to him. The speech identifies correspondence and kinship: “bone of my bones, and flesh of my flesh.”

**Textual distinction:** God performs the formation and presentation; the man speaks and names. These are distinct agents and actions.

**GUA-W recognition:** The man's naming registers recognition of what God has brought; it does not author what God made. This is a narrative witness to the Author/Witness distinction, not a lexical definition of the word “witness.”

## 5. Genesis 2:24 — Leaving, cleaving, and one flesh

KJV:

> Therefore shall a man leave his father and his mother, and shall cleave unto his wife: and they shall be one flesh.

The verse shifts from the specific first man and woman to a general statement about the man/woman marriage relation.

### 5.1 עָזַב — *‘azav*, “leave”

The verb עָזַב (*‘azav*) means leave, forsake, or abandon. In this context, “leave” describes the man's changed primary family attachment when he forms a marriage union.

**Caution:** It does not necessarily mean that a married man literally abandons or ceases all responsibility toward his parents. The NET notes understand the statement as describing a change in relational priority within the ancient family context.

### 5.2 דָּבַק — *davaq*, “cleave”

The verb דָּבַק (*davaq*) means cling, stick, or hold fast. It is used elsewhere for close attachment, including Ruth's attachment to Naomi (Ruth 1:14).

**Finding:** The KJV “cleave” conveys a strong attachment, not a casual association.

**Limit:** The verb alone does not specify every legal, emotional, sexual, or social dimension of marriage. Those are developed in wider Scriptural contexts.

### 5.3 בָּשָׂר אֶחָד — *basar ’echad*, “one flesh”

The phrase means “one flesh.” *Basar* is flesh; *’echad* is one. The phrase follows the man's declaration in verse 23 that the woman is bone of his bones and flesh of his flesh.

The NET notes argue that “one flesh” refers not merely to sexual union but also to the formation of a new family/kinship unit. This is a contextual interpretation supported by the relationship between verses 23 and 24 and by other biblical uses of “bone and flesh” as kinship language.

**Finding:** The text describes a new unity arising through the marriage relation. It should not be reduced to physical contact alone.

**Caution:** Do not make “one flesh” mean that the two persons cease to be distinct persons. The text describes union, not the erasure of their personal distinction.

### 5.4 “Therefore”

The opening עַל־כֵּן (*‘al-ken*) means “therefore” or “for this reason.” It links the general marriage statement to the preceding account.

**Finding:** Verse 24 explicitly grounds the marriage pattern in the narrated formation and recognition of the woman in verses 21–23.

## 6. Genesis 2:25 — Nakedness and absence of shame

KJV:

> And they were both naked, the man and his wife, and were not ashamed.

### 6.1 עֲרוּמִּים — *‘arummim*, “naked”

The adjective describes both the man and his wife as naked. The verse emphasizes their shared condition.

### 6.2 לֹא יִתְבֹּשָׁשׁוּ — *lo yitboshashu*, “were not ashamed”

The verb is from בּוֹשׁ (*bosh*), be ashamed. The text says they were not ashamed.

**Finding:** The passage closes with nakedness and absence of shame. This becomes significant in Genesis 3:7–10, where the awareness of nakedness and hiding enter the narrative.

**Caution:** Do not import the post-Fall meaning of shame into Genesis 2:25. The verse itself describes an unashamed condition before the events of Genesis 3.

## 7. Sequence and agency across Genesis 2:18–25

| Verse | Agent / subject | Action or statement |
|---|---|---|
| 2:18 | God | Declares man's aloneness not good; promises a corresponding help |
| 2:19 | God | Forms animals and brings them to the man |
| 2:19–20 | Man | Names the animals; no corresponding help is found among them |
| 2:21 | God | Causes deep sleep; takes the *tsela‘*; closes the flesh |
| 2:22 | God | Builds the woman and brings her to the man |
| 2:23 | Man | Recognizes shared bone/flesh and names her Woman |
| 2:24 | Narrator | States the continuing marriage pattern: leave, cleave, become one flesh |
| 2:25 | Man and wife | Are naked and not ashamed |

### What this sequence clearly distinguishes

- God's declaration of need is not the man's initiative.
- God forms and brings; the man does not author the woman's existence.
- The man names the animals and then recognizes/names the woman.
- The woman's correspondence is not found among the animals.
- The narrator links the first union to a continuing marriage pattern.
- Union does not erase the distinction between man and woman in the passage.

## 10. Hebrew-language witness summary

Genesis 2:18–25 distinguishes God's declaration, God's forming and bringing, the man's naming and recognition, and the narrator's description of marriage as a lasting union. The Hebrew terms are not interchangeable:

- *‘Ezer kenegdo*: help corresponding to the man.
- *Yatsar*: form (used of the man and animals in this chapter).
- *Bo’*: bring.
- *Qara’*: call/name.
- *Tardemah*: deep sleep.
- *Tsela‘*: rib/side.
- *Sagar*: close.
- *Banah*: build.
- *Davaq*: cling/hold fast.
- *Basar ’echad*: one flesh.
- *Bosh*: be ashamed.

The central Scriptural distinction is one of **agency and registration**: God forms and brings; the man names and recognizes; the narrator describes the joining. These actions witness to the established Author/Word/Creation/Witness hierarchy and Joining Primacy relationship within the completed GUA-W. Genesis 2:18–25 is a witness to the Map, not a source from which the Map is constructed.

The completed GUA-W remains unchanged. This audit records its Scriptural witness and preserves the Hebrew distinctions.
