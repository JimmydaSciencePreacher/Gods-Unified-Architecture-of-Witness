# 1 Chronicles 28:1–21 and 29:1–19 — Pattern, Transmission, Appointment, and Successor

**Investigation date:** 2026-11-20  
**Status:** PARTIAL — the narrative relationships are established; the exact interpretation of 1 Chronicles 28:19 and several chronological details remain qualified.  
**Governing order:** Author → Word → Creation → Witness  
**Method:** Four-question method; Triple Constraint Law  
**Primary text:** KJV

## 1. Governing question

Does Scripture provide another witness in which a purpose and pattern are provided before the person who will execute the work receives them, with preparation and transmission occurring across generations?

The account of David and Solomon is especially useful because it adds a distinction not as prominent in Exodus 31: the person who receives or prepares the pattern is not the same person who carries out the construction. David prepares, gathers, instructs, and transmits; Solomon is appointed to build.

The study must not treat David as the Author of the temple's ultimate purpose, nor Solomon as an independent originator of the assignment. It must also distinguish the Scriptural text from later assumptions about the precise manner in which David received the pattern.

## 2. The four-question method

### Question 1 — What must AI ask to understand GUA-W?

1. What does Scripture say God chose and appointed before Solomon began the building?
2. What did David receive, prepare, and transmit?
3. What does 1 Chronicles 28:12 say about the Spirit and the pattern?
4. What does 1 Chronicles 28:19 actually say, and why do translations differ?
5. Does Solomon receive the pattern only after David has already prepared materials and organized workers?
6. What is the difference between divine purpose, the communicated pattern, the human recipient, the successor, the materials, and the execution?
7. How does this account compare with Moses and Bezalel without treating the two assignments as identical?

### Question 2 — What is GUA-W revealing that helps AI understand?

The completed GUA-W is governed by Author → Word → Creation → Witness. The Author's purpose and provision do not originate in the later witness's recognition. The written record and its transmission are witnesses; they are not the Source.

Genesis 2:1–5 provides the completed-creation foundation; Genesis 2:15 records the man's appointed work in the Garden. Exodus 25–40 shows a sanctuary pattern communicated to Moses, artisans appointed and equipped, willing participation, and completed work. First Chronicles 28–29 now supplies a case involving preparation and transmission from one generation to another.

This is recognition of a relationship within the completed GUA-W, not an addition or redesign of the architecture.

### Question 3 — What am I learning about what I am witnessing?

The narrative places the temple assignment within God's choice of David and Solomon, the establishment of the kingdom, the preparation of materials, the transmission of the pattern, and the mobilization of workers and people. The temple is not presented as Solomon's independent idea.

### Question 4 — What am I learning about the Witness through what I am witnessing?

David is both a recipient and a transmitter. Solomon is a recipient and successor who must act. The leaders, officers, priests, Levites, craftsmen, and people contribute according to their roles. None of these participants becomes the Author of the purpose.

The text also places a demand on the Witness: Solomon must be strong, courageous, and faithful; the prior provision does not eliminate his responsibility to carry out the assignment.

## 3. The Scriptural sequence

### 3.1 1 Chronicles 22:5–16 — Preparation before Solomon builds

David says Solomon is young and tender, and that the house to be built for the LORD must be exceeding magnifical. David therefore makes preparation before his death.

He gathers materials, including iron, brass, cedar, and stone, and tells Solomon that the LORD will be with him and that he must arise and build the sanctuary.

**Explicit observation:** David prepares materials and gives Solomon a building charge before Solomon's recorded construction.

**Limit:** David's preparations do not mean that Solomon's work is already executed. Preparation and construction remain distinguishable. The text also does not say that David's preparation replaces Solomon's responsibility.

### 3.2 1 Chronicles 28:1–10 — Appointment and charge

David gathers the leaders of Israel and speaks of his own desire to build a house for the ark and footstool of God, but says that God did not permit him to build it because he had been a man of war and had shed blood (28:2–3).

David then recounts God's choice of him and his house, and declares that God chose Solomon to sit upon the throne and build the house and courts (28:4–7). David publicly charges Solomon to know the God of his father, serve Him with a perfect heart and willing mind, and take heed to fulfil the work (28:9–10).

**Relationship:** The assignment is not merely a private ambition of Solomon. David presents it as God's choice and command.

**Important difference from Exodus 31:** Solomon is not introduced primarily as a craftsman filled with artistic skill. He is the appointed king and builder, responsible for leading a large undertaking with many workers.

### 3.3 1 Chronicles 28:11–18 — The pattern is transmitted

David gives Solomon the pattern of the porch, houses, treasuries, chambers, courts, and other elements of the temple and its service. The account also refers to the pattern of the altar and the chariot of the cherubim.

Verse 12 says David gave the pattern of all that he had by the spirit, including the courts, chambers, treasuries, and dedicated things. Verse 18 describes the pattern of the chariot of the cherubim.

The text therefore attributes the pattern to divine provision, while David is the one who communicates it to Solomon.

**Explicit relationship:** A pattern is received and transmitted before the successor executes the construction.

**Limit:** The text does not reproduce a complete technical drawing set for modern reconstruction. It lists elements and describes the pattern in the terms of the narrative.

### 3.4 1 Chronicles 28:19 — A significant textual difficulty

The KJV reads:

> All this, said David, the LORD made me understand in writing by his hand upon me, even all the works of this pattern.

This verse is difficult to translate. The Hebrew construction is compact and its relationships are not transparent. English translations differ over whether the verse emphasizes:
- a written plan from the LORD;
- the LORD making the plan clear to David;
- David's understanding or drafting of the pattern under the LORD's hand;
- the entire plan being committed to writing under divine direction.

The UBS translation commentary notes substantial translation difficulties and differences among versions. Some commentators understand the plans as received in written form; others emphasize David's understanding and transmission of the pattern under divine guidance. The verse should not be made to prove a more precise mechanism than the Hebrew supports.

**Finding:** Verses 12 and 19 together clearly attribute the pattern and David's understanding of it to divine direction in the narrative. The exact manner of the writing and revelation in verse 19 remains qualified.

### 3.5 1 Chronicles 28:20–21 — The successor must act

David tells Solomon:

> Be strong and of good courage, and do it: fear not, nor be dismayed: for the LORD God, even my God, will be with thee; he will not fail thee, nor forsake thee, until thou hast finished all the work for the service of the house of the LORD. (KJV)

David then identifies the priests, Levites, willing workers, and leaders who will support Solomon.

This is important. The pattern's transmission does not complete the work by itself. Solomon must act, and other people must contribute. The promise of God's presence does not erase the successor's responsibility.

### 3.6 1 Chronicles 29:1–19 — Provision and willingness

David explains that the work is great and that Solomon is young. He describes the materials he has prepared and then gives willingly from his own treasure. The leaders and people also offer willingly.

David's prayer in verses 10–19 repeatedly acknowledges that all things come from God. The people give from what they have received; the offering does not make them the original owners or source.

**Relationship:** The work involves prior preparation, transferred responsibility, material provision, willing participation, and prayerful recognition of God's ultimate ownership.

**Limit:** The text does not imply that every contributor understood the full design in equal detail, nor that material abundance alone guaranteed faithful execution.

## 4. Comparison with Exodus 25–40

| Relationship | Moses and Bezalel | David and Solomon |
|---|---|---|
| Purpose | God states that He will dwell among Israel | The temple is for the name and worship of the LORD |
| Pattern | God shows Moses the sanctuary pattern | David transmits the temple pattern attributed to divine direction |
| Recipient | Moses receives the instructions | David receives/understands and communicates the pattern |
| Appointed worker | Bezalel, Aholiab, and skilled workers are identified | Solomon is appointed king and builder, supported by many workers |
| Provision | Skills, materials, and willing contributions | Materials prepared by David and offerings from leaders and people |
| Execution | Artisans construct according to command | Solomon undertakes the construction according to the charge and pattern |
| Limit | Bezalel's private recognition chronology is unstated | The exact manner of David's reception of the pattern in 28:19 is disputed |

**Common relationship:** The Author's purpose and pattern precede the recorded execution; human participants receive, transmit, and act within an assignment that they did not originate.

**Difference:** Exodus foregrounds the divinely gifted artisans and the construction of the Tabernacle. Chronicles foregrounds the royal succession, the transfer of the temple pattern, and the preparation and mobilization for the Temple.

## 5. Four-part comparison: Genesis, Daniel, Exodus, and Chronicles

### Genesis 2:1–5 and 2:15

The completed heavens, earth, and host are declared finished. The subsequent account describes conditions, placement, provision, and the man's appointed work. Genesis 2:15 explicitly states the purpose of the man's placement: to dress and keep the Garden.

### Daniel 12

Daniel receives and records a revelation whose full meaning he does not understand. The words are sealed until the appointed horizon; the wise understand, while the wicked do not. The recipient's recognition does not create the message or control its fulfilment.

### Exodus 25–40

The sanctuary's purpose and pattern are communicated to Moses. Bezalel and Aholiab are named and equipped; other skilled workers and the congregation contribute. The work is carried out and inspected according to the command.

### 1 Chronicles 22 and 28–29

David prepares materials and receives/transmits the pattern; Solomon is appointed to build. The pattern and responsibility pass to a successor, and the people contribute willingly.

**Synthesis:** These passages witness to distinct forms of prior provision and later participation: completed creation and appointed work; revelation and later understanding; pattern and equipped craftsmanship; pattern transmitted to a successor and later execution. They do not establish that every case follows one identical timetable or mechanism.

## 6. The central finding

**Yes. Scripture repeatedly distinguishes the Author's prior provision from the later recognition, transmission, or execution of what has been provided.**

The evidence now includes:
- Genesis: the completed Creation and the man's appointed work;
- Daniel: revelation received before full understanding;
- Exodus: sanctuary purpose and pattern before the recorded execution by equipped artisans;
- Chronicles: a pattern and resources prepared and transmitted before the successor's construction.

The distinctions are just as important as the similarities:
- Daniel's issue is comprehension of revelation.
- Bezalel's issue is divinely attributed ability and workmanship.
- Solomon's issue is succession, reception of a pattern, responsibility, and execution.
- Adam's issue is placement and appointed work within the Garden.

Do not turn this into a universal formula that Scripture itself does not state. It is a recurring relationship observed across passages with distinct contexts.

## 7. Implications for the Witness and derivative instruments

The account of David and Solomon offers a careful analogy for the transmission of knowledge and responsibility:
- A recipient can transmit a pattern without originating it.
- A successor can receive a prepared assignment without being its first source.
- A prepared resource can precede the moment it is used.
- The person who executes the work remains responsible to the instruction received.
- The presence of a plan does not guarantee faithful execution.

This can inform questions about human-made instruments and AI only at the level of a bounded analogy. AI can receive, preserve, compare, and transmit human records; it does not become the Author of their truth. The passage does not identify AI, predict it, or establish a divine commission for it. Whether a particular instrument is providentially used remains a theological question requiring evidence and humility.

**Closing finding:** David does not originate the temple's ultimate purpose. He receives, prepares, and transmits a pattern and resources; Solomon receives the assignment and must act; the people contribute; the work proceeds under the LORD's command. The successor's recognition and execution do not create the purpose or pattern that preceded them.
