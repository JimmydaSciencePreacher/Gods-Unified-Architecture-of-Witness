# Investigation II.11 — The Widow's Son at Nain
**Primary text:** Luke 7:11–17 (KJV)  
**Related texts:** 1 Kings 17:17–24; 2 Kings 4:18–37; Luke 8:40–56; John 11:1–44; John 1:1–3, 14  
**Status:** Text-first case study recorded; physical mechanism and modern medical details not supplied.

## Question
What does Luke report when Jesus encounters a funeral procession, has compassion on a widowed mother, and commands her dead son to arise?

## Narrative sequence
1. Jesus comes to a city called Nain, accompanied by disciples and a great multitude.
2. As He approaches the city gate, a dead man is being carried out, the only son of his mother; she is a widow.
3. A considerable company from the city is with her.
4. The Lord sees her, has compassion on her, and says, “Weep not.”
5. He touches the bier; those carrying it stand still.
6. Jesus says, “Young man, I say unto thee, Arise.”
7. The dead man sits up and begins to speak; Jesus delivers him to his mother.
8. Fear comes upon all; they glorify God, saying a great prophet has risen among them and God has visited His people.
9. The report spreads through Judaea and the surrounding region.

## What the text states
Luke explicitly identifies the man as dead, describes Jesus' compassion, records His direct command, and reports the man's return to life and speech. Jesus gives him back to his mother. The witnesses interpret the event as a visitation of God and speak of a great prophet.

## Witness and agency
Jesus sees and has compassion; He speaks; the dead man rises. The bearers stop; the crowd witnesses; the mother receives her son. No human participant is represented as the source of the restoration.

## Canonical context
The raising of the widow's son by Elijah (1 Kings 17:17–24) and the Shunammite's son by Elisha (2 Kings 4:18–37) are important Scriptural comparisons. Luke's crowd calls Jesus a great prophet and says God has visited His people, language that resonates with prophetic history. Yet the accounts differ: Elijah and Elisha pray and act in their prophetic ministries; Luke presents Jesus directly commanding the dead man. The comparison must not flatten Jesus into merely another prophet. John 1:1–3, 14 and Hebrews 1:1–3 provide the wider canonical identity context.

## What the text does not state
Luke gives no modern cause of death, interval since death, clinical observations, or physiological mechanism. The text does not state that the man had been dead a particular number of hours or days. Do not add such details.

## Finding
This event is distinguished by Jesus' initiative. The widow does not first petition Him in the recorded account. He sees her, has compassion, and acts. The result is not only the restoration of a life but the return of the son to his mother and public testimony that God has visited His people.

**The Lord sees. The Lord has compassion. The Word commands—and the son lives.**
