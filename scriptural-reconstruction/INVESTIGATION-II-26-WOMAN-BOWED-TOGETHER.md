# Investigation II.26 — The Woman Bowed Together
**Primary text:** Luke 13:10–17 (KJV)  
**Related texts:** Luke 4:16–21; Luke 6:6–11; Luke 14:1–6; John 8:31–36  
**Status:** Text-first case study recorded; diagnosis and physical mechanism not supplied.

## Question
What does Luke record when Jesus sees a woman who has been bowed together for eighteen years, calls her, and looses her from her infirmity on the sabbath?

## Narrative sequence
1. Jesus teaches in one of the synagogues on the sabbath.
2. A woman is present who has a spirit of infirmity eighteen years; she is bowed together and cannot lift herself up.
3. Jesus sees her, calls her to Him, and says, “Woman, thou art loosed from thine infirmity.”
4. He lays His hands on her; immediately she is made straight and glorifies God.
5. The ruler of the synagogue answers with indignation because Jesus healed on the sabbath, telling the people to come for healing on other days.
6. Jesus calls him a hypocrite and reasons that people loose an ox or ass to water on the sabbath; should not this daughter of Abraham, whom Satan has bound eighteen years, be loosed from this bond on the sabbath?
7. His adversaries are ashamed; the people rejoice for the glorious things done by Him.

## What the text states
The duration, visible posture, Jesus' initiative, declaration, touch, immediate straightening, and glorification of God are explicit. Jesus interprets the woman's condition as bondage by Satan and argues that her release is fitting on the sabbath.

## Witness and agency
Jesus sees, calls, speaks, touches, and interprets the bondage. The woman receives restoration and glorifies God. The synagogue ruler objects; Jesus answers; the people respond with joy. The conflict concerns the meaning of sabbath release, not uncertainty about the woman's changed condition.

## Canonical context
The language of loosing and bondage connects with Jesus' wider ministry of release (Luke 4:18). The comparison with animals being loosed for water is Jesus' own argument. The woman is called a “daughter of Abraham,” a significant covenant designation. Do not reduce the passage to a generic posture correction or a general medical claim.

## What the text does not state
Luke does not provide an anatomical diagnosis, imaging, or a modern clinical category. The text gives eighteen years and the observable limitation but no physiological mechanism. Do not infer a specific spinal disease.

## Triple Constraint
- **Biblical compliance — PASS:** duration, condition, action, immediate result, sabbath dispute, and Jesus' explanation are explicit.
- **Scientific coherence — PASS for restraint; diagnosis/mechanism UNRESOLVED:** no clinical evidence or mechanism is supplied.
- **GUA-W consistency — PASS at governing level; technical mapping UNRESOLVED:** Jesus initiates and releases; the woman receives and glorifies God; witnesses interpret differently. No new architecture is proposed.

## Finding
Jesus does not merely restore the woman's posture. He identifies her condition as bondage and her healing as release, then places that release within the meaning of the sabbath. The woman's response is praise; the opponents' response is shame.

**Jesus sees her, calls her, and looses her. She is made straight and glorifies God.**
