# Investigation II.5 — Jesus Walks on the Sea
**Primary texts:** Matthew 14:22–33; Mark 6:45–52; John 6:16–21 (KJV)  
**Related texts:** Job 9:8; Psalm 77:19; John 1:1–3, 14; Hebrews 1:1–3  
**Status:** Comparative textual observations recorded; physical mechanism not described.

## Question
What do the Gospel witnesses report when Jesus comes to His disciples upon the sea, and what do their different accounts reveal about recognition, fear, faith, and His identity?

## Narrative sequence
- Jesus sends the disciples across the sea while He dismisses the multitude and goes apart to pray (Matthew and Mark).
- The disciples are distressed by contrary wind and waves; Jesus comes to them walking upon the sea.
- The disciples are troubled and cry out, supposing they see a spirit.
- Jesus speaks: “Be of good cheer; it is I; be not afraid.”
- Matthew alone records Peter asking to come to Jesus, walking on the water, becoming afraid, beginning to sink, and being rescued by Jesus.
- The accounts record the wind ceasing or the boat reaching its destination; Matthew records the disciples worshipping Him and saying, “Of a truth thou art the Son of God.”
- Mark adds that the disciples were amazed beyond measure and had not considered the miracle of the loaves, “for their heart was hardened” (Mark 6:51–52).
- John records that they willingly received Jesus into the ship and that the boat was immediately at the land whither they went (John 6:21).

## Differences to preserve
Matthew's Peter episode is not in Mark or John. Mark's comment about the loaves and hardened hearts is a distinctive interpretive detail. John's wording about the boat reaching land should not be expanded into a technical account of instantaneous transportation without further evidence.

## What the text states
Jesus walks upon the sea; the disciples are afraid; Jesus identifies Himself and tells them not to fear. Matthew records Peter's partial participation and rescue. The wind ceases in the Synoptic accounts, and the disciples respond with worship/confession in Matthew.

## Witness and agency
The disciples experience the storm and see Jesus. Their first interpretation is fear and mistaken identification. Jesus speaks and reveals Himself. Peter responds to Jesus' invitation but cannot sustain himself when afraid; Jesus rescues him. The disciples' understanding is incomplete, as Mark's comment explicitly notes.

## Canonical context
Job 9:8 describes God as the One who “treadeth upon the waves of the sea.” Psalm 77:19 speaks of God's way in the sea. These texts are relevant canonical background, but the precise allusion should be described as a supported theological correspondence rather than asserted as a direct quotation. John 1 and Hebrews 1 provide the wider identity context of the Son and Creation.

## What the text does not state
No Gospel gives a physical mechanism for walking upon the sea or the cessation of the wind. The accounts do not supply wave measurements, weather records, or experimental conditions. The event must not be converted into a general physical law.

## Triple Constraint
- **Biblical compliance — PASS:** the accounts' shared core and individual details are distinguished.
- **Scientific coherence — PASS for restraint; mechanism UNRESOLVED:** no physical mechanism or measurement is described.
- **GUA-W consistency — PASS for governing relationship; technical mapping UNRESOLVED:** the account preserves Christ's authority and the disciples' derivative witnessing without adding architecture.

## Provisional finding
The sign is not only an extraordinary event over the sea. It exposes the disciples' fear and incomplete understanding while revealing Jesus' authority and eliciting worship/confession in Matthew.

**The disciples see the sea. They fear the sight. Jesus speaks—and identifies Himself.**
