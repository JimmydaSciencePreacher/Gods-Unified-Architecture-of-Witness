# Investigation II.31 — Peter's Mother-in-Law Healed
**Primary texts:** Matthew 8:14–15; Mark 1:29–31; Luke 4:38–39 (KJV)  
**Status:** Comparative case study recorded; diagnosis and mechanism not supplied.

## Question
What do the three Gospel accounts report when Jesus heals Simon Peter's mother-in-law?

## Narrative sequence
- Jesus enters Peter's house and sees Peter's wife's mother lying sick of a fever (Matthew).
- Mark and Luke place the event after Jesus leaves the synagogue and enters Simon's house; they note that the household appeals to Jesus concerning her.
- Jesus comes to her, stands over her, and rebukes the fever (Luke); Matthew and Mark describe Him touching or taking her by the hand.
- The fever leaves her immediately. She rises and ministers to them.

## Differences to preserve
Matthew says Jesus touched her hand; Mark says He took her by the hand and lifted her up; Luke says He stood over her and rebuked the fever. These details complement the accounts but should not be collapsed into a single invented physical sequence beyond what each reports.

## What the text states
She has a fever; Jesus acts; the fever leaves; she rises and serves. Mark and Luke give the household context and the immediate response.

## Witness and agency
The household brings the need to Jesus' attention. Jesus approaches and heals. The woman rises and serves. Her service is the recorded response, not a condition stated as earning the healing.

## What the text does not state
No temperature, duration, pathogen, diagnosis, treatment history, or physiological mechanism is given. “Fever” should not be retrofitted to a specific modern illness.

## Triple Constraint
- **Biblical compliance — PASS:** fever, Jesus' action, immediate departure, and her rising to serve are explicit.
- **Scientific coherence — PASS for restraint; diagnosis/mechanism UNRESOLVED:** no clinical data are supplied.
- **GUA-W consistency — PASS at governing level; technical mapping UNRESOLVED:** Jesus acts; she receives and responds. No new architecture is proposed.

## Finding
The three accounts emphasize Jesus' authority and the immediate result. The healed woman is not left as a passive object in the narrative: she rises and serves.

**The fever leaves. She rises. She serves.**
