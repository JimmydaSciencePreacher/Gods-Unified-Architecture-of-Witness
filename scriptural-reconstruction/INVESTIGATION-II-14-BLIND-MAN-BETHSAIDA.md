# Investigation II.14 — The Blind Man at Bethsaida
**Primary text:** Mark 8:22–26 (KJV)  
**Related texts:** Mark 7:31–37; John 9:1–41; Matthew 11:2–6; Isaiah 35:5  
**Status:** Textual observations recorded; medical mechanism not supplied.

## Question
Why does Mark preserve a healing in which the man's sight is restored in stages, and what does the text actually say about the process?

## Narrative sequence
- Jesus and His disciples come to Bethsaida.
- People bring a blind man and ask Jesus to touch him.
- Jesus takes the man by the hand and leads him out of the town.
- He spits on the man's eyes, puts His hands upon him, and asks whether he sees anything.
- The man looks up and says he sees men as trees, walking.
- Jesus puts His hands upon his eyes again; the man is made to look up, and his sight is restored so that he sees every man clearly.
- Jesus sends him to his house and instructs him not to enter into the town or tell it to anyone in the town.

## What the text states
Mark records a two-stage encounter: an initial partial perception followed by clear sight. This is the only Gospel account that narrates this particular healing in this form. The text does not say that Jesus lacked power or that an unforeseen failure occurred; it simply records the sequence Jesus chose to enact.

## Witness and agency
Others bring the man. Jesus leads him, acts, asks what he sees, acts again, and sends him away with an instruction. The man reports his perception. The narrative gives no role to an independent healing force in the man's sight.

## Canonical context
The restoration of sight is among the signs associated with God's promised salvation (Isaiah 35:5; Matthew 11:2–6). The account may also be read in its immediate Markan context: the disciples have struggled to understand despite seeing Jesus' works (Mark 8:14–21), and the following passage records Peter's confession and Jesus' teaching about His suffering (Mark 8:27 onward). This juxtaposition is worth investigating, but the text does not explicitly state that the two-stage healing is an allegory of the disciples' understanding. That proposal remains an interpretation, not a textual fact.

## What the text does not state
No cause of blindness, ophthalmic diagnosis, anatomical detail, or physical mechanism is supplied. The passage does not explain why Jesus uses two stages or authorize a generalized two-stage healing model.

## Triple Constraint
- **Biblical compliance — PASS:** the two-stage sequence and final restoration are stated plainly.
- **Scientific coherence — PASS for restraint; mechanism UNRESOLVED:** no medical data or mechanism are supplied.
- **GUA-W consistency — PASS for governing relationship; technical mapping UNRESOLVED:** Christ's initiative and authority are preserved; no new architecture is proposed.

## Finding
This account is important because it prevents us from imposing a simplistic pattern on every miracle. Jesus' works are not all narrated with the same sequence. Here the text records partial sight followed by clear sight, without explaining the reason. We must preserve the sequence and leave the reason open unless further Scriptural evidence resolves it.

**The first sight is partial. Jesus acts again. The man sees clearly.**
