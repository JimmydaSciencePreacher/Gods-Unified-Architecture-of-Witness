# Investigation II.9 — The Woman with the Issue of Blood and Jairus' Daughter
**Primary texts:** Matthew 9:18–26; Mark 5:21–43; Luke 8:40–56 (KJV)  
**Related texts:** Leviticus 15:19–30; Numbers 19; 2 Kings 4:18–37; John 11:1–44; John 20:30–31  
**Status:** Comparative study of two interwoven accounts recorded; medical details remain limited.

## Question
What does the interwoven narrative show about Jesus' authority over long-standing illness and death, and how do faith, touch, command, and witness appear in the sequence?

## Sequence
1. Jairus, a ruler of the synagogue, falls at Jesus' feet and asks Him to come because his only daughter (Luke) is dying or dead (accounts differ in when the report of death arrives).
2. Jesus goes with him; a multitude presses around Him.
3. A woman who has had an issue of blood for twelve years approaches from behind and touches the border/hem of His garment.
4. She has suffered under many physicians and spent what she had; Mark says she grew worse. She believes that touching His clothes will make her whole.
5. Immediately the issue of blood dries up / she feels in her body that she is healed.
6. Jesus asks who touched Him. Mark records His perception that virtue had gone out of Him; Luke records that He perceived virtue had gone out.
7. The woman comes trembling, declares the truth, and Jesus addresses her as “Daughter,” commending her faith and sending her in peace.
8. During this interruption, messengers report that Jairus' daughter is dead. Jesus tells Jairus, “Be not afraid, only believe.”
9. Jesus enters the house with selected disciples and the parents. He takes the child by the hand and speaks: “Maid, arise” (Luke) / “Talitha cumi” (Mark, with translation).
10. The child arises and walks; the witnesses are astonished. Jesus directs that she be given food and instructs them concerning the report.

## Differences to preserve
Matthew compresses the sequence and presents Jairus saying his daughter is already dead when he first approaches. Mark and Luke narrate the initial request while she is dying and the later message that she has died. Mark includes the most detail about the woman's suffering and the Aramaic words spoken to the child. Luke calls her Jairus' only daughter. These details should not be flattened into a single wording.

## What the text states
The woman has suffered twelve years and is immediately healed after touching Jesus' garment. Jesus identifies her and addresses her personally. The child is dead according to the messengers; Jesus enters, takes her by the hand, commands her to arise, and she returns to life.

## Witness and agency
The woman approaches and touches; Jesus knows and addresses her. Jairus asks, receives the report, and is told to believe. The messengers report death; Jesus speaks; the child rises. The crowd, disciples, and family witness different parts of the events. Neither the woman's touch nor Jairus' request is presented as the source of Jesus' authority.

## Canonical context
Leviticus 15 gives ritual instructions concerning bodily discharges; this helps explain the woman's social and ceremonial context but should not be used to claim details the Gospel does not state. The raising of the child can be compared with Elijah's raising of the widow's son (1 Kings 17:17–24) and Elisha's raising of the Shunammite's son (2 Kings 4:18–37), while preserving the differences in each narrative. John 11 provides a later and distinct raising account.

## Limits
The Gospels do not supply a modern diagnosis for the woman's condition, a clinical record, or a mechanism. The child's death and restoration are reported as narrative facts; no medical measurements are provided. Do not infer that the woman's faith functions as a controllable physical force.

## Triple Constraint
- **Biblical compliance — PASS:** the interwoven sequence and differences among the Gospel accounts are preserved.
- **Scientific coherence — PASS for restraint; medical mechanism UNRESOLVED:** the accounts do not supply modern clinical data.
- **GUA-W consistency — PASS for governing relationship; technical mapping UNRESOLVED:** Jesus acts with authority; the woman, Jairus, disciples, messengers, and family are witnesses/participants.

## Finding
The narrative interweaves two cases: a woman with twelve years of suffering and a child at the threshold of death. Jesus is not delayed beyond authority by the interruption or by the report of death. He addresses the woman and commands the child to arise. The account directs attention to Him, not to a technique of touch or faith.

**The woman is healed. The child is raised. Jesus is the acting centre of both accounts.**
