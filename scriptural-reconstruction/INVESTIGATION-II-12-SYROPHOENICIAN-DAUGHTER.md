# Investigation II.12 — The Daughter of the Syrophoenician Woman
**Primary texts:** Matthew 15:21–28; Mark 7:24–30 (KJV)  
**Related texts:** Matthew 8:5–13; John 4:46–54; Matthew 11:2–6; John 1:1–3, 14  
**Status:** Comparative textual study recorded; the daughter's condition and physical mechanism are not medically specified.

## Question
What do Matthew and Mark record about a mother's appeal, Jesus' words, the daughter's deliverance at a distance, and the mother's faith?

## Narrative sequence
- Jesus withdraws into the region of Tyre and Sidon. Mark identifies the woman as a Greek, a Syrophenician by nation; Matthew calls her a woman of Canaan.
- She cries for mercy, addressing Jesus as Lord and Son of David in Matthew, and asks Him to cast the devil out of her daughter.
- Matthew records an initial silence and the disciples' request that Jesus send her away.
- Jesus speaks about His mission to the lost sheep of the house of Israel; the woman persists and worships Him.
- The exchange concerning the children's bread and the dogs follows. The woman answers that even the dogs eat of the crumbs which fall from their masters' table.
- Jesus commends her faith in Matthew and grants her request.
- Mark records Jesus saying, “For this saying go thy way; the devil is gone out of thy daughter.”
- The woman returns home and finds the daughter laid upon the bed, with the devil gone out.

## Differences to preserve
Matthew emphasizes the woman's address to Jesus as Son of David, her worship, the disciples' intervention, and Jesus' explicit commendation of her great faith. Mark identifies her as Greek/Syrophenician and records the daughter's condition and the mother's return home to find the child delivered. Neither account should be used to erase the other's distinctive emphasis.

## What the text states
The daughter is oppressed by a devil; the mother appeals to Jesus; Jesus speaks; the deliverance occurs while the daughter is not physically with Him; the mother finds the child delivered at home. Matthew records Jesus' commendation of her faith.

## Witness and agency
The mother appeals and persists; Jesus grants the request and declares the deliverance; the mother returns and verifies the result. The daughter is the recipient. The mother's faith is commended, but the text attributes the authority to deliver to Jesus, not to faith as an independent force.

## Canonical context
This account can be compared with the centurion's servant (Matthew 8:5–13; Luke 7:1–10) and the nobleman's son (John 4:46–54): in each, Jesus' word is associated with a result at a distance. The accounts are not identical: here the issue is deliverance from a devil, and the narrative includes a sustained exchange about the woman's request and Jesus' mission. The text's setting among Gentile territory and its language about Israel and the children's bread require careful reading in Matthew and Mark's wider presentation.

## What the text does not state
The accounts do not describe the daughter's symptoms, a modern psychiatric or neurological diagnosis, or a medical mechanism. They do not teach a repeatable formula for exorcism or remote intervention.

## Triple Constraint
- **Biblical compliance — PASS:** the woman's request, Jesus' words, the deliverance, and the differences between accounts are preserved.
- **Scientific coherence — PASS for domain restraint; modern diagnosis/mechanism UNRESOLVED:** no clinical description is supplied.
- **GUA-W consistency — PASS for governing relationship; technical mapping UNRESOLVED:** Christ is the authority; mother and household witness the result. No new architecture is proposed.

## Finding
The daughter is delivered without Jesus travelling to her location. The mother's persistence and faith are noted, but the deliverance is attributed to Jesus' authority and word. This account extends the distance theme while adding the distinct subject of Gentile faith and the scope of Jesus' mission.

**The mother asks. Jesus speaks. At home, the daughter is found delivered.**
