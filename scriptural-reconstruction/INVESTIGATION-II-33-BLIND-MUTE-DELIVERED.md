# Investigation II.33 — The Blind and Mute Man Delivered
**Primary texts:** Matthew 12:22–30; Luke 11:14–23 (KJV)  
**Related text:** Matthew 9:32–34  
**Status:** Comparative case study recorded; relation to Matthew 9 remains unresolved.

## Question
What do Matthew and Luke report when Jesus delivers a man whose blindness and muteness are associated with demonic oppression, and what dispute follows?

## Narrative sequence
- A man is brought to Jesus who is blind and mute, and is possessed with a devil (Matthew); Luke reports a mute man and the devil's expulsion.
- Jesus heals/delivers him so that the blind and mute man both speaks and sees (Matthew); the mute speaks after the devil is cast out (Luke).
- The people are amazed and ask whether this is the Son of David (Matthew).
- Some accuse Jesus of casting out devils through Beelzebub, the prince of devils.
- Jesus answers that a kingdom divided against itself cannot stand, and that Satan cannot cast out Satan without division.
- Jesus speaks of the stronger man overcoming the strong man, and declares that the kingdom of God is come upon them if He casts out devils by the Spirit of God (Matthew); Luke similarly says by the finger of God.

## Differences and relation to Matthew 9
Matthew 9:32–34 reports a mute man delivered, followed by a Beelzebub accusation. Matthew 12:22–30 reports a blind and mute man and an extended answer. Luke 11:14–23 reports a mute man and the dispute. These may reflect a related episode or summary tradition, but the texts do not expressly establish the identity of the occasions. Retain the entries separately until evidence supports a conclusion.

## What the text states
The man receives sight and speech in Matthew's account; Luke records speech restored. Jesus expels the devil; the crowd and opponents interpret the event differently. Jesus answers the charge by explaining the incoherence of Satan opposing himself and declaring the arrival of God's kingdom.

## Witness and agency
The man is brought; Jesus delivers and restores; the crowd wonders; opponents attribute the work to Beelzebub; Jesus answers. The disagreement concerns the source and meaning of the witnessed act.

## What the text does not state
No modern diagnosis or physiological mechanism is provided. The text does not authorize treating every case of blindness or muteness as demonic oppression.

## Triple Constraint

## Finding
This is a central witness-attribution dispute. The physical result is visible, but observers disagree radically about its source. Jesus' answer directs attention to the coherence of the kingdom and to the arrival of God's rule.

**The man sees and speaks. The witnesses disagree about the source. Jesus answers.**
