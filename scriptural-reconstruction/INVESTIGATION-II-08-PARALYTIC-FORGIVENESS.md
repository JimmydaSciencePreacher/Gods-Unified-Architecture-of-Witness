# Investigation II.8 — The Paralytic: Forgiveness and Healing
**Primary texts:** Matthew 9:1–8; Mark 2:1–12; Luke 5:17–26 (KJV)  
**Related texts:** Psalm 103:1–3; Isaiah 35:4–6; Daniel 7:13–14; John 5:19–30; John 20:30–31  
**Status:** Comparative textual study recorded.

## Question
Why does Jesus first say, “Son, thy sins be forgiven thee,” and then heal the paralytic? What does the event itself say about the Son of man's authority?

## Sequence
- Jesus is teaching in a house; crowds gather so densely that there is no room to enter.
- Men bring a man sick of the palsy on a bed. Mark and Luke describe their effort to reach Jesus by opening the roof and lowering him before Him.
- Jesus sees their faith and says, “Son, thy sins be forgiven thee.”
- Scribes reason in their hearts that Jesus blasphemes, because God alone can forgive sins.
- Jesus perceives their reasoning and asks whether it is easier to say that sins are forgiven or to say, “Rise up and walk.”
- Jesus declares that the Son of man hath power on earth to forgive sins, then commands the man to rise, take up his bed, and go home.
- The man immediately rises, takes up what he had been carried on, and departs before them.
- The people glorify God and express amazement.

## Differences to preserve
Mark gives vivid detail about uncovering the roof. Luke specifies that the power of the Lord was present to heal. Matthew's account is more compressed and records the crowd glorifying God for giving such power unto men. The differences should remain visible.

## What the text states
Jesus pronounces forgiveness, perceives the scribes' reasoning, identifies the healing as a visible demonstration of the Son of man's authority to forgive sins, and heals the paralytic by command. The man rises immediately.

## Witness and agency
The bearers bring the man; Jesus sees their faith; the scribes reason; Jesus knows their thoughts; the paralytic receives and obeys the command; the crowd witnesses and glorifies God. The bearers' faith is noted, but the authority to forgive and heal is attributed to Jesus.

## Canonical context
Psalm 103:1–3 associates the Lord with forgiveness of iniquities and healing of diseases. Isaiah 35:4–6 connects God's coming salvation with the lame leaping. Daniel 7:13–14 provides background for the Son of man's dominion, though the exact relation to this saying must be established by wider study. Most importantly, Jesus Himself states the purpose of the visible healing: “that ye may know that the Son of man hath power on earth to forgive sins.”

## Limits
The accounts do not identify the man's modern neurological diagnosis or describe a physiological mechanism. The theological claim about forgiveness is not reducible to a medical claim. Do not treat the healing as laboratory proof of forgiveness; the text presents Jesus' declared purpose and the sign together.

## Triple Constraint
- **Biblical compliance — PASS:** Jesus' explicit statement about the purpose of the healing governs interpretation.
- **Scientific coherence — PASS for distinction of domains; diagnosis/mechanism UNRESOLVED:** no modern clinical details are supplied.
- **GUA-W consistency — PASS for governing relationship; technical mapping UNRESOLVED:** the authority belongs to Jesus; bearers, healed man, scribes, and crowd occupy derivative witness roles.

## Finding
This event is especially explicit: the physical healing is performed so witnesses may know the Son of man's authority to forgive sins. The visible result is not detached spectacle; it serves the stated testimony about Jesus' authority.

**The man is lowered. Forgiveness is spoken. The Son of man commands. The man rises.**
