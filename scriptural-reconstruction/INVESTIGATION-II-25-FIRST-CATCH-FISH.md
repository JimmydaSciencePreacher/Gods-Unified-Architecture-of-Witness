# Investigation II.25 — The First Miraculous Catch of Fishes
**Primary text:** Luke 5:1–11 (KJV)  
**Related texts:** Matthew 4:18–22; Mark 1:16–20; John 21:1–14; Habakkuk 1:14–17  
**Status:** Textual case study recorded; comparison with John 21 remains distinct.

## Question
What does Luke record when Jesus directs Simon to let down the nets after an unsuccessful night, and how does the catch lead to Simon's response and calling?

## Narrative sequence
- The people press upon Jesus to hear the word of God as He stands by the lake of Gennesaret.
- Jesus enters Simon's ship and asks him to thrust out a little from the land; He sits and teaches the people.
- When He finishes speaking, He tells Simon to launch out into the deep and let down the nets for a draught.
- Simon answers that they have toiled all night and taken nothing, but says, “nevertheless at thy word I will let down the net.”
- They enclose a great multitude of fishes; the net breaks.
- They beckon to partners in the other ship, who come and help; both ships are filled so that they begin to sink.
- Simon Peter falls at Jesus' knees, saying, “Depart from me; for I am a sinful man, O Lord.”
- Those with him are astonished, including James and John, partners with Simon.
- Jesus tells Simon not to fear and says, “from henceforth thou shalt catch men.”
- They bring their ships to land, forsake all, and follow Him.

## What the text states
Luke contrasts an unsuccessful night of labour with an extraordinary catch following Jesus' direction. Simon explicitly acts “at thy word.” The catch is so large that nets break and ships are endangered. The event leads to Simon's recognition of his own sinfulness and to Jesus' calling.

## Witness and agency
Jesus teaches, directs, and calls. Simon reports the night's failure, obeys the instruction, and witnesses the catch. Partners assist with the nets and ships. Their work participates in retrieval, but the narrative gives the initiating direction to Jesus. Simon's response is not merely amazement at fish; he recognizes the Lord and his own condition.

## Canonical context
Matthew 4:18–22 and Mark 1:16–20 record the calling of fishermen but do not narrate Luke's miraculous catch. John 21:1–14 records another miraculous catch after Jesus' resurrection. The two catches share features but differ in setting, sequence, and outcome; do not merge them. Luke's account immediately leads into the calling of disciples and the mission image of catching men.

## What the text does not state
Luke gives no number or species of fish, no measured weight, and no biological or hydrological mechanism. The text does not describe a reproducible fishing technique. Do not infer that Jesus' instruction is a general fishing method.

## Triple Constraint
- **Biblical compliance — PASS:** unsuccessful night, instruction, Simon's response, catch, partners, astonishment, and calling are explicit.
- **Scientific coherence — PASS for restraint; mechanism UNRESOLVED:** no ecological measurements or mechanism are provided.
- **GUA-W consistency — PASS at governing level; technical mapping UNRESOLVED:** Jesus' word directs; Simon and partners respond; the catch is witnessed and becomes part of a calling. No new architecture is proposed.

## Finding
Luke explicitly places Simon's action under Jesus' word: “at thy word.” The extraordinary catch is not the endpoint; it reveals the authority of Jesus and leads to a new vocation. John 21 must be investigated separately, rather than treating the two catches as one repeated event.

**An empty night. A word from Jesus. Nets filled—and Simon follows.**
