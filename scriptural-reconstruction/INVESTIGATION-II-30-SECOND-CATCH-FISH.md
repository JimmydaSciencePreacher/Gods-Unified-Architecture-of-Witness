# Investigation II.30 — The Miraculous Catch after the Resurrection
**Primary text:** John 21:1–14 (KJV)  
**Related texts:** Luke 5:1–11; John 20:19–29; John 21:15–25  
**Status:** Text-first case study recorded; comparison with Luke 5 remains distinct.

## Question
What does John report when the risen Jesus directs the disciples' fishing, provides a great catch, and prepares food for them on the shore?

## Narrative sequence
1. Jesus manifests Himself again to the disciples at the sea of Tiberias.
2. Seven disciples are together; Peter says he will go fishing, and the others join him.
3. They labour that night and catch nothing.
4. At daybreak Jesus stands on the shore, though the disciples do not initially know it is Jesus.
5. He asks whether they have any meat; they answer no.
6. He tells them to cast the net on the right side of the ship, and they will find.
7. They cast and are unable to draw the net for the multitude of fishes.
8. The disciple whom Jesus loved identifies Him to Peter: “It is the Lord.” Peter girds his fisher's coat and casts himself into the sea; the others come in the little ship, dragging the net.
9. On shore they see a fire of coals, fish laid thereon, and bread. Jesus tells them to bring of the fish they have caught.
10. Peter draws the net to land, full of great fishes, an hundred and fifty and three; although so many, the net is not broken.
11. Jesus invites them to dine, gives them bread and fish, and none of the disciples dares ask who He is, knowing it is the Lord.
12. John identifies this as the third manifestation of the risen Jesus to the disciples as a group in the sequence he has narrated.

## Comparison with Luke 5
Both accounts include a fruitless night, Jesus' direction, and an extraordinary catch. But Luke 5 occurs at the beginning of the disciples' calling and leads to their leaving all to follow Jesus; John 21 occurs after the resurrection and leads into Peter's restoration/commission in John 21:15–19. In Luke the nets break; in John the net is not broken. Do not merge the events.

## What the text states
The risen Jesus directs the catch; the disciples obey; the net fills with 153 large fish and does not break. Jesus already has a fire, fish, and bread on shore, and also invites them to bring some of their catch. He serves them.

## Witness and agency
Jesus stands on shore, speaks, directs, provides food, and serves. The disciples initially fail to recognize Him; the beloved disciple identifies Him; Peter responds immediately; the group brings in the catch and eats. The catch is not the only provision in the scene: food is already prepared by Jesus before their fish arrive.

## What the text does not state
John does not describe fish behaviour, the location of the shoal, or a biological mechanism. The text does not explain why the number 153 is recorded; proposed symbolic interpretations should remain hypotheses unless independently established. It does not state that the fish catch itself is the cause of Peter's restoration.

## Triple Constraint
- **Biblical compliance — PASS:** post-resurrection setting, recognition, direction, 153 fish, unbroken net, prepared meal, and service are explicit.
- **Scientific coherence — PASS for restraint; mechanism UNRESOLVED:** no ecological measurements or physical mechanism are given.
- **GUA-W consistency — PASS at governing level; detailed mapping UNRESOLVED:** the risen Christ directs and provides; disciples respond and witness. No new architecture is proposed.

## Finding
John's scene contains a subtle but important distinction: Jesus directs the disciples to a catch, yet the meal does not depend on their success because He already has food prepared. Their contribution is received into provision that precedes it. The scene then leads to Jesus' words to Peter.

**The risen Lord directs the catch—and already has breakfast prepared.**
