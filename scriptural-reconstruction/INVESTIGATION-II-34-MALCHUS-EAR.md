# Investigation II.34 — Jesus Heals the Ear of Malchus
**Primary texts:** Luke 22:47–53; John 18:1–11 (KJV)  
**Related text:** Matthew 26:47–56; Mark 14:43–50  
**Status:** Comparative case study recorded; the Gospels differ in the level of detail.

## Question
What do Luke and John report when one of Jesus' followers strikes the servant of the high priest during the arrest, and Jesus responds?

## Narrative sequence
- Judas approaches with a multitude to identify and arrest Jesus.
- One of those with Jesus strikes the high priest's servant and cuts off his right ear. John identifies the disciple as Simon Peter and the servant as Malchus.
- Luke records Jesus saying, “Suffer ye thus far,” touching the servant's ear, and healing him.
- John records Jesus' command to Peter to put up his sword into the sheath and asks whether He shall not drink the cup the Father has given Him.
- Matthew also preserves Jesus' warning about the sword and His ability to appeal to the Father; Mark records the cutting off of the ear but not its healing.

## Differences to preserve
Luke alone explicitly narrates the healing touch. John identifies Peter and Malchus and records Jesus' command concerning the sword and the cup. Matthew and Mark include the injury but not the healing. Do not attribute Luke's explicit healing detail to every Gospel account as though each narrates it.

## What the text states
A servant's right ear is cut off; Luke says Jesus touches and heals it. Jesus rejects armed resistance and submits to the Father's appointed cup.

## Witness and agency
Peter acts violently; Malchus receives the injury; Jesus heals and restrains Peter. The arresting party witnesses the event. Jesus' action does not prevent His arrest; He proceeds toward the Passion.

## What the text does not state
No anatomical description, extent of tissue loss, treatment process, or mechanism is supplied. Luke does not specify whether the ear is reattached, regenerated, or restored by another process. Do not invent one.

## Triple Constraint
- **Biblical compliance — PASS:** injury, identities, healing in Luke, and Jesus' response to Peter are explicit in their respective accounts.
- **Scientific coherence — PASS for restraint; mechanism UNRESOLVED:** no clinical description is given.
- **GUA-W consistency — PASS at governing level; technical mapping UNRESOLVED:** Jesus heals but does not evade the Passion; Peter's action is corrected. No new architecture is proposed.

## Finding
This healing occurs at the threshold of Jesus' arrest. It is not an act of self-preservation or escape: Jesus heals the injured servant and then willingly proceeds in obedience to the Father's cup.

**Jesus restores the ear—and still goes forward to the cross.**
