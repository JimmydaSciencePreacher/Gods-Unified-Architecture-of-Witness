# Investigation II.35 — The Transfiguration
**Primary texts:** Matthew 17:1–9; Mark 9:2–10; Luke 9:28–36 (KJV)  
**Related texts:** Exodus 24:12–18; Exodus 34:29–35; Deuteronomy 18:15; 2 Peter 1:16–18  
**Status:** Comparative study recorded; classify as revelation/sign, not as an ordinary healing miracle.

## Question
What do the three Synoptic Gospels report when Jesus is transfigured before Peter, James, and John, Moses and Elias appear, and the Father's voice is heard?

## Narrative sequence
- Jesus takes Peter, James, and John up into a high mountain apart.
- He is transfigured before them; His face shines as the sun and His raiment becomes white/light.
- Moses and Elias appear and speak with Him. Luke says they speak of His decease which He should accomplish at Jerusalem.
- Peter proposes making three tabernacles, one for Jesus, one for Moses, and one for Elias.
- A bright cloud overshadows them; a voice out of the cloud declares, “This is my beloved Son, in whom I am well pleased; hear ye him” (Matthew). Mark and Luke preserve the declaration of His Sonship and the command to hear Him.
- The disciples fall on their faces / are sore afraid. Jesus comes, touches them, and tells them not to fear.
- They see Jesus only; He instructs them to tell the vision to no man until the Son of man is risen from the dead.

## Differences to preserve
Luke records the subject of the conversation—Jesus' decease at Jerusalem—and says the disciples were heavy with sleep before becoming fully awake. Matthew records the disciples falling on their faces and Jesus touching them. Mark notes that the disciples questioned what rising from the dead should mean. Preserve the distinct details.

## What the text states
Jesus' appearance is transformed; Moses and Elias appear; the cloud overshadows; the Father's voice identifies Jesus as His beloved Son and commands the disciples to hear Him. The event anticipates the Passion and resurrection.

## Witness and agency
The Father speaks and identifies the Son. Jesus is transfigured and speaks with Moses and Elias. The disciples are witnesses, but initially respond with fear and confusion. The command is not “hear all three equally”: the voice directs them to hear the Son.

## Canonical context
The mountain, cloud, shining appearance, and Moses recall Sinai, while the command to hear the Son recalls the promised Prophet (Deuteronomy 18:15). Peter later identifies the event as a manifestation of Christ's majesty and heavenly honour (2 Peter 1:16–18). The Transfiguration is revelation of Jesus' identity and glory, not simply another healing event.

## What the text does not state
The accounts do not provide a physical mechanism for the transformation, a modern optical description, or an explanation of how Moses and Elias appeared. Do not reduce the event to metaphor or assert an unreported mechanism.

## Triple Constraint
- **Biblical compliance — PASS:** transformation, appearances, cloud, voice, disciples' response, and instruction are explicit.
- **Scientific coherence — UNRESOLVED as a physical mechanism; PASS for accurate classification:** this is presented as divine revelation, not a repeatable laboratory event.
- **GUA-W consistency — PASS at governing level; detailed mapping UNRESOLVED:** the Father identifies the Son; the disciples receive testimony. Christ's unique identity remains central. No new architecture is proposed.

## Finding
The Transfiguration is not merely a display of unusual appearance. Its interpretive centre is the Father's testimony concerning the Son: “hear ye him.” Moses and Elias appear, but the voice directs the disciples to Jesus.

**The Father identifies the Son. The witnesses are commanded: Hear Him.**
