# Investigation II.23 — The Mute Man Delivered
**Primary text:** Matthew 9:32–34 (KJV)  
**Related texts:** Matthew 12:22–30; Luke 11:14–23; Matthew 8:28–34; Mark 5:1–20  
**Status:** Textual case study recorded; parallel-event identity remains unresolved.

## Question
What does Matthew report when a mute man brought to Jesus is delivered from a devil, and how do the witnesses interpret the result?

## Narrative sequence
- As the two men whose sight was restored depart, others bring to Jesus a man who is mute and possessed with a devil.
- When the devil is cast out, the mute man speaks.
- The multitudes marvel, saying that it was never so seen in Israel.
- The Pharisees respond that Jesus casts out devils through the prince of the devils.

## What the text states
Matthew directly associates the man's muteness with demonic oppression in this narrative and reports that he speaks after the devil is cast out. The crowd marvels; the Pharisees offer a hostile attribution.

## Witness and agency
The people bring the man. Jesus casts out the devil. The man speaks. The multitude and Pharisees witness the result but interpret its source differently. Matthew's sequence makes the dispute over attribution central.

## Canonical comparison and unresolved identity
Matthew 12:22–30 and Luke 11:14–23 describe a blind and mute man delivered from a devil, followed by a dispute over Beelzebub. These passages may be related to the event here, but the texts do not explicitly identify them as the same occasion. The register therefore retains them as separate textual entries pending comparison rather than asserting two events or one event prematurely.

## What the text does not state
No medical diagnosis or neurological explanation for muteness is supplied. The passage does not provide a physical mechanism for the deliverance or speech restoration.

## Triple Constraint
- **Biblical compliance — PASS:** the account's sequence and contrasting interpretations are explicit.
- **Scientific coherence — PASS for restraint; diagnosis/mechanism UNRESOLVED:** no clinical data are supplied.
- **GUA-W consistency — PASS at governing level; technical mapping UNRESOLVED:** Jesus acts; the man receives; witnesses differ in attribution. No new architecture is proposed.

## Finding
The same result produces sharply different witness reports: the multitude marvels, while the Pharisees attribute the work to an evil source. This is a direct case for examining not only what a witness observes, but how the witness attributes the cause.

**The man speaks. The crowd marvels. The Pharisees dispute the source.**
