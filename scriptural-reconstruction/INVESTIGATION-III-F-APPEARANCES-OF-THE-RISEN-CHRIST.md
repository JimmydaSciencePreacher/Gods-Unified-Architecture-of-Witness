# Investigation III.F — Appearances of the Risen Christ: Witnesses, Encounters, and Testimony

**Primary texts:** Matthew 28:8–20; Mark 16:9–20 (KJV, with textual-critical qualification); Luke 24:13–53; John 20:11–21:25; Acts 1:1–11; 1 Corinthians 15:3–8  
**Related texts:** John 10:17–18; John 11:25–26; Romans 6:9; 1 Corinthians 15:12–58  
**Status:** Comparative Scriptural study written; chronology and some harmonization questions remain open.

## 1. Investigative question

**Whom does the risen Jesus encounter, what do those witnesses report, and what does Scripture establish about the identity and condition of the risen Christ?**

This study follows the empty-tomb examination. The appearances must not be collapsed into the tomb discovery, nor arranged into a seamless chronology before the individual accounts are understood. The Gospels provide selected encounters, not a single continuous transcript of every event during the forty days.

## 2. Matthew 28:8–20 — The women and the eleven in Galilee

Matthew records the women departing from the sepulchre with fear and great joy to tell the disciples. Jesus meets them; they come, hold Him by the feet, and worship Him. Jesus tells them not to fear and instructs them to tell His brethren to go into Galilee, where they shall see Him.

Matthew then records the eleven disciples going into Galilee, to a mountain where Jesus had appointed them. They see Him and worship Him, though Matthew also records that some doubted. Jesus declares that all power is given unto Him in heaven and in earth and commissions them to teach all nations, baptizing and teaching them to observe His commandments. He promises, “lo, I am with you alway, even unto the end of the world.”

**TEXT STATES:** The women physically encounter Jesus and worship Him. The eleven later meet Him in Galilee, worship, and receive the commission. Matthew preserves the detail that some doubted.

**LIMIT:** Matthew does not identify the mountain by name or provide a complete itinerary connecting this meeting to every other appearance.

## 3. Mark 16:9–20 — The KJV longer ending

The KJV records that Jesus first appeared to Mary Magdalene; she tells those who had been with Him, and they do not believe. He appears in another form to two walking in the country; they report to the others, who again do not believe. Later He appears to the eleven as they sit at meat, reproves their unbelief and hardness of heart, and commissions them.

**TEXTUAL-CRITICAL NOTE:** The longer ending is present in the KJV and many manuscripts, but absent from some important early Greek witnesses, including Codices Sinaiticus and Vaticanus. Its relationship to the earliest recoverable text remains disputed. Its content should be retained as part of the received KJV text and clearly marked when used in comparison; claims depending uniquely on Mark 16:9–20 should be distinguished from claims independently recorded in Matthew, Luke, John, or 1 Corinthians 15.

## 4. Luke 24:13–35 — The road to Emmaus

Luke records two disciples traveling to a village called Emmaus, discussing the events. Jesus Himself draws near and goes with them, but their eyes are holden that they should not know Him. He asks what they are discussing. They recount Jesus of Nazareth, His crucifixion, and the women's report that the tomb was empty and that angels had said He was alive.

Jesus rebukes their slowness of heart to believe what the prophets had spoken and expounds in all the Scriptures the things concerning Himself. At the meal, He takes bread, blesses it, breaks, and gives it to them; their eyes are opened and they know Him, and He vanishes out of their sight. They return to Jerusalem and report to the eleven and those with them that the Lord is risen indeed and has appeared to Simon.

**TEXT STATES:** Jesus walks and speaks with them, reasons from Scripture, shares a meal, is recognized, and then vanishes from their sight. The disciples report the encounter and refer to an appearance to Simon.

**DISTINCTIVE DETAIL:** Recognition is delayed. The narrative links understanding of the events with Jesus' exposition of Scripture, but does not give a modern physiological explanation for their initially restrained recognition or His vanishing.

## 5. Luke 24:36–49 — Appearance to the gathered disciples

While they are speaking, Jesus Himself stands in their midst and says, “Peace be unto you.” They are terrified and suppose they have seen a spirit. Jesus asks why they are troubled and why thoughts arise in their hearts. He directs them to behold His hands and feet and says, “handle me, and see; for a spirit hath not flesh and bones, as ye see me have.”

He shows them His hands and feet. While they yet believe not for joy and wonder, He asks whether they have food; they give Him a piece of broiled fish and of an honeycomb, and He takes it and eats before them. He then opens their understanding so they may understand the Scriptures, and explains what was written concerning His suffering and rising, repentance and remission of sins being preached among all nations, beginning at Jerusalem. He identifies them as witnesses of these things and promises the Father's promise upon them.

**TEXT STATES:** The risen Jesus identifies Himself, invites inspection and touch, declares that He has flesh and bones, and eats in their presence. The disciples' reaction includes terror, troubled thoughts, disbelief for joy, and wonder. Jesus opens their understanding of Scripture and calls them witnesses.

**LIMIT:** The passage does not provide a full anatomy or physical theory of the risen body. The text's statements about flesh and bones, recognition, and eating must be preserved without reducing the event to an ordinary resuscitation or inventing a mechanism.

## 6. John 20:11–18 — Mary Magdalene

Mary remains outside the sepulchre weeping. She sees two angels in white sitting where Jesus' body had lain. Turning, she sees Jesus but does not know that it is Jesus; she supposes Him to be the gardener. Jesus asks why she weeps and whom she seeks. When He calls her “Mary,” she recognizes Him and addresses Him as Rabboni. Jesus gives her a message for His brethren: “I ascend unto my Father, and your Father; and to my God, and your God.” Mary goes and tells the disciples, “I have seen the Lord,” and reports His words.

**TEXT STATES:** Mary sees Jesus but initially does not recognize Him. Recognition occurs when He calls her by name. She is sent to report the encounter.

**DISTINCTIVE DETAIL:** John gives the personal recognition scene and Mary's explicit testimony, “I have seen the Lord.”

## 7. John 20:19–23 — The disciples behind shut doors

The same day at evening, the first day of the week, the disciples are gathered with the doors shut for fear of the Jews. Jesus comes and stands in the midst, saying, “Peace be unto you.” He shows them His hands and His side. The disciples are glad when they see the Lord. Jesus again speaks peace, sends them as the Father sent Him, breathes on them, and says, “Receive ye the Holy Ghost.” He speaks concerning remission and retention of sins.

**TEXT STATES:** Jesus appears among them while the doors are shut, shows His hands and side, and commissions them. John records their gladness upon seeing Him.

**LIMIT:** John does not explain the physical manner by which Jesus comes to stand among them. Do not convert “the doors were shut” into a detailed theory of bodily permeability or spatial mechanics.

## 8. John 20:24–29 — Thomas

Thomas, one of the twelve, is absent during the first appearance. The other disciples tell him they have seen the Lord. Thomas says he will not believe unless he sees the print of the nails, puts his finger into the print, and puts his hand into Jesus' side.

After eight days the disciples are again within, and Thomas is with them. The doors are shut; Jesus stands in the midst and says, “Peace be unto you.” He addresses Thomas directly, invites him to behold His hands and reach toward His side, and tells him not to be faithless but believing. Thomas answers, “My Lord and my God.” Jesus speaks of those who have not seen and yet have believed.

**TEXT STATES:** Thomas is not present at the first appearance; he reports the evidentiary conditions he requires; Jesus later addresses those exact concerns; Thomas confesses Jesus as “My Lord and my God.”

**WITNESS OBSERVATION:** The testimony of other disciples does not immediately persuade Thomas. His encounter is personal and specific. Jesus' response directly addresses Thomas's stated demand.

## 9. John 21:1–14 — The sea of Tiberias

John records Jesus manifesting Himself again to disciples by the sea of Tiberias. The disciples fish through the night and catch nothing. At Jesus' direction they cast the net on the right side of the ship and are unable to draw it for the multitude of fishes. The beloved disciple recognizes, “It is the Lord.” Peter casts himself into the sea and comes to Jesus. On shore they find a fire of coals, fish laid thereon, and bread. Jesus invites them to dine and gives them bread and fish.

**TEXT STATES:** Jesus is recognized by the disciples, directs an extraordinary catch, meets them on shore, and provides food. John says this is the third time Jesus showed Himself to His disciples after He was risen from the dead, referring to the appearances counted in the immediate Johannine narrative (John 20:19–29; 21:1–14).

**LIMIT:** John's count should not be assumed to be a complete count of every appearance recorded across all four Gospels and Paul. It is the count expressed within John's narrative.

## 10. Acts 1:1–11 — The forty days and Ascension boundary

Acts says Jesus showed Himself alive after His passion by many infallible proofs, being seen of the disciples over forty days and speaking of things pertaining to the kingdom of God. He commands them not to depart from Jerusalem but to wait for the promise of the Father. He speaks of the Holy Ghost coming upon them and their being witnesses unto Him. He is taken up; a cloud receives Him out of their sight; two men in white apparel speak of His return.

**TEXT STATES:** Luke's second volume explicitly places appearances and teaching over forty days before the Ascension. This distinguishes the Resurrection appearances from the later Ascension. Acts 1:9–11 is reserved for the dedicated Ascension investigation, but its chronological boundary belongs here.

## 11. Paul's testimony — 1 Corinthians 15:3–8

Paul states that Christ died for our sins according to the Scriptures, was buried, rose again the third day according to the Scriptures, and was seen by Cephas, then the twelve, above five hundred brethren at once, James, all the apostles, and last of all Paul.

Paul's list is not identical to the Gospel narratives. It is an early apostolic summary that names witnesses and includes an appearance to more than five hundred at once, which is not narrated in the four canonical Gospels. The list should not be forced into a precise chronology that Paul does not provide.

**TEXT STATES:** Paul presents appearances as part of the received Resurrection proclamation and identifies multiple groups and individuals as witnesses.

## 12. Comparison and unresolved chronology

The accounts preserve different appearances and details:

- Mary Magdalene: John 20:11–18; also Matthew 28:9–10 and Mark 16:9–11 in the KJV longer ending.
- Other women: Matthew 28:9–10.
- Two disciples on the road: Luke 24:13–35; Mark 16:12–13 in the longer ending.
- Simon/Peter: Luke 24:34; 1 Corinthians 15:5; the Gospel accounts do not narrate this appearance in the same detail.
- Gathered disciples: Luke 24:36–49; John 20:19–23.
- Thomas with the disciples: John 20:24–29.
- Disciples in Galilee: Matthew 28:16–20; John 21:1–14.
- More than five hundred: 1 Corinthians 15:6.
- James: 1 Corinthians 15:7.
- All the apostles: 1 Corinthians 15:7.
- Paul: 1 Corinthians 15:8.
- The forty-day period: Acts 1:3.

The texts do not provide a complete, uncontested sequence of every appearance. A proposed harmony must account for each source, its stated location, named participants, temporal markers, and narrative purpose. Where sequence remains uncertain, it remains uncertain.

## 13. The risen Christ: continuity and transformation

The canonical testimony holds several claims together:

1. **Identity:** The one encountered is Jesus who was crucified. The messengers identify Him as Jesus of Nazareth, which was crucified (Mark 16:6); Jesus shows His hands and side (Luke 24:39–40; John 20:20, 27).
2. **Embodied presence:** Luke records Jesus saying He has flesh and bones and records Him eating (Luke 24:39–43).
3. **Recognition:** He is recognized in differing ways: by name (John 20:16), by His words and actions (Luke 24:30–31), by His wounds (John 20:20, 27), and by the miraculous catch (John 21:7).
4. **Changed condition:** He appears in settings where doors are shut (John 20:19, 26), and in Emmaus He vanishes from the disciples' sight (Luke 24:31). The text reports these events but does not supply a physical mechanism.
5. **Death no longer has dominion:** Romans 6:9 states that Christ, being raised from the dead, dieth no more; death hath no more dominion over Him.
6. **Resurrection body:** 1 Corinthians 15:35–54 speaks of the body raised in incorruption, glory, and power, contrasting the natural body with the spiritual body. “Spiritual body” must not be equated casually with “immaterial” or “nonbodily”; Paul still calls it a body.

The accounts must be read together without selecting only the details that fit a preferred modern model.

## 14. Witness, agency, registration, and testimony

The narratives distinguish:

- **The risen Jesus:** initiates encounters, speaks, shows Himself, teaches, commissions, and is recognized.
- **The Father:** is named by Jesus in John 20:17; the Father's promise is spoken of in Luke 24 and Acts 1.
- **The Spirit:** is mentioned in John 20:22 and Acts 1:4–8; the relationship of these passages must be examined in their own contexts.
- **The women:** encounter Jesus and report what they have seen and heard.
- **The disciples:** hear reports, doubt, encounter Jesus, inspect His wounds, and testify.
- **Thomas:** moves from stated refusal to his confession, “My Lord and my God.”
- **Paul:** reports his own later encounter and includes it in the apostolic witness list.
- **The Gospel and apostolic writers:** preserve testimony for later readers.

The witness does not author the event. Nor does every witness immediately understand what has occurred. The accounts record fear, disbelief, joy, recognition, worship, confession, and commissioned testimony. These responses are part of the record and should not be flattened into a single reaction.

## 15. Relationship to the completed GUA-W

The completed GUA-W remains unchanged. The governing order remains:

**Author → Word → Creation → Witness**

The appearance narratives provide rich examples of the distinction between event, registration, recognition, interpretation, and testimony. The risen Christ is the One encountered; the disciples do not cause His presence by seeing Him. Their recognition varies and develops. Jesus speaks, interprets Scripture, and commissions witnesses.

This is a descriptive relationship to the established Witness distinction, not a new architecture stage or Gear. Jesus Christ is not a component of GUA-W. No new structure is inferred from the fact that the disciples recognize Him in different ways.

## 16. Triple Constraint Law

### Biblical compliance — PASS for the recorded appearance testimony

The study preserves the distinct Gospel narratives, Paul's witness list, the forty-day statement in Acts, the details of Thomas's encounter, and Luke's account of the embodied risen Jesus. Mark 16:9–20 is retained in the KJV with its textual-critical qualification.

### Scientific coherence — PARTIAL; historical assessment and physical mechanism bounded

Historical study can assess the sources, their transmission, the early proclamation, and the social and literary contexts. The texts report embodied encounters, recognition, eating, wounds, and appearances in shut-door settings. They do not provide a repeatable experimental protocol or a modern physical theory of the risen body's properties. No unreported mechanism is asserted.

### GUA-W consistency — PASS at governing level; no new Gear established

The derivative role of witnesses is preserved. Jesus initiates and is encountered; witnesses register and testify. Their uncertainty and differing responses are recorded. The completed GUA-W remains unchanged.

## 17. Findings

1. The canonical texts report multiple encounters with the risen Jesus, not merely an empty tomb.
2. The women, Mary Magdalene, Peter/Cephas, the eleven, Thomas, two disciples, James, more than five hundred, the apostles, and Paul appear in the combined witness record, though not every appearance is narrated in detail by each source.
3. Luke explicitly records Jesus' statement that He has flesh and bones and records Him eating.
4. John records Jesus showing His hands and side and directly addressing Thomas's doubts.
5. Recognition is not instantaneous or uniform: Mary initially mistakes Jesus for the gardener; the Emmaus disciples do not recognize Him at first; Thomas initially refuses the report; some in Matthew's Galilee scene doubt.
6. Jesus interprets Scripture and commissions witnesses; testimony follows the encounter.
7. Acts distinguishes the forty-day appearance period from the Ascension.
8. Paul's list in 1 Corinthians 15 supplies witness claims not narrated in the same form in the four Gospels.
9. The exact chronology of all appearances remains unresolved in some details.
10. The text reports the risen body's identity and embodied characteristics but does not provide a modern anatomical or physical mechanism.

## 18. Unresolved questions

1. What is the most responsible chronology of the appearances when each Gospel's temporal markers are preserved?
2. How should Matthew's Galilee appearance and Luke's Jerusalem-focused ending be related without assuming one account omits or contradicts the other?
3. What can be established about the appearance to Peter/Cephas from Luke 24:34 and 1 Corinthians 15:5, given the lack of a full narrative?
4. Who are the more than five hundred in 1 Corinthians 15:6, and what can be responsibly established about Paul's report?
5. What does the appearance to James in 1 Corinthians 15:7 signify in the wider canonical record?
6. How should the phrase “spiritual body” in 1 Corinthians 15 be interpreted alongside Luke 24:39–43 and John 20–21?
7. What does the forty-day period in Acts 1 establish about the scope of the appearances?
8. How do John 20:22 and Acts 1:4–8 relate in their respective contexts?
9. What is the exact relationship between the Resurrection appearances and the Ascension, which is a distinct event?
10. Which historical claims can be assessed from the early witness traditions, and which remain beyond independent verification?

## 19. Provisional disposition

**Canonical appearance testimony:** WRITTEN.  
**Embodied identity of the risen Jesus:** EXPLICITLY ATTESTED in Luke 24 and John 20–21.  
**Pauline witness list:** RECORDED; historical assessment remains open.  
**Appearance chronology:** PARTIAL; some sequencing remains unresolved.  
**Mark 16:9–20:** KJV reading retained; textual-critical question open.  
**Scientific mechanism:** NOT SUPPLIED BY THE TEXT; no speculative mechanism asserted.  
**GUA-W consistency:** PASS at governing level; no new Gear established.

The next distinct event in the register is the Ascension. It must remain separate from the Resurrection and from the appearances during the forty days.

**The risen Christ appears. The witnesses encounter Him. He speaks, shows Himself, interprets the Scriptures, and commissions them. They report what they have seen and heard.**
