# Investigation II.15 — Ten Lepers Cleansed; One Returns
**Primary text:** Luke 17:11–19 (KJV)  
**Related texts:** Leviticus 13–14; 2 Kings 5:1–14; Luke 5:12–16; John 9:1–41  
**Status:** Textual observations recorded; medical mechanism not supplied.

## Question
What does Luke emphasize when ten men with leprosy cry for mercy, are cleansed as they go to the priests, and only one returns to give glory to God?

## Narrative sequence
- Jesus travels toward Jerusalem through the midst of Samaria and Galilee.
- Ten men with leprosy stand afar off and lift up their voices, saying, “Jesus, Master, have mercy on us.”
- Jesus sees them and tells them, “Go shew yourselves unto the priests.”
- As they go, they are cleansed.
- One of them, seeing that he is healed, turns back with a loud voice glorifying God.
- He falls on his face at Jesus' feet, giving Him thanks; Luke identifies him as a Samaritan.
- Jesus asks whether ten were cleansed and where the nine are.
- He notes that the one who returned is a stranger, then says, “Arise, go thy way: thy faith hath made thee whole.”

## What the text states
Ten are cleansed as they obey Jesus' instruction and go. One returns, glorifies God, and gives thanks at Jesus' feet. The returning man is a Samaritan. Jesus distinguishes the one who returned from the nine who did not, and speaks to him of faith and wholeness.

## Witness and agency
The ten cry for mercy; Jesus sees and gives the instruction; they go; cleansing occurs. One recognizes the result and returns to give glory to God and thanks to Jesus. The priests are the intended witnesses under the Mosaic procedure, though Luke does not narrate their inspection. The one man's return becomes a further testimony.

## Canonical context
Leviticus 13–14 supplies the priestly framework for examination and cleansing. Luke's explicit identification of the grateful man as a Samaritan is significant in the Gospel's wider concern with mercy across social boundaries. Compare the single leper in Luke 5:12–16: both accounts include priestly testimony, but their narrative emphases differ. The phrase “thy faith hath made thee whole” should be read in context and not converted into a mechanical formula that guarantees every requested outcome.

## What the text does not state
Luke does not identify a modern disease diagnosis, give medical measurements, or explain the biological mechanism. He does not say the priests actually examined the ten; he records Jesus' instruction and the men's cleansing as they went.

## Triple Constraint
- **Biblical compliance — PASS:** all ten are cleansed; one returns; Jesus' words and the Samaritan identity are explicit.
- **Scientific coherence — PASS for restraint; mechanism UNRESOLVED:** no modern diagnostic or mechanistic evidence is supplied.
- **GUA-W consistency — PASS for governing relationship; technical mapping UNRESOLVED:** Jesus is the source of cleansing; the ten receive, and the one returns as a grateful witness. No new architecture is proposed.

## Finding
Luke's focus extends beyond the cleansing itself. The account distinguishes receiving mercy from returning to glorify God and give thanks. The Samaritan recognizes the source and returns to Jesus. The sign therefore includes a witness-response question: what does the recipient do after receiving?

**Ten are cleansed. One returns. The witness gives glory to God at Jesus' feet.**
