# Investigation II.18 — The Gadarene/Gerasene Demoniac
**Primary texts:** Matthew 8:28–34; Mark 5:1–20; Luke 8:26–39 (KJV)  
**Related texts:** Mark 1:21–28; Luke 4:31–37; Luke 10:17–20; John 1:1–3, 14  
**Status:** Comparative textual study recorded; modern psychiatric diagnosis is not inferred.

## Question
What do the three Gospel accounts report about Jesus confronting the devils, the man's condition, the swine, and the man's restoration and commission to testify?

## Narrative sequence
- Jesus arrives in the region described with differing geographical forms in the Gospel manuscripts and translations: Matthew's KJV reads “country of the Gergesenes,” while Mark and Luke's KJV read “country of the Gadarenes.” The textual and geographical question should remain visible rather than silently harmonized.
- Matthew records two men possessed with devils; Mark and Luke focus their narrative on one man. Preserve the difference.
- The man/men are described as extraordinarily distressed and difficult to restrain. Mark gives details of dwelling among tombs, breaking chains, crying, and cutting himself with stones.
- The man runs to Jesus and worships/addresses Him; the devils recognize Jesus and speak.
- Jesus commands the unclean spirit to come out. Mark records Jesus asking the man's name; the reply is “Legion: for we are many.”
- The devils beg not to be sent out of the country/into the deep and ask permission to enter a herd of swine.
- Jesus gives permission. The swine rush down a steep place into the sea and perish.
- The herdsmen flee and report the event. People come and find the formerly tormented man sitting, clothed, and in his right mind.
- The local people ask Jesus to depart, being afraid.
- The restored man asks to remain with Jesus. Jesus sends him home to tell what great things God has done for him; Mark records the wording, “what great things the Lord hath done for thee.” The man publishes the account in Decapolis.

## Differences to preserve
Matthew reports two men; Mark and Luke narrate one. The number of swine and some geographical wording differ across the textual accounts. Mark and Luke provide extended detail about the man's prior condition and subsequent commission. Do not force every difference into a false uniformity.

## What the text states
The man is under the power of devils, Jesus commands, the devils depart and enter the swine by permission, and the man is found restored. The narrative explicitly contrasts his former condition with sitting clothed and in his right mind. Jesus sends him to testify.

## Witness and agency
The man approaches; the devils recognize and plead; Jesus commands and permits; the swine perish; herdsmen report; townspeople observe; the restored man asks to accompany Jesus and is instead commissioned to tell what God/the Lord has done. The deliverance is attributed to Jesus' authority, not to the man's technique.

## Canonical context
The accounts belong with other Gospel reports of Jesus' authority over unclean spirits. The command and the spirits' response are not a medical account. Jesus' instruction to testify at home gives this event a distinctive witness commission. The differing Gospel details must be retained.

## What the text does not state
The narratives do not supply a modern psychiatric diagnosis, neurological assessment, or mechanism. Do not retrospectively diagnose the man or reduce the text's explicit spiritual claims to a modern clinical label. Nor do the accounts establish a general practice for handling animals or a formula for deliverance.

## Triple Constraint
- **Biblical compliance — PASS:** the accounts' differences, command, deliverance, restoration, and commission are retained.
- **Scientific coherence — PASS for domain discipline; modern diagnosis/mechanism UNRESOLVED:** no clinical data are supplied; avoid unsupported diagnosis.
- **GUA-W consistency — PASS for governing hierarchy; detailed technical mapping UNRESOLVED:** Jesus' authority is central; the restored man becomes a witness, not the source. No new architecture is proposed.

## Finding
The narratives contrast severe bondage with restored personal condition and then with a commission to testify. The man who had been a spectacle of distress becomes a sent witness. The event is about Jesus' authority and the testimony that follows, not an instruction to reproduce the event's details as a method.

**The devils recognize His authority. Jesus commands. The man is restored and sent to witness.**
