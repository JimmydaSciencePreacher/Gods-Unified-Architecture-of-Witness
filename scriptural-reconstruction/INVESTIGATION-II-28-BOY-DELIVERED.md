# Investigation II.28 — The Boy Delivered from an Unclean Spirit
**Primary texts:** Matthew 17:14–21; Mark 9:14–29; Luke 9:37–43 (KJV)  
**Related texts:** Matthew 10:1, 7–8; Mark 6:7, 13; Luke 9:1–2; John 9:1–3  
**Status:** Comparative case study recorded; modern diagnosis is not inferred.

## Question
What do the Synoptic accounts report when Jesus delivers a boy from a destructive spirit after the disciples have been unable to do so?

## Narrative sequence
1. Jesus and the disciples come down from the mountain and encounter a crowd and disputing scribes.
2. A father appeals for his son. Matthew describes him as lunatick and sore vexed; Mark gives an extended account of a spirit that seizes the boy, causes convulsions, and threatens his life; Luke describes a spirit that takes and tears him.
3. The father says he brought the boy to Jesus' disciples, but they could not cure/deliver him.
4. Jesus rebukes the unbelieving and perverse generation and asks that the boy be brought to Him.
5. As the boy approaches, the spirit throws him down and tears him. Jesus asks the father how long this has been happening; the father answers, “Of a child.”
6. The father asks Jesus to help if He can. Jesus responds, “If thou canst believe, all things are possible to him that believeth.” The father cries, “Lord, I believe; help thou mine unbelief.”
7. Jesus rebukes the foul spirit, commands it to come out and enter no more into him.
8. The spirit cries, convulses the boy, and comes out. The boy appears as one dead, so that many say he is dead; Jesus takes him by the hand, lifts him up, and he arises.
9. The disciples ask privately why they could not cast it out. Jesus teaches them concerning prayer; Matthew also records the saying about faith as a grain of mustard seed and, in the KJV text, verse 21 concerning prayer and fasting.

## Differences to preserve
Matthew's account is shorter and uses “lunatick”; Mark gives the father's exchange and the most detailed description of the episode; Luke emphasizes the spirit's seizure and the crowd's astonishment at God's mighty power. The accounts are parallel reports of the same episode, not three separate deliverances.

## What the text states
The boy has suffered since childhood; the disciples fail; Jesus rebukes and commands the spirit; the boy is delivered and raised by Jesus' hand. The father's confession includes both belief and an appeal for help with unbelief. The disciples' question receives a teaching answer.

## Witness and agency
The father brings and pleads for his son. The disciples have attempted but failed. Jesus commands; the spirit departs; Jesus takes the boy by the hand and raises him. The crowd witnesses; the disciples ask for understanding. The father's faith is not portrayed as an independent force that compels the result.

## What the text does not state
The Gospel descriptions do not provide a modern neurological or psychiatric diagnosis. Do not equate “lunatick” with epilepsy as a settled diagnosis, nor reduce the narrative's explicit account of an unclean spirit to a clinical label. No modern mechanism is described.

## Triple Constraint
- **Biblical compliance — PASS:** parallel accounts, disciples' failure, father's words, Jesus' command, deliverance, and subsequent instruction are retained.
- **Scientific coherence — PASS for restraint; diagnosis/mechanism UNRESOLVED:** the accounts do not supply modern clinical assessment.
- **GUA-W consistency — PASS at governing level; detailed mapping UNRESOLVED:** Jesus' authority is distinct from the disciples' limited action; the father and disciples remain witnesses and responders. No new architecture is proposed.

## Finding
The narrative explicitly distinguishes the disciples' inability from Jesus' authority. The father voices belief and unbelief together; Jesus does not present the father's psychological certainty as the originating cause. The deliverance is effected by Jesus' command.

**The disciples could not. The father pleads. Jesus rebukes the spirit, and the boy rises.**
