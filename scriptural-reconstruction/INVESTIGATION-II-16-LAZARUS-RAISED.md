# Investigation II.16 — Lazarus Raised from the Dead
**Primary text:** John 11:1–57 (KJV)  
**Related texts:** John 5:19–30; John 10:17–18; John 12:1–11; 1 Kings 17:17–24; 2 Kings 4:18–37; John 1:1–3, 14; Hebrews 1:1–3  
**Status:** Text-first case study recorded; medical mechanism not supplied.

## Question
What does John report when Jesus deliberately delays, arrives after Lazarus has died and been buried, declares Himself the resurrection and the life, and calls Lazarus out of the tomb?

## Narrative sequence
1. Lazarus of Bethany, brother of Martha and Mary, is sick. The sisters send word to Jesus.
2. Jesus says the sickness is not unto death, but for the glory of God, that the Son of God might be glorified thereby.
3. Jesus loves Martha, Mary, and Lazarus, yet remains two days where He is before going to Judaea.
4. Jesus tells His disciples Lazarus is dead and that He is glad He was not there, “to the intent ye may believe.”
5. When Jesus arrives, Lazarus has lain in the grave four days. Martha and Mary each meet Him and speak of what might have been had He been present.
6. Jesus tells Martha, “Thy brother shall rise again,” and declares, “I am the resurrection, and the life.”
7. Jesus is deeply moved; He weeps. He comes to the tomb, a cave with a stone laid upon it.
8. Jesus commands, “Take ye away the stone.” Martha notes the four-day burial and the expected odour.
9. Jesus reminds her of His word concerning the glory of God if she would believe.
10. Jesus prays aloud to the Father, explaining that He speaks for the people standing by, “that they may believe that thou hast sent me.”
11. He cries with a loud voice, “Lazarus, come forth.”
12. The man who had been dead comes forth, bound hand and foot with graveclothes and his face bound with a napkin. Jesus commands, “Loose him, and let him go.”
13. Many who witness the event believe; others report it to the Pharisees. The chief priests and Pharisees convene and resolve to put Jesus to death. The narrative continues toward the Passover and Jesus' own death.

## What the text states
John explicitly states Lazarus died, had been in the tomb four days, and came forth in response to Jesus' command. Jesus identifies Himself as resurrection and life and explicitly connects the event with belief that the Father sent Him and with the glory of God. The event intensifies both belief and opposition.

## Witness and agency
The sisters send word, grieve, and speak with Jesus. The disciples accompany Him. Jesus speaks to the Father before the assembled witnesses and then commands Lazarus. Lazarus comes forth; others remove the graveclothes. The crowd witnesses, with differing responses. The stone-removal and unbinding are human actions commanded within the event; they are not the source of life.

## Canonical context
The accounts of Elijah and Elisha raising the dead (1 Kings 17; 2 Kings 4) provide Scriptural precedents, but John's account uniquely places Jesus' own declaration “I am the resurrection, and the life” at the centre. John 5:21–29 has already recorded Jesus' teaching about the Son giving life. John 10:17–18 speaks of His authority concerning His own life. The raising of Lazarus also directly precedes the leadership's intensified plot and the approach of Jesus' Passion. It must not be detached from that narrative trajectory.

## What the text does not state
John gives no modern cause of death, post-mortem measurements, biological pathway, or physical mechanism. The four days are explicit; any additional clinical detail is not. The event is not a report of ordinary resuscitation or a repeatable medical procedure.

## Triple Constraint
- **Biblical compliance — PASS:** the chronology, four days, Jesus' declaration, prayer, command, result, and responses are explicit.
- **Scientific coherence — PASS for evidential restraint; mechanism UNRESOLVED:** the text does not provide modern clinical observations or a physical mechanism. Do not invent one or relabel the event as an ordinary clinical recovery.
- **GUA-W consistency — PASS for governing hierarchy; detailed technical mapping UNRESOLVED:** Jesus' unique authority is preserved; human actions remain subordinate responses. No new architecture is proposed.

## Finding
John does not present the raising of Lazarus as an isolated display of power. Jesus states its purpose before it occurs: the glory of God and belief that the Father sent Him. He identifies Himself not merely as one who can obtain resurrection, but as “the resurrection, and the life.” The sign is inseparable from His identity and the approaching Passion.

**Jesus declares who He is. Jesus calls. Lazarus comes forth. The witnesses must decide what they have seen.**
