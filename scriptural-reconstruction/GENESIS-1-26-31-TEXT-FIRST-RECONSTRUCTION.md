## Genesis 1:26 — Counsel, image, likeness, dominion

> And God said, Let us make man in our image, after our likeness: and let them have dominion over the fish of the sea, and over the fowl of the air, and over the cattle, and over all the earth, and over every creeping thing that creepeth upon the earth.

Textual observations:
- God speaks.
- The stated action is “Let us make man.”
- The text specifies “in our image, after our likeness.”
- Dominion is stated in the same speech, with its scope enumerated.

The verse joins the making of man, image/likeness language, and dominion. It does not define image and likeness as a technical architecture, nor does it say that dominion means unrestricted authority over the Creator or permission to redefine good and evil.

**Question:** How do image/likeness and dominion relate? The text places them together, but their exact theological meaning requires wider Scriptural examination.

## Genesis 1:27 — Creation and differentiation

> So God created man in his own image, in the image of God created he him; male and female created he them.

Textual observations:
- The text reports that God created man in His own image.
- The phrase “in the image of God” is repeated.
- The text says “male and female created he them.”

The verse identifies God as Creator and records male/female distinction within the creation of mankind. It does not supply a modern biological, sociological, or computational definition of sex or personhood.

**Architectural caution:** The text's distinction must be recorded accurately; do not reduce it to an analogy or silently substitute a different conceptual framework.

## Genesis 1:28 — Blessing, fruitfulness, multiplication, filling, subduing, dominion

> And God blessed them, and God said unto them, Be fruitful, and multiply, and replenish the earth, and subdue it: and have dominion over the fish of the sea, and over the fowl of the air, and over every living thing that moveth upon the earth.

Textual observations:
- God blesses them.
- God speaks directly to them.
- The commands include being fruitful, multiplying, replenishing the earth, subduing it, and exercising dominion over specified living creatures.

This is a consequential change in the narrated sequence: the command is addressed to mankind. It follows the report of mankind's creation in verses 26–27.

**Architectural question:** Does this establish an appointed human role within an already created order? The text supports examining that question. The exact GUA-W category—Purpose, Function, Stewardship, or another term—must not be fixed without wider context, especially Genesis 2:15.

## Genesis 1:29 — Provision

> And God said, Behold, I have given you every herb bearing seed, which is upon the face of all the earth, and every tree, in the which is the fruit of a tree yielding seed; to you it shall be for meat.

Textual observations:
- God speaks of what He has given.
- The provision named includes seed-bearing herbs and fruit-bearing trees with seed.
- The text identifies this provision as food for mankind.

The verse adds a provision statement to the human commission. It should not be turned into a complete modern nutritional or ecological model.

## Genesis 1:30 — Provision for other living creatures

> And to every beast of the earth, and to every fowl of the air, and to every thing that creepeth upon the earth, wherein there is life, I have given every green herb for meat: and it was so.

Textual observations:
- Provision is also stated for beasts, fowl, and creeping things.
- The text identifies green herb as their food.
- The verse closes “and it was so.”

The passage distinguishes the provision stated for mankind in verse 29 from the provision stated for other living creatures in verse 30. The precise implications for diet, ecology, or later changes require a broader Scriptural investigation.

## Genesis 1:31 — Evaluation and completion of the sixth day

> And God saw every thing that he had made, and, behold, it was very good. And the evening and the morning were the sixth day.

Textual observations:
- God sees everything that He had made.
- The evaluation is “very good.”
- The evening-and-morning formula closes the sixth day.

The evaluation now refers to “every thing that he had made,” not merely one isolated item in the immediately preceding verse. The text marks a comprehensive evaluation at this point in the account.

**Important distinction:** Genesis 1:31 says “very good.” It does not yet say that the work is finished in the exact wording later used in Genesis 2:1–2. The relation between “very good,” completion, rest, and sanctification must be examined in sequence rather than treated as synonyms.

## Observations for architectural reconstruction

1. **The human commission follows the creation account.** Genesis 1:26–27 narrates the making of mankind; verse 28 records blessing and commands addressed to them. This supports investigating the distinction between creation and appointed role.
2. **Image/likeness and dominion are textually joined.** Their theological meaning must be examined in wider Scripture; neither should be given an unsupported technical definition.
3. **Blessing and command are explicit.** Fruitfulness and multiplication are not merely inferred from the biological descriptions; they are directly commanded in verse 28.
4. **Provision is part of the account.** Verses 29–30 specify food provision for mankind and other living creatures.
5. **The evaluation is comprehensive.** Verse 31 says God saw everything He had made and that it was “very good.”
6. **The sixth-day closure is not yet the seventh-day completion statement.** Genesis 2:1–3 must be read next to identify what “finished,” “rested,” and “sanctified” add.
7. **The text does not state a human authority to author the order.** Dominion is a delegated role within the created order described by the passage; any fuller account of its limits must be drawn from Scripture, not assumed.
